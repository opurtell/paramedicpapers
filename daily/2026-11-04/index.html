Daily scan

Paramedic research, 4 November 2026

Cardiac arrest follow-through dominates today: post-ROSC ST-segment elevation and five-year ECPR survival from Resuscitation, plus a Spanish revalidation of the M-DIRECT prehospital stroke triage scale in 1,950 patients. The most road-relevant pick is a UK realist evaluation of what actually helps children in pain in ambulances.

  • What helps kids in pain in ambulances

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Qualitative · Hypothesis-generating

    A UK realist evaluation across three English ambulance services and two children's EDs — 44 interview transcripts plus 34,068 clinical records — refined the programme theory for paediatric pain care, with clinician preparedness and compassionate person-centred care emerging as the key mechanisms.

  • M-DIRECT stroke triage scale revalidated

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Spanish prospective study of 1,950 prehospital code-stroke patients revalidated the M-DIRECT scale five years after implementation, showing 77% sensitivity and 76% specificity for identifying endovascular-treatment candidates, with a positive score more than tripling the odds of EVT.

  • Post-ROSC ST elevation tracks acute coronary occlusion

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese single-centre review of 149 angiography-evaluable OHCA survivors with presumed culprit lesions found post-resuscitation ST-segment elevation marked an acute occlusive coronary phenotype but was not independently linked to neurological outcome once prehospital factors were accounted for.

  • Two-thirds of ECPR survivors alive at five years

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Context only

    A US–Czech retrospective cohort of 728 patients given ECPR for refractory VT/VF OHCA found 29% survived to hospital discharge and, among those survivors, about two-thirds were still alive at five years with favourable cerebral performance in most.

  • STEMI deaths now mostly prehospital

    Circulation · Other · Context only

    A US Circulation viewpoint argues that because most STEMI deaths now occur out of hospital, the biggest gains lie in shortening symptom-onset-to-reperfusion, and sets out five recommendations spanning patient education, prehospital pharmaceuticals and reimbursement reform.

  • Long wildfire shifts linked to broken sleep

    Journal of Occupational and Environmental Medicine · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    A US survey of 90 first responders during the 2025 Los Angeles wildfires found 24-hour shifts were associated with 1.65 fewer total sleep hours and four or more consecutive shifts with shorter sleep, with 83% reporting poor or very poor sleep quality.

  • Turning HEMS debriefs into shared learning

    Paramedicine · Qualitative · Hypothesis-generating

    Focus groups with 30 HEMS clinicians found a quality improvement project capturing routine debriefs ('Coffee and Cases') strengthened organisational learning through three themes: education, accessibility and calibration of learning points.

  • US Medicaid cuts threaten EMS funding

    Health Affairs · Other · Context only

    A US Health Affairs commentary warns that H.R. 1 and subsequent Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services rules will deepen financial strain on local EMS systems already struggling with inadequate reimbursement.

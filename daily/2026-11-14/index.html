Daily scan

Paramedic research, 14 November 2026

A Japanese nationwide registry found prehospital advanced airway was not linked to better outcomes in traumatic cardiac arrest, and an Australian scoping review found paramedic recognition of seizures — especially non-convulsive ones — remains patchy.

  • Advanced airway not linked to traumatic arrest survival

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese nationwide registry cohort of 7,217 adults with traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest found successful prehospital advanced airway management was not significantly associated with better 30-day survival or neurological outcome when resuscitation-time bias was corrected.

  • Paramedics miss many non-convulsive seizures

    Seizure · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    An Australian scoping review of 18 studies found paramedic recognition of seizure presentations remains suboptimal, with sensitivity of 54.0–58.5% and non-recognition of non-convulsive seizures reaching 53.0–81.4%.

  • Sepsis suspicion at dispatch, earlier antibiotics

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Norwegian registry study of 527 sepsis admissions found dispatch suspicion of sepsis was associated with a 31-minute shorter adjusted time from ED arrival to antibiotic administration.

  • 3% of callers drive a fifth of all calls

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    A Danish observational cohort of 443,977 emergency calls found 3.0% of callers (6,415 people) generated 19.9% of all calls, with post-threshold frequent callers seeing more telephone resolution (23.8%) and non-conveyance (27.4%).

  • EtCO2 trends predict ROSC cycle by cycle

    Resuscitation · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    A Taiwanese retrospective multicentre study of 1,480 patients with OHCA built a two-layer LSTM model on sequential EtCO2 that predicted ROSC well at event level (AUROC 0.876) but only 0.685 per two-minute cycle.

  • US naloxone use varies by region, not rurality

    Journal of Substance Use and Addiction Treatment · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A US cross-sectional analysis of 110,261 EMS activations for suspected opioid overdose found documented naloxone administration in 33.9%, with regional variation (South aOR 2.10 vs Northeast) that persisted while the rural-urban difference attenuated to null after clinical adjustment.

  • Untangling trauma terms for first responders

    Trauma, Violence & Abuse · Systematic review · Context only

    A French integrative review of 76 studies on first responders (including paramedics) found the concepts of secondary traumatic stress, vicarious trauma, compassion fatigue and PTSD overlap symptomatically, with organisational constraints playing a central role in traumatic responses.

  • Video laryngoscopes available but rarely first choice

    Cureus · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A Belgian nationwide survey of mobile intensive care units found 91.4% of responding units had videolaryngoscope access, yet only 6.2% of physician users reached for it first-choice and 81.5% reserved it for failed direct laryngoscopy.

  • Weekly simulation linked to better student exams

    BMC Medical Education · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    An Australian retrospective analysis of 305 paramedicine students across 1,243 simulated scenarios found weekly practice frequency was positively correlated with summative exam performance (r = 0.257, p < 0.001), with competence scores rising across the trimester.

  • VR leads mass-casualty training but frameworks lag

    PloS One · Systematic review · Context only

    A scoping review of 28 studies on technology-enhanced prehospital mass-casualty training found virtual reality was the most common modality (43%) and 56% of completed studies demonstrated applied learning, but 75% lacked an explicit training design framework.

Paramedic research, 13 October 2026

7 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 13 October 2026 scan.

Listen to the 13 October 2026 podcast episode

  • Lactate drives prehospital mSOFA risk

    QJM · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Prospective Spanish multicentre study of 12,212 high-priority patients deconstructed the prehospital mSOFA score with a decision tree, finding lactate ≥3.8 mmol/L was the primary discriminator for 2-day in-hospital mortality, ahead of SaFi, GCS and creatinine.

  • Who calls an ambulance for low-acuity problems

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective study of 41,761 low-acuity ED visits in the southeastern US found only 7.7% arrived by ambulance, with the highest odds among retired or disabled (aOR 3.60) and unemployed (aOR 2.26) patients and the lowest among those with an established GP (aOR 0.57).

  • What makes an ambulance driver competent

    Paramedicine · Systematic review · Context only

    Scoping review of 19 studies (2007–2026) mapped how ambulance-driver roles and competencies are conceptualised across EMS systems, identifying seven domains from technical driving through to work ability and fitness.

  • Prehospital olanzapine for meth toxicity

    BMJ Open · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Population-based Winnipeg cohort evaluating a 2018 policy letting paramedics give olanzapine for methamphetamine intoxication — treated patients were far more likely to be transported to ED (aOR 11.83) and seen by a doctor, and less likely to leave before being seen (aOR 0.56).

  • Doppler beats pulse check after tourniquet

    Healthcare · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    Randomised crossover study of 41 practising paramedics assessing distal circulation after tourniquet application found paramedic-performed Doppler ultrasonography beat pulse palpation on sensitivity (88.9% vs 66.7%) and accuracy (90.2% vs 78.0%) against an assessor-Doppler reference.

  • Child visitor injuries in Queensland

    Safety · Cohort · Context only

    Descriptive study of 3,802 Queensland Ambulance Service attendances for injuries in child visitors aged ≤15 (2009–18) found interstate visitors accounted for 85.8% of cases, with falls and transport incidents the leading mechanisms and burns third in 0–4-year-olds.

  • Two paramedic attitudes to paediatric pain

    Turkish Journal of Emergency Medicine · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    Cross-sectional survey of 129 paramedics in a standardised Turkish EMS system used latent class analysis to identify two paediatric analgesia attitudinal profiles — proactive/system-oriented (57.4%) and conservative/low-engagement (42.6%) — unrelated to age, sex or experience.

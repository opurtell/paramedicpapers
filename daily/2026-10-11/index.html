Daily scan

Paramedic research, 11 October 2026

Trauma dominates today: pooled prehospital TXA trial data shows a survival benefit in fibrinolytic shutdown, while imaging data confirms prehospital variables can't reliably pick which pelvic fractures a binder will help.

  • Prehospital TXA helps fibrinolytic shutdown

    The Journal of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    Pooled analysis of two prospective prehospital TXA trials found fibrinolytic shutdown carried 14.5% 30-day mortality versus 6.5% with physiological fibrinolysis, and shutdown patients randomised to TXA had about a third the risk of death.

  • Opioid OHCA outcomes similar to other arrests

    Resuscitation · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 10,786 EMS-treated OHCAs in a Canadian registry found opioid-associated arrests had similar outcomes to undifferentiated arrests (OR 1.02), but Utstein variables like initial rhythm and witnessed status predicted outcome differently in the opioid group.

  • Three post-ROSC lab phenotypes predict outcome

    Resuscitation · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Latent class analysis of 14 laboratory values drawn within six hours of ROSC in 1,514 resuscitated OHCA patients identified three phenotypes, with the multi-organ dysfunction group at roughly double the odds of poor neurological recovery compared with the others.

  • Prehospital variables can't pick pelvic binder benefit

    The American Surgeon · Cross-sectional · Context only

    Blinded imaging review of 298 CT-confirmed pelvic ring injuries found binder application would be potentially mechanically unfavourable in 56% of cases, and no prehospital variable (hypotension, shock index, mechanism, GCS) discriminated favourable morphology well.

  • Italian HEMS trauma mortality predictors

    European Journal of Trauma and Emergency Surgery · Cohort · Context only

    Six-year Italian HEMS cohort of 3,152 transported trauma patients found 3.4% in-hospital mortality, independently predicted by older age, lower Revised Trauma Score, intubation and prehospital CPR — the last two acting mainly as severity markers.

Paramedic research, 6 October 2026

5 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 6 October 2026 scan.

Listen to the 6 October 2026 podcast episode

  • Stroke triage tools: cost-effective, hard to sustain

    Journal of Evaluation in Clinical Practice · Systematic review · Context only

    Systematic review of prehospital stroke triage tools (mobile stroke units, telemedicine, biomarkers, AI) found most reports of cost-effectiveness positive, but sustained implementation is limited by complexity, workflow fit and costs.

  • How rare is a prehospital birth? Very

    Acta Anaesthesiologica Scandinavica · Cross-sectional · Context only

    Eight-year cross-sectional review of Danish EMS records found unplanned prehospital births attended by ambulance staff are rare — 310 assisted deliveries, 0.03% of all missions — with most complications manageable by crews.

  • NOF fracture care runs on tacit knowledge

    Paramedicine · Qualitative · Context only

    Australian exploratory qualitative study running eight online focus groups with 21 registered paramedics on suspected neck of femur fracture care — participants described managing these patients through diagnostic uncertainty, tacit knowledge, peer learning and contextual judgement in the absence of dedicated prehospital guidance, and called for clearer evidence-informed direction covering movement, dignity, communication and analgesia.

  • Paediatric OHCA: half had warning symptoms

    Open Heart · Case series · Practice prompt

    UK single-centre review of 58 consecutive paediatric out-of-hospital VF arrest survivors without a pre-existing diagnosis found 50.9% had reported cardiac symptoms beforehand — syncope in 23.6%, half the arrests during exertion — and of 13 children with prior syncope only three had sought medical attention and none had follow-up arranged, pointing to missed opportunities to diagnose inherited cardiac conditions before arrest.

  • Tabletop MCI exercises: how they're run and measured

    Interactive Journal of Medical Research · Scoping review · Context only

    Scoping review of 13 studies from 9 countries on tabletop exercises for prehospital mass casualty preparedness — it built a preliminary three-tier 'TTX Design Spectrum' typology and classified outcomes with the Kirkpatrick model (plus an applied-learning level), finding evaluation focused on participant learning with less assessment of applied performance or system outcomes.

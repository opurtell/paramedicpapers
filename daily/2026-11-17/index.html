Paramedic research, 17 November 2026

A light day: England's air ambulance services report on field amputation practice, an umbrella review maps paramedic competence into five domains, and Norwegian crews share what running an ambulance RCT actually takes.

  • Field amputation: broad capability, little standardisation

    Injury · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A survey of all English air ambulance services found every one could perform prehospital field amputation and four had completed six cases in five years, with a Gigli saw the standard bone instrument and little national standardisation.

  • What competence actually means for paramedics

    Emergency Medicine International · Systematic review · Context only

    A Finnish-led umbrella review of 20 studies defined the competences paramedics need in out-of-hospital emergency care, organised into five domains: interpersonal and teamwork, cognitive and decision-making, professionalism and ethical, clinical, and situation-specific or high-stress competence.

  • What running an ambulance trial really takes

    Trials · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A Norwegian survey of 67 ambulance personnel in the PreMeFen RCT found integrating research into EMS was feasible but demanding: 28% protocol deviations across 338 inclusions, motivation declining over 18 months, and 51% finding study communication excessive — yet 90% would take part in future research.

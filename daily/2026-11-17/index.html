  • Field amputation: broad capability, little standardisation

    Injury · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A survey of all English air ambulance services found every one could perform prehospital field amputation and four had completed six cases in five years, with a Gigli saw the standard bone instrument and little national standardisation.

  • What competence actually means for paramedics

    Emergency Medicine International · Systematic review · Context only

    A Finnish-led umbrella review of 20 studies defined the competences paramedics need in out-of-hospital emergency care, organised into five domains: interpersonal and teamwork, cognitive and decision-making, professionalism and ethical, clinical, and situation-specific or high-stress competence.

  • What running an ambulance trial really takes

    Trials · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A Norwegian survey of 67 ambulance personnel in the PreMeFen RCT found integrating research into EMS was feasible but demanding: 28% protocol deviations across 338 inclusions, motivation declining over 18 months, and 51% finding study communication excessive — yet 90% would take part in future research.

  • rSIG cutoff flags trauma patients needing intervention

    BMJ Open · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese single-centre retrospective cohort tested prehospital rSIG (reverse shock index multiplied by GCS) in 747 trauma patients, finding rSIG below 24.8 was associated with resuscitative interventions (adjusted OR 3.17) and 24-hour transfusion (aOR 4.20) in the validation cohort, but not with surgery, interventional radiology or in-hospital mortality.

  • Prehospital trauma scores poorly predict secondary transfer

    Journal of Clinical Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Turkish EMS record review of 1,194 multiply injured patients compared eight prehospital trauma scores for predicting secondary interhospital transfer, finding CRAMS performed best but only modestly (AUC 0.676), while a random forest model reached AUC 0.836.

  • Dispatch triage overcalls and misses across call types

    African Journal of Emergency Medicine · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A South African review of 242,576 emergency dispatches found dispatch priority ratings overtriaged 62% and undertriaged 15% of incidents against scene triage, with the highest undertriage in respiratory, diabetic and chest pain calls.

  • What the evidence says about ambulance-ED handover

    Natural Resources for Human Health · Other · Context only

    A narrative review of ambulance-ED coordination evidence found prehospital notification consistently associated with faster diagnosis and reperfusion in stroke and myocardial infarction, structured handover tools with better information transfer, and ambulance diversion with little crowding relief and links to higher mortality in time-sensitive conditions.

  • Paediatric IO sim success beat IV in students

    Journal of Nursing, Social Studies, Public Health and Rehabilitation · Simulation · Context only

    A Czech within-subject simulation study had 50 paramedic students attempt paediatric IV on an arm trainer and intraosseous insertion on a high-fidelity manikin, finding IO completed with verified patency in 50 of 50 attempts versus IV in 4 of 50.

  • Harder ambulance transports, higher pneumonia mortality

    Acute Medicine & Surgery · Registry · Context only

    A Japanese population registry study of 78,720 patients transported for suspected bacterial pneumonia in Osaka found difficult ambulance transport (four or more hospital selections and 30+ minutes on scene) rose from 2.7% pre-pandemic to 10.3% during COVID-19 and was associated with higher in-hospital mortality (adjusted OR 1.14).

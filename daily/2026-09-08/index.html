Paramedic research, 8 September 2026

12 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 8 September 2026 scan.

  • Relatives' needs after failed OHCA resuscitation

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Qualitative · Context only

    Interviews with 15 relatives present at unsuccessful out-of-hospital resuscitations showed their needs shift across three phases — dispatch guidance, help-or-withdraw during resus, then compassionate unhurried communication when efforts stop.

  • Most ECPR-eligible London OHCAs can't reach ECMO in time

    Resuscitation Plus · Cohort · Context only

    Geospatial analysis of five years of London Ambulance Service OHCA data — of 776 patients meeting ECPR eligibility criteria, only 3% could reach an ECMO-capable centre within the 45-minute target, and stretching it to 60 minutes would lift access by 217%.

  • Fentanyl in prehospital RSI for TBI linked to lower mortality

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    In 423 TBI patients intubated by a Finnish HEMS unit, fentanyl as an anaesthetic adjuvant was associated with lower mortality (OR 0.38), though selection bias can't be excluded.

  • Women post-OHCA get less angiography

    Resuscitation Plus · Registry · Context only

    Nationwide Swedish cohort of 8,641 post-OHCA admissions — women were less likely to undergo coronary angiography and PCI than men, yet intervention rates were even once angiographic findings were accounted for, and a third of angiographed women without shockable rhythm or ST elevation had significant coronary disease.

  • Ramping driven by hospital 'back door'

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    A mixed-methods Queensland study of six years of ambulance and hospital records found access block is largely a hospital discharge ('back door') problem, with recommendations spanning discharge metrics, primary care support and prehospital flow.

  • Predicting who needs prehospital blood

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective cohort of 25,473 EMS-transported trauma patients in Los Angeles County — of those meeting prehospital blood transfusion criteria, 52% received blood within four hours, with SBP <90 mmHg and HR ≥110 bpm the best predictors of emergent transfusion.

  • Ventilation feedback devices inaccurate during CPR

    Resuscitation Plus · Simulation · Context only

    Simulation cross-over study of two commercial ventilation feedback devices used during continuous compressions with asynchronous ventilations — both were clinically inaccurate, one displaying 111 breaths/min when the true rate was 12 and underestimating tidal volume by up to 440 mL in reversed airflow.

  • Admission hypocalcaemia predicts trauma mortality

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Registry · Context only

    Multicentre trauma-registry cohort of 977 severely injured patients — admission hypocalcaemia was independently associated with hospital mortality and trauma-induced coagulopathy, while apparently elevated ionised calcium mostly reflected profound acidosis.

  • Double-click metronome preserves CPR quality

    Resuscitation Plus · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    Secondary analysis of 477 volunteers in prolonged manikin CPR — compression depth and chest recoil decayed within minutes, and a double-click metronome with separate compression and release cues preserved quality significantly longer than a standard metronome.

  • LVOT VTI unreliable for mild hypovolaemia

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    Prospective study measuring LVOT VTI in 63 volunteers before and after donating about 6–7 mL/kg of blood — VTI fell 11.5% on average but with marked inter-individual variability, so it can't reliably flag mild hypovolaemia on its own.

  • ED volume not linked to mortality

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    Cohort of 724,256 ED presentations across Dutch and Danish hospitals — annual ED volume had no association with in-hospital mortality, while mortality was lower on busier days, driven by excess deaths on quiet ones.

  • AI predicts ED admitting team from triage notes

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    Sydney single-centre deep learning study of 40,054 admitted ED patients — a fine-tuned clinical language model predicted the inpatient admitting specialty from triage notes with AUROC 0.97, though precision was a moderate 0.71.

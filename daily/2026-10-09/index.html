Daily scan

Paramedic research, 9 October 2026

Resuscitation and cardiac care dominate today: a Victorian registry found only 60% of paramedic-suspected STEMIs were confirmed at discharge, and a 320,000-patient Japanese registry suggests rising bystander CPR in the very old isn't translating into better neurological outcomes.

  • Only 60% of paramedic-suspected STEMI confirmed

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · Registry · Context only

    A retrospective Victorian statewide ambulance registry cohort (2020–25) followed up paramedic-suspected STEMI presentations and found STEMI confirmed in only 60% of those with a recorded discharge diagnosis, with 30-day mortality of 6.5%.

  • Bystander CPR up but outcomes flat in the very old

    Resuscitation Plus · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 322,943 bystander-witnessed OHCA patients aged ≥65 in the All-Japan Utstein Registry (2005–22) found bystander CPR rose over time but stalled near 50%, and 1-month favourable neurological outcome stayed around 1% in those aged ≥90 despite CPR rates near 80%.

  • CPR injuries common after long arrests

    Resuscitation Plus · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre retrospective cohort of 136 OHCA patients found CPR-related injuries in 43% (16.9% clinically significant), with CPR duration over 20 minutes — not ECPR or mechanical CPR — independently associated with injury (OR 2.45).

  • Ice-water immersion wins for drug hyperthermia

    Clinical Toxicology · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    A systematic review of 71 studies (1,932 patients) comparing cooling methods for drug-induced (and exercise-induced) hyperthermia found ice-water and cold-water immersion fastest at ~0.16–0.17 °C/min and clearly superior to passive cooling.

  • Video laryngoscopy edges out direct in kids

    Resuscitation Plus · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    A systematic review and meta-analysis of 18 studies (17 observational, 1 RCT) found video laryngoscopy for paediatric emergency intubation was associated with higher first-pass success (RR 1.11) and fewer severe adverse events (RR 0.76), on very low certainty evidence.

  • Sepsis intubations: haemodynamic trouble common

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Registry · Context only

    A retrospective analysis of the ANZEDAR registry (79 hospitals, 2010–26) covering 280 sepsis intubations found haemodynamic complications in 27.9% and peri-intubation cardiac arrest in 4.6%, with less waveform capnography use than non-sepsis intubations.

  • Field fluids before ROSC in crush arrests

    Ulusal Travma Ve Acil Cerrahi Dergisi = Turkish Journal of Trauma & Emergency Surgery · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre retrospective study of 37 earthquake victims in cardiac arrest after the 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes found ROSC in 27% — exclusively in the 25 patients who received pre-hospital fluids (mean ~1.5 L) — with better pH, lactate, and potassium on arrival.

  • 12-lead transmission lags equipment availability

    Journal of Cardiology · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A national survey of 588 of Japan's 723 fire departments found 92.7% had at least partial 12-lead ECG recording capability but only 9.7% routinely recorded and transmitted, with chest pain/ACS protocols — not equipment — the strongest driver of transmission uptake.

  • Little evidence for call handler wellbeing programs

    Stress and Health · Systematic review · Context only

    A PRISMA systematic review of 12 studies of health, wellbeing, and job satisfaction interventions for emergency call handlers in ambulance, police, and fire services found limited evidence that any intervention works, with no role-specific programs validated.

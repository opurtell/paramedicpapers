Paramedic research, 22 October 2026

15 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 22 October 2026 scan.

Listen to the 22 October 2026 podcast episode

  • Dispatcher training + volunteer app lifts bystander CPR

    The Lancet. Public Health · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial across 14 French EMS agencies found dispatcher training in arrest recognition, telephone CPR and a geolocated volunteer responder app increased bystander CPR from 61.4% to 70.9% (adjusted OR 1.54), though survival did not improve.

  • Real-time feedback tidies BVM ventilation in arrest

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Paris before-and-after study of 163 arrests found real-time feedback during bag-valve-mask ventilation improved insufflated-volume adherence (68% vs 42% of breaths in the 400–600 mL range) and cut leak volume by 12%, although expired tidal volumes were unchanged.

  • Fatigue common a year after cardiac arrest

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Context only

    A Victorian population-based cohort of 728 out-of-hospital cardiac arrest survivors found fatigue in 35.8%, anxiety in 22.6% and depression in 18.0% at 12 months, with heavier symptom burden linked to worse quality of life, function and return to work.

  • Keep pulse checks under 5 seconds

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A 252-patient multicentre study in Türkiye found pulse checks completed within 5 seconds were associated with roughly double the ROSC rate of checks lasting 6–10 seconds (36.3% vs 19.5%, OR 2.39), alongside better capnographic dynamics.

  • Pad application is the AED time bottleneck

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Timestamps from 8,115 remotely monitored Japanese AEDs showed pad application consumed a median 81% of the power-on-to-first-analysis interval, with completion inside about 50 seconds marking shocks delivered within 60 seconds of power-on.

  • Whole blood on ambulances: first two years

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cohort · Context only

    The first two years of a prehospital low-titre O-positive whole blood program in Oklahoma (71.6% penetrating trauma) showed significantly improved prehospital vital signs after paramedic transfusion, with improved systolic blood pressure tied to higher survival to hospital arrival.

  • Physician-directed ALS: no survival gain

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Context only

    A 129,207-case Korean difference-in-differences study found regions adopting video physician-directed prehospital ALS for cardiac arrest had modestly higher prehospital ROSC (+1.35 percentage points) but no gain in survival or neurological outcome, with prehospital times stretching about 4 minutes.

  • Mass casualty care: slower but no deadlier

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    A matched German trauma-registry cohort (423 severely injured MCI patients vs 1,692 routine controls) found mass-casualty patients waited longer on scene (85 vs 67 minutes) and received more intubation and tranexamic acid, yet in-hospital mortality was similar (13.5% vs 11.7%).

  • Prehospital flumazenil: 69 doses, no seizures

    The Western Journal of Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    A five-year Kansas review of 69 prehospital flumazenil administrations for suspected benzodiazepine overdose found clinical improvement with no seizures or adverse events recorded, even where possible contraindications existed.

  • OHCA registry clock times run short

    Resuscitation Plus · Cohort · Context only

    Linking 56,969 Swedish OHCA registry records to dispatch-centre data showed unit response times matched almost exactly, but registry-recorded total response times ran a median 81 seconds shorter than the dispatch clock.

  • Resilience as a skill, not a trait

    European Journal of Psychotraumatology · Qualitative · Context only

    Semi-structured interviews with 20 Queensland paramedics and dispatchers framed frontline resilience as a trainable expert skill — a deliberately entered 'work mode' with preparation, containment and recovery phases — rather than a fixed trait.

  • Bathroom jobs eat scene time

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Cross-sectional · Context only

    Among 26,058 older Japanese patients transported from home, EMS on-scene time ran up to 9% longer when the patient was found in the toilet, bathroom or kitchen and on higher floors, and shorter in corridors and gardens.

  • POC troponin on ambulances: fewer ED visits

    Health Economics Review · Modelling · Context only

    A French economic model built on 737 patients found prehospital point-of-care high-sensitivity troponin reduced ED attendance (90.9% vs 96.7%) and ED length of stay (1.63 vs 2.76 hours) versus standard care for suspected acute coronary syndrome.

  • Decision support eases stroke triage load

    The Western Journal of Emergency Medicine · Simulation · Context only

    In a randomised crossover simulation trial, 35 Taiwanese emergency medical technicians using a stroke triage decision-support interface showed reduced mental workload (RMSSD up 4.3 ms, heart rate down, NASA-TLX lower) versus dispatch orders alone.

  • Dutch TEAM tool validated for resus training

    Resuscitation Plus · Other · Context only

    A Dutch translation of the TEAM team-performance instrument showed high internal consistency (Cronbach's α 0.93) and good three-rater reliability (ICC 0.78) across 104 simulated resuscitation team performances in ALS courses.

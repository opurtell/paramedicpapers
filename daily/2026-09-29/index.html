7 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 29 September 2026 scan.

  • No sex differences in OHCA with confirmed CAD

    Resuscitation Plus · Registry · Context only

    Post hoc analysis of a prospective European OHCA registry (2012–2023) testing whether sex-based differences in care persist when obstructive CAD is angiographically confirmed as the arrest cause — among 580 patients (15.3% women), PCI use, procedural success, targeted temperature management and ECPR were similar between sexes, 30-day survival was 66.2% in women vs 61.5% in men (p=0.33), favourable neurological outcome 55.1% vs 53.8% (p=0.82), and female sex was not independently associated with 30-day survival (adjusted OR 0.75, not significant).

  • Prehospital minithoracostomy vs chest drain

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective physician-led HEMS cohort (94 trauma patients) comparing prehospital minithoracostomy with chest drainage — minithoracostomy carried no meaningful scene-time penalty and fewer recorded complications, but non-random allocation and few events make it hypothesis-generating only.

  • NRB + nasal cannula inferior to flush-rate preoxygenation

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Other · Supports current practice

    Healthy-volunteer crossover study — NRB plus nasal cannula at 15–25 L/min each produced expired oxygen of 68–75% versus 85% for flush-rate NRB, so the portable-cylinder workaround is inferior when supply allows flush rate.

  • Post-OHCA outcomes at Victorian Heart Hospital

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Cohort · Context only

    Audit of 121 post-arrest patients at the Victorian Heart Hospital — 50.4% survived to discharge with 92.6% prehospital ROSC and 61.2% shockable rhythms, and 24% needed interhospital transfer, mostly for non-cardiac causes.

  • Paediatric non-transport re-call rates low

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cross-sectional · Context only

    US retrospective study of 1.85 million paediatric ambulance calls (40% not transported) — only 1% of non-transported children re-called and were transported within 72 hours and deaths were rare (0.1% dead on scene at re-call), with under-5s, neurologic impressions and disadvantaged areas over-represented among re-calls.

  • Co-design of urine culture stewardship in ED

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Qualitative · Context only

    Qualitative co-design study at Townsville ED (7 workshops, 35 emergency physicians) building a theory-informed intervention against low-value urine cultures — culture, complexity and efficiency drove over-ordering, with education, nudges, clinical champions and audit/feedback chosen as the fix.

  • AI triage of ED CT brain scans

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective validation of an AI model classifying 3,424 ED CT brain scans as urgent or not — 98.3% negative predictive value with no false negative needing urgent intervention at the index visit, putting ~64% of scans in the possibly-expedite bucket pending a prospective trial.

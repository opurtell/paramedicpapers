Paramedic research, 29 August 2026

13 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 29 August 2026 scan.

  • Adrenaline given in only 60% of paediatric OHCA

    Resuscitation Plus · Registry · Context only

    Retrospective analysis of US NEMSIS data found adrenaline was administered in only 60% of paediatric out-of-hospital cardiac arrests, with longer scene times, advanced airway attempts, and defibrillation associated with higher odds of administration, while public settings and female sex correlated with lower odds.

  • Predicting prehospital blood need after MVC trauma

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Machine learning models using NEMSIS data achieved strong discrimination (AUC 0.906) for predicting prehospital blood transfusion after motor vehicle trauma, identifying physiologic measures and shock designation as key predictors.

  • Paediatric trauma diagnosis accuracy in Swiss HEMS

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    Multi-centre Swiss HEMS study of 282 paediatric trauma patients found moderate diagnostic accuracy between prehospital and in-hospital diagnoses (Cohen's κ=0.64), with polytrauma underdiagnosed and extremity/pelvic injuries overdiagnosed, and overall survival of 92.2%.

  • Community paramedics for paediatric concussion follow-up

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    Experimental mixed-methods study demonstrated that a Mobile Integrated Health program using community paramedics for paediatric concussion follow-up was feasible and acceptable, with 19% patient enrolment and improvement in Post-Concussion Symptoms Scale scores.

  • Indirect ICU admissions from Australian ED

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Cohort · Context only

    Australian observational study found indirect ICU admissions (ED admission to non-critical care ward requiring ICU transfer within 24 hours) accounted for 11.3% of ED-to-ICU admissions, with circulatory insufficiency as the most common reason and hospital mortality of 12.8%.

  • First responders don't shift OHCA survival

    British Paramedic Journal · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective cohort testing whether community first responder attendance improves OHCA survival in North East England — it didn't, with survival to discharge essentially unchanged (9.6% vs 10.2%, p=0.797) after Utstein adjustment.

  • Sedating CPR-induced consciousness: no answers

    British Paramedic Journal · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    Systematic review asking whether pharmacological management of CPR-induced consciousness in prehospital cardiac arrest affects mortality — only two Australian cohort studies were found, and medicated patients had lower survival, though the evidence is too confounded to act on.

  • Paralysing post-ROSC patients prehospital

    British Paramedic Journal · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    UK cohort of advanced paramedics in critical care giving neuromuscular blockade to post-ROSC patients poorly tolerating the tube — 99 patients over a year, all PGD indications met, with no adverse physiological trends in sats, end-tidal CO2, MAP or heart rate.

  • HART paramedics delivering lyophilised plasma

    British Paramedic Journal · Other · Context only

    UK service evaluation of hazardous area response team paramedics delivering lyophilised plasma under a major haemorrhage protocol — 17 patients received 30 units over 12 months with full protocol authorisation, low wastage (3.1%) and no safety incidents.

  • Caller triage score for paediatric breathing calls

    British Paramedic Journal · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    Development of the PREP score, a caller-reported decision-support tool for children aged 0-4 with breathing problems in a Welsh dispatch system — moderate discrimination (AUC 0.73) with high negative predictive value (93%) for identifying low-risk calls.

  • What makes paramedic teleconsultation work

    British Paramedic Journal · Qualitative · Context only

    Qualitative study of 12 UK critical care and clinical advisory paramedics on teleconsultation — its effectiveness hinges on a 'teleconsultation competence gap' in training, organisational culture and whether governance feels punitive rather than supportive.

  • Paramedics working in UK general practice

    British Paramedic Journal · Cross-sectional · Context only

    Survey of 37 paramedics employed in Cornwall general practices — all saw urgent patients in clinic or at home, 38% were non-medical prescribers, and 13 different job titles were in use, highlighting unclear employment and training models.

  • Pregnancy and return-to-work guidance for prehospital staff

    British Paramedic Journal · Guideline / statement · Context only

    UK Faculty of Pre-hospital Care guidelines on pregnancy, maternity and return to work — every prehospital organisation should run individualised risk assessments, support maternal choice and offer personalised return-to-work programmes.

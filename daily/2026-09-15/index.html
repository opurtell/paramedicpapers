  • Gelatin and HES impair clot formation ex vivo

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    Ex vivo human whole-blood model testing graded dilution with gelatin 4%, HES 130/0.42 and a balanced crystalloid — both colloids produced a clearly hypocoagulable ROTEM profile (prolonged clotting and clot formation times, reduced alpha-angle and maximum clot firmness, worst with HES) while the crystalloid alone stayed haemostatically neutral, and combining crystalloid with colloid dilution worsened the coagulopathy further.

  • CPR quality maintained across paramedic-to-ED handover

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cohort · Context only

    Observational study of 35 OHCA transfers tracking CPR quality across the paramedic-to-ED handover — chest compression fraction (89–92%) and rate stayed guideline-compliant in every interval with no deterioration at transition, though compression depth was significantly deeper during paramedic intervals.

  • HFNO reduces intubation vs conventional oxygen

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    Systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 RCTs (1,955 patients) comparing high-flow nasal oxygen with conventional oxygen and NIV for acute respiratory failure in the ED — HFNO reduced intubation versus conventional oxygen (RR 0.61) but not versus NIV, with no clear benefit over either in COPD or acute heart failure.

  • Knowledge gaps in local anaesthetic dosing and LAST

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    Multicentre survey of 114 emergency clinicians across three Queensland EDs — fewer than half correctly identified the recommended maximum dose for any of four local anaesthetics, 21.9% couldn't recall any features of LAST, and only 10.5% could state the lipid emulsion dosing regimen.

  • Fatal illicit drug toxicity: mostly polysubstance OHCA

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Case series · Context only

    Case series of 46 deaths in Victoria's EDNAV toxicosurveillance registry (2020–2025) — 67% had out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, 91% were polysubstance with mixed stimulant–sedative patterns most common (methamphetamine 54%, heroin 52%), and hypoxic brain injury was the most frequent terminal complication.

  • Embedded low-acuity pathway improves rural ED performance

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Quasi-experimental study of an embedded ultra-low acuity pathway at Dubbo Base Hospital (n=4,798) — pathway availability halved did-not-wait rates (14.2%→6.9%), lifted seen-on-time and treatment performance, and cut mean ED length of stay by 51.7 minutes.

  • Frostbite depth and extent predict amputation

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective cohort of 128 frostbite admissions in eastern Turkey — injury depth drove amputation risk (81.5% of third-degree injuries required amputation vs 1.6% of first/second-degree), and combining degree with %TBSA predicted amputation at admission (AUC 0.948), while inflammatory markers added nothing after adjustment.

  • Delphi consensus on paediatric procedural sedation

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Guideline / statement · Context only

    Two-round Delphi consensus of 28 paediatric emergency clinicians from 23 Spanish centres — strong agreement on monitoring standards, staffing and safety requirements for paediatric procedural sedation, with ketamine the preferred agent for painful procedures.

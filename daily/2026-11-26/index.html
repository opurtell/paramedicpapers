Daily scan

Paramedic research, 26 November 2026

Cardiac arrest timing dominated the day: a 38-study synthesis confirms the shockable-rhythm window closes with every minute to defibrillation, and Austrian simulation work builds the time case for prehospital eCPR.

  • Shockable rhythms decay fast in untreated arrest

    International Emergency Nursing · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    Italian-led systematic review of 38 studies (1990–2025, more than 262,700 adult OHCA cases) found shockable rhythms decline progressively with increasing time-to-defibrillation, with earlier shock linked to higher defibrillation success, more ROSC and better survival.

  • Simulated prehospital eCPR shows large time savings

    Prehospital and Disaster Medicine · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    Austrian simulation study comparing 23 simulated prehospital eCPR activations with 25 real in-hospital eCPR patients found the prehospital pathway reached the cannulation decision far sooner (18 vs 49 minutes from call), shortening estimated call-to-ECMO-flow time.

  • Heat and ozone together raise cardiac arrest risk

    Environment & Health · Case-control · Hypothesis-generating

    Japanese nationwide case-crossover analysis of 610,613 cardiac-origin OHCAs (2012–2019) found ground-level ozone and extreme heat act synergistically, with each 10 ppb ozone rise above a ~60 ppb threshold linked to a 4% higher OHCA risk at extreme heat.

  • Recorded burn cooling varied by family citizenship

    Nursing Reports · Cohort · Context only

    A Florentine single-centre cohort of 369 hospitalised paediatric burns found recorded pre-hospital first aid differed by citizenship, with non-Italian children less often recorded as receiving running-water cooling (−17.8 percentage points) and more often 'no first aid' or a remedy.

  • Where transfer delay hits paediatric testicular torsion

    World Journal of Urology · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    Chinese referral-centre modelling of 311 boys with surgically confirmed testicular torsion estimated that eliminating transfer-associated excess prehospital delay would cut nonviability risk from 42.1% to 34.7% and orchiectomy from 33.4% to 27.3%.

  • Gen Z learning evidence skips paramedicine

    Journal of Medical Education and Curricular Development · Systematic review · Context only

    Czech-led scoping review mapping Generation Z learning preferences across six health professions identified three themes — technology-supported learning, active/interactive approaches, and autonomy with feedback — but found no eligible studies in paramedicine.

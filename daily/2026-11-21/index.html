Daily scan

Paramedic research, 21 November 2026

OHCA day: a 19-year Japanese national registry found bystanders doing rescue-breathing CPR on children had better neurological survival than compression-only CPR, just as compression-only messaging has taken over. Plus paediatric on-scene intervention rates from Los Angeles and an onboard blood-storage study.

  • Rescue breaths linked to better paediatric arrest survival

    JAMA Network Open · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A 19-year Japanese national registry cohort of 26,785 children with OHCA found compression-only bystander CPR rose from 14.6% to 55.1% of cases as rescue-breathing CPR fell from 41.7% to 10.4%, and rescue-breathing CPR was associated with higher 30-day neurologically favourable survival than compression-only CPR (9.5%; 3.6% with no bystander CPR).

  • Incomplete on-scene care in paediatric cardiac arrest

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A US retrospective analysis of 176 paediatric non-traumatic OHCA cases in Los Angeles County found goal-directed on-scene interventions before transport were often incomplete — adrenaline in only 41% of non-shockable arrests — with 6% survival to discharge.

  • Blood stored on ambulances stays within limits to day 42

    Prehospital Emergency Care · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A French randomised experimental study stored 12 red cell units continuously in operational ground ambulances for 35 days and found all units stayed within the European 0.8% haemolysis limit at day 42, though ambulance-stored units showed more haemolysis and storage-induced microerythrocytes than blood-bank controls.

  • Blood biomarker assay fits inside an EMS transport window

    Trauma Surgery & Acute Care Open · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    A US pilot simulation study had 24 paramedics run a whole-blood point-of-care GFAP/UCH-L1 bioassay workflow (i-STAT Alinity with teleconsent via Pulsara) for simulated mild TBI and found a median 6.5 minutes from consent to assay completion with over 90% rating the training sufficient.

  • Five dispatch codes drive a third of prehospital blood use

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A US analysis of Delaware's prehospital blood database found five trauma dispatch codes — led by high-mechanism crashes and penetrating trauma with central wounds — accounted for 29% of low-titre O whole blood given over 33 months.

  • Machine-learning OHCA survival model from 370 patients

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    A Chinese single-centre retrospective study built an AutoML model on 370 OHCA patients that predicted ROSC and 30-day survival with a test-set ROC-AUC of 0.98, with on-scene defibrillation, cumulative adrenaline dose and time to CPR the leading features.

  • More arrests, more TORs during the pandemic years

    Western Journal of Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    A US retrospective analysis of 4,423 OHCA encounters in Minnesota and Wisconsin communities found OHCA incidence rose across all seasons during the pandemic (largest spike fall 2020), termination-of-resuscitation rates increased from 56.6% to 61.2%, and scene times lengthened modestly.

  • Smoking flags wider heart risk in EMS staff

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A US cross-sectional survey analysis of nationally certified EMS clinicians found current smokers had poorer cardiovascular health beyond the tobacco exposure itself — lower adjusted Life's Essential 7 scores (β −6.02) and lower odds of ideal cardiovascular health (aOR 0.37) than never smokers.

  • Debriefing works, but nobody reports it the same way

    BMC Nursing · Systematic review · Context only

    A scoping review of 18 studies (2005–2025) on debriefing after prehospital simulation for practising paramedics and ambulance nurses found debriefing was associated with better technical performance, communication and teamwork, but how it was run and reported varied widely.

  • Heat-alert days bring more ambulance dispatches, not fewer

    International Journal of Biometeorology · Cohort · Context only

    A Japanese time-series study matching heat-alert days with similar-heat non-alert days across 47 prefectures found emergency ambulance dispatches rose on alert days — all-cause +3.11% and heatstroke +31.1% — with no significant mortality difference.

  • Community paramedicine programs can't agree on names

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Qualitative · Context only

    A US qualitative study interviewing 23 expanded-EMS programs in Ohio found inconsistent use and interpretation of mobile integrated health and community paramedicine terminology, with one in four programs using alternative names.

  • Flipped classroom beats lectures for ECG skills

    BMC Medical Education · Other · Context only

    An Iranian quasi-experimental pre-post study comparing flipped-classroom with lecture-based teaching found the flipped group had significantly higher ECG interpretation competency scores at two weeks and one month after training among prehospital emergency personnel.

Daily scan

Paramedic research, 20 November 2026

A quiet clinical day led by two strong pieces: updated North American guidance on paediatric traumatic cardiac arrest, and a 105,000-case Canadian registry showing choking-related arrests get a pulse back often but rarely survive to discharge.

  • Updated paediatric traumatic arrest position statement

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Guideline / statement · Supports current practice

    A US joint position statement and resource document from NAEMSP, ACS-COT, ACEP and AAP updates guidance on withholding or discontinuing resuscitation in paediatric traumatic out-of-hospital circulatory arrest, prioritising reversible causes, rapid haemorrhage control, least-invasive airway care and transport to a trauma centre when safe and appropriate.

  • Choking arrests: ROSC common, survival still poor

    Resuscitation · Registry · Supports current practice

    A Canadian CanROC registry cohort of 105,178 EMS-treated out-of-hospital cardiac arrests (2005–2023) found 1,106 (1.1%) were obviously foreign-body-airway-obstruction-related: prehospital ROSC was far more frequent than in presumed cardiac arrests (62.7% vs 37.8%) but survival to discharge was similar (10.6% vs 9.0%).

  • Traumatic pneumothorax: most EDs don't drain early

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Cohort · Context only

    An Australian two-centre retrospective cohort of 339 ED patients with traumatic pneumothorax found two-thirds were managed conservatively before leaving the ED, and of the 50 who arrived after a prehospital pleural intervention, 27 had chest drains inserted to formalise finger thoracostomy or needle decompression.

  • Studying barriers to trauma triage tool use

    HRB Open Research · Other · Context only

    An Irish study protocol describes a qualitative project using the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research to explore barriers and enablers to using the HSE trauma triage tool among up to 30 Dublin Fire Brigade paramedics, advanced paramedics, EMTs, firefighters and managers.

  • Shift work is rough on paramedics' teeth

    BMC Oral Health · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A Hungarian nationwide cross-sectional survey of 1,061 National Ambulance Service staff found over half (54.9%) reported current or recent dental or oral problems, and on-duty hygiene-kit availability was strongly associated with daily on-shift brushing (85% vs 21%) among 24-hour shift workers.

Paramedic research, 10 November 2026

6 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 10 November 2026 scan.

Listen to the 10 November 2026 podcast episode

  • Prehospital ECPR linked to doubled arrest survival

    The New England Journal of Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A nationwide Dutch study compared HEMS with prehospital ECPR against conventional HEMS in adults 50 or younger with refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and found 40% survival to discharge with ECPR versus 20% without, with better neurological outcomes at 6 months.

  • Big community arrest programme didn't change survival

    The New England Journal of Medicine · RCT · Supports current practice

    A cluster-randomised trial across 55 North Carolina county clusters (22,459 patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest) found a multifaceted community and EMS programme did not improve survival with good neurological outcome (9.0% vs 9.4%).

  • Intubation linked to more ROSC in OHCA registry

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Chinese regional EMS registry cohort of 6,162 adult non-traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients found prehospital endotracheal intubation associated with more prehospital ROSC than basic airways (21.1% vs 3.8%, propensity-matched OR 6.33).

  • Whole blood vs components: mixed mortality, lower transfusion needs

    International Journal of Medicine in Developing Countries · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    A PRISMA systematic review of 22 comparative studies (about 95,000 patients) on whole blood versus component therapy for trauma resuscitation found mixed mortality results — survival signals in hypotensive, penetrating and geriatric subgroups but not everywhere — with consistently lower transfusion requirements and comparable or better safety with whole blood.

  • Heat alerts eased heat-dispatch risk but demand still grew

    Environmental Health Perspectives · Time-series · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese multi-prefecture time-series analysis of 577,027 heat-related ambulance dispatches (2015–2023) found the association between wet-bulb globe temperature and dispatches attenuated by 44% after the pandemic and national heatstroke alerts, yet absolute heat-attributable demand still grew 18%.

  • EMS students report obs/gyn training gaps

    BMC Medical Education · Qualitative · Hypothesis-generating

    An Iranian qualitative study of 14 EMS students and recent graduates identified six categories of educational challenge in obstetric and gynaecologic emergencies, centred on a disconnect between educational preparation and real-world demands, with calls for more simulation, clinical exposure and gender-sensitive communication training.

Paramedic research, 3 November 2026

11 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 3 November 2026 scan.

Listen to the 3 November 2026 podcast episode

  • Prehospital stroke triage: high under- and overtriage rates

    Swiss Medical Weekly · Cohort · Context only

    A Swiss single-centre quality-control study of a prehospital stroke triage pathway found EMS undertriaged 21% and overtriaged 63% of acute reperfusion candidates, with both rates falling when a coordinated medical team (including a neurologist) was phone-consulted.

  • Reported LOC didn't flag post-traumatic amnesia in ED

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    An Australian retrospective cohort of 6,259 adults tested with the Abbreviated Westmead PTA Scale after head injury found 91.8% passed, with post-traumatic amnesia associated with initial GCS 13–14 but not with reported loss of consciousness.

  • Wearables detecting cardiac arrest: systematic review

    Resuscitation · Systematic review · Context only

    Abstract not yet available — this Resuscitation systematic review maps wearable-based automated cardiac arrest detection and alerting systems.

  • Predicting ground vs helicopter EMS travel times

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Modelling · Context only

    A modelling study validated open-source routing and historical-data methods for predicting ground and helicopter EMS travel times, achieving R² of 0.79 for ground drive time to scene but only 0.38 for helicopter time to hospital.

  • Mapping anti-violence measures for ambulance personnel

    HRB Open Research · Other · Context only

    An Irish scoping-review protocol will map measures against verbal and physical violence toward ambulance personnel — reported by 57–93% of prehospital responders in a career — across five safety-science and human-factors frameworks.

  • EMS rarely suspects PE, but detection speeds CT

    Journal of Clinical Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A German retrospective cohort of 171 EMS-transported patients with confirmed pulmonary embolism found prehospital crews correctly suspected PE in only 5.3% of cases, but correct detection was associated with time to CT pulmonary angiography halving (median 2.1 vs 4.0 hours).

  • Workplace violence follows prehospital staff home

    Journal of Clinical Nursing · Qualitative · Context only

    An Italian qualitative study of 15 prehospital nurses and ambulance drivers who had experienced workplace violence described violence being taken home, under-recognised microaggressions shaped by gender and culture, and coping built on de-escalation, situational awareness and peer support amid thin organisational backing.

  • Paediatric transport ventilation: nCPAP first, transfers dominate

    South African Medical Journal · Cohort · Context only

    A South African retrospective review of 1,548 ventilated under-fives in Western Cape EMS found 99.4% were interfacility transfers, mostly for respiratory distress, with non-invasive support dominating (nCPAP 66.5%, high-flow 14.5%) over invasive ventilation (18.9%).

  • AI in prehospital care: cautious optimism, thin validation

    AI in Medicine · Review · Context only

    A critical narrative review of AI in emergency and prehospital care found the most credible near-term uses are bounded decision support — triage, risk stratification, stroke pathways, forecasting, documentation — while retrospective designs, thin external validation and workflow gaps keep real-world benefit unproven.

  • Trained ChatGPT matched paramedics on triage scenarios

    Fire Science and Engineering · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    A Korean simulation study of 100 prehospital triage scenarios found a Pre-KTAS-trained ChatGPT matched 10 active-duty paramedics on accuracy (0.81 vs 0.80) with the lowest under-triage rate (6.1%), while an untrained LLM lagged well behind at 0.69.

  • Remote reads of paramedic ultrasound held up in pilot

    JMIR Human Factors · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    A usability pilot had four physicians interpret paramedic-acquired lung and IVC ultrasound remotely via a cloud platform across laptop, tablet and smartphone, with substantial diagnostic agreement (0.68–0.80) and the laptop version rated most usable.

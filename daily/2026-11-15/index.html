Paramedic research, 15 November 2026

An Australian pilot simulation found a one-hour airway-assistant course nearly halved intubation time in a continuously regurgitating airway, while Japanese Utstein data quantified how quickly the odds of on-scene ROSC collapse after 10 minutes of resuscitation.

  • Airway assistant training halved sim intubation time

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    An Australian pilot randomised simulation of 12 paramedic–ICP pairs found a one-hour airway assistant training course cut median intubation time in a continuously regurgitating airway from 102.5 to 53 seconds.

  • Numbers for when on-scene ROSC stops happening

    Journal of Clinical Medicine · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese national Utstein cohort of 22,383 adults with shockable cardiogenic OHCA found the probability of subsequent on-scene ROSC fell from 6.97% at 10 minutes to 0.50% at 20 minutes of resuscitation, with favourable neurological outcome in 50.0% of under-75s achieving on-scene ROSC at or after 10 minutes.

  • Co-dispatch modelled to cut OHCA response times

    Journal of Clinical Medicine · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    An Austrian geospatial simulation of 500 modelled OHCA locations found police and volunteer fire brigade co-dispatch cut modelled median first-unit response time from 9.81 to 5.41 minutes and lifted 10-minute coverage from 51.1% to 94.6%.

  • Social support buffers the violence-to-burnout pathway

    Healthcare · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A South Korean cross-sectional study of 210 fire-service paramedics found workplace violence exposure was associated with burnout indirectly through post-traumatic stress symptoms, with social support weakening that pathway.

  • Dispatch stress, depression and suicidal ideation linked

    Healthcare · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A South Korean survey of 211 emergency dispatch personnel found secondary traumatic stress and depression were associated with suicidal ideation, with the indirect pathway through depression weakening as social support rose.

  • Hot days, more injury calls to ambulance services

    Ecotoxicology and Environmental Safety · Case-control · Context only

    A Shanghai case-crossover study of 170,538 unintentional injury-related EMS calls found extreme apparent temperature (42.5 °C vs −2.9 °C) was associated with 21% higher odds of injury calls, peaking at 80% higher for traffic injuries.

  • Mapping 50 years of EMS cardiac arrest research

    International Journal of Emergency Medicine · Other · Context only

    A US-led bibliometric analysis of 3,005 Web of Science records from 1972–2026 mapped EMS-focused OHCA research, showing output above 200 papers a year since 2022 and registry-centred international collaboration.

  • Prehospital TXA lowers mortality in bleeding trauma

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    A systematic review of randomised evidence, authored in Saudi Arabia, pooled three trials of prehospital TXA in trauma and found lower 28–30-day mortality (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68–0.95) without a significant rise in thromboembolic events or seizures.

  • Bystander-attached AEDs linked with best OHCA survival

    Resuscitation · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A Swedish registry cohort of 9,413 OHCAs in Västra Götaland found bystander-attached AEDs were associated with the highest 30-day survival (28% vs 9.0% when EMS attached first, OR 2.25), pointing to earlier defibrillation as the key lever.

  • First responder alerts linked to more defibrillation

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Danish registry study of 5,753 OHCAs found HeartRunner community-first-responder activation was associated with more bystander defibrillation at every AED distance (TMLE +6.1 pp) and a modest 1.8-point gain in 30-day survival.

  • Mobile app lifts simulated trauma performance

    Prehospital Emergency Care · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A US crossover RCT found paramedic pairs using a mobile app with protocols, decision-support tools and procedure videos completed all critical actions in simulated trauma far more often than with standard practice (35.4% vs 6.3%).

  • Prehospital STEMI thrombolysis is value for money

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Modelling · Supports current practice

    An Australian microsimulation of the Queensland prehospital thrombolysis program estimated paramedic-administered STEMI thrombolysis costs AUD 16,750 per QALY gained, concluding it is value for money.

  • Five years of automated head-up CPR outcomes

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A five-year US registry of 3,709 OHCAs treated with automated head-up CPR (head/thorax positioning plus active compression–decompression and an impedance threshold device) reported 10.5% survival to discharge with favourable neurology, 91% of survivors at CPC 1.

  • MIRA-29: early prehospital prognosis after arrest

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A French derivation-and-validation study built the MIRA-29 score from five prehospital variables and externally validated it on 82,696 registry patients, identifying poor neurological outcome with 0.99 specificity.

  • Digital support pathway for OHCA bystanders feasible

    Resuscitation · Other · Context only

    A one-year UK service evaluation in Essex found the RescQ digital bystander support pathway feasible, with 50.9% of eligible OHCA incidents captured and 96.2% of registered adult bystanders accessing the resources.

  • Video calls showed no clear resource savings

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    A post hoc analysis of 18,745 calls from the Danish CAM-VISION cluster-randomised trial found video-assisted emergency calls produced no statistically significant reduction in prehospital resource use, though point estimates consistently favoured video.

  • Neonatal chest compressions: most survive a month

    Resuscitation · Cohort · Context only

    An Australian population cohort of 3,077 Queensland infants who received chest compressions at birth found 89.0% survived to 28 days, with survival improving from 82% in 2008 to 89% in 2024.

  • Chinese OHCA registry: low survival, low AED use

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Registry · Context only

    A Chinese province-wide registry study of 17,646 EMS-treated OHCAs in Zhejiang found 3.0% survived to 30 days, with bystander CPR in 47.7% and bystander AED use just 1.8%.

  • OHCA cause attribution shifts with definitions

    BMC Public Health · Registry · Context only

    A Chinese registry–death-registration linkage study in Zhejiang showed cardiac cause attribution in OHCA ranged from 20.7% to 42.3% depending on attribution rules and eligibility definitions used.

  • Clinician smartphones linked to tripled interpreter use

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A US pre-post cohort found issuing EMS clinicians lightweight smartphones (in place of heavy laptops) tripled virtual interpreter use, from 0.19 to 0.57 requests per 100 encounters.

  • Extrication after road collisions: an evidence map

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Systematic review · Context only

    An international scoping review mapped the rescue and extrication literature after road traffic collisions and found fragmented definitions, inconsistent time intervals and heterogeneous outcomes, with no clear effectiveness evidence for any extrication approach.

  • A US systems approach to MCI triage

    The Surgical Clinics of North America · Other · Context only

    A US systems review of mass-casualty prehospital triage and transport promotes SALT as the national triage standard, with casualty collection points, ambulance exchange points and incident-command-led transport by a designated transport officer.

  • Self-efficacy predicts bystander willingness for CPR

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    A Malaysian mixed-methods study of 449 community members trained in CPR found self-efficacy the strongest predictor of willingness to perform CPR and use an AED, with attitude and perceived norms contributing far less.

  • Paramedic students learn safety from unsafe events

    Paramedicine · Qualitative · Context only

    A Czech qualitative study of 58 undergraduate paramedic students using structured SLERT narratives found patient-safety learning in placements was shaped by role modelling, feedback, emotional processing and reflective meaning-making.

  • Drowning and trauma lead Swedish mountain deaths

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Cohort · Context only

    A Swedish study of mountain rescue fatalities (2018–2023) found drowning and traumatic injuries the leading causes of death among 26 autopsied cases, with half the victims alone when the fatal event occurred.

  • Protocol: climate and emergency dispatch volumes

    JMIR Research Protocols · Other · Context only

    A German study protocol plans to link emergency medical dispatch data from two northern metropolitan regions with high-resolution climate data to model temperature–dispatch relationships — no results yet.

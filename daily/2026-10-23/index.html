Daily scan

Paramedic research, 23 October 2026

A paediatric safety day: an English registry found infant carry-sling cardiac arrests are vanishingly rare (2 in five years) compared with co-sleeping, and US data links paediatric EMS care coordinators to fewer harmful errors, while a Japanese trauma registry challenges the GCS 8 threshold.

  • Infant carry slings rarely linked to OHCA

    Resuscitation Plus · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A five-year English regional registry review of 162 cardiac arrests in children under two found only two linked to infant carry slings, while co-sleeping featured in a third of cases.

  • No unique GCS 8 threshold for intubation

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A two-era Japan Trauma Data Bank analysis of roughly 138,000 adults found recorded intubation more likely at GCS 8 than 9, but no evidence of a unique risk threshold at that boundary.

  • Paediatric deterioration in the field poorly defined

    Paramedicine · Systematic review · Context only

    A scoping review of 28 studies found no standardised definition of paediatric out-of-hospital clinical deterioration and almost no research on how crews actually recognise it.

  • Paediatric care coordinators linked to fewer EMS errors

    Prehospital Emergency Care · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    A US cross-sectional study of 2,219 paediatric EMS encounters found agencies with a paediatric emergency care coordinator delivered more procedures and medications, with fewer and less severe adverse safety events.

  • AU/NZ emergency care research priorities being set

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · Guideline / statement · Context only

    ACEM's Clinical Trials Network, with the James Lind Alliance, has launched a priority setting partnership to set the emergency care research agenda for Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand for the next 5–10 years.

Paramedic research, 17 October 2026

10 new paramedic and prehospital papers from the 17 October 2026 scan.

Listen to the 17 October 2026 podcast episode

  • POC GFAP for brain haemorrhage in coma

    JAMA Network Open · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A UK physician-paramedic HEMS team tested whether prehospital point-of-care GFAP and UCH-L1 blood tests could detect intracranial haemorrhage in comatose patients — GFAP performed strongly (97% sensitivity, AUC 0.94), UCH-L1 less so.

  • Every minute counts: early bystander CPR

    Resuscitation Plus · Registry · Supports current practice

    A time-to-event analysis of the All-Japan Utstein registry found overall bystander CPR rates stayed stable through the pandemic, but initiation within 10 minutes slipped — and earlier starts sharply improved neurological outcomes (aOR 3.27 for 0–3 min vs >10 min).

  • Commotio cordis outcomes hinge on fast AED

    Acute Medicine & Surgery · Registry · Supports current practice

    A nationwide Japanese school-sports database reconstructed 30 sports-related OHCAs from external causes and found commotio cordis cases did dramatically better when AEDs were applied fast — 80% favourable neurological outcome — while other external causes fared poorly.

  • Prehospital qSOFA adds a little prognostic signal

    Journal of Clinical Medicine · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Turkish retrospective cohort tested whether the prehospital qSOFA already recorded on the chart adds prognostic information for ambulance-transported suspected infection — it predicted 30-day mortality within every ED triage stratum but added almost nothing to a full arrival model (ΔAUC 0.007).

  • Prehospital ACS risk stratification: the evidence

    Clinical and Experimental Emergency Medicine · Other · Context only

    A narrative review of prehospital ACS risk stratification found HEART-derived pathways combined with point-of-care troponin carry the strongest evidence for safe field risk assessment, while AI-assisted ECG interpretation remains promising but prospectively unvalidated.

  • Burnout linked to suicidal risk in EMS

    Psychological Services · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A cross-sectional survey of 315 EMS professionals found burnout, anxiety and depression each tracked with higher suicidal risk, with elevated scores among respondents outside the male/female binary.

  • TeamSTEPPS training shows no measurable gain

    BMC Emergency Medicine · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A Norwegian TeamSTEPPS programme for ambulance staff measured teamwork and safety-culture perceptions at baseline, 4 and 12 months — and found no statistically significant change in any dimension despite the structured training.

  • Paramedics strained by end-of-life care gaps

    Healthcare · Cross-sectional · Context only

    A UK survey of 182 paramedics found end-of-life care in the community is undermined by practical gaps — 54% often had no access to the medicines they needed, 88% couldn't reach advance care planning documentation, and only 28% felt competent in the role.

  • Most physician-unit dispatches are overtriage

    Wiener Klinische Wochenschrift · Cohort · Context only

    A retrospective Austrian analysis of 3,454 dispatches of a physician-staffed response vehicle found 76% met retrospective overtriage criteria, and dispatch priority didn't predict appropriateness at all.

  • Heel-of-foot compressions beat ball-of-foot

    Resuscitation Plus · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    A manikin study of leg-delivered chest compressions found the heel-of-foot technique achieved deeper and more often guideline-adequate compressions than the ball of the foot, with foot orientation making no difference.

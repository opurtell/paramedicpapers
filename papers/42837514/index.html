Bystander AEDs work — when someone actually grabs one

Bystander AED effectiveness despite low utilisation in OHCA
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-10-06 · Added 8 October 2026

Korean multicentre OHCA registry analysis estimating bystander AED effectiveness against a no-defibrillation reference — AED application was associated with outcomes comparable to or better than EMS shocks, but was applied in only a fraction of eligible cases, especially at home.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective multicentre registry analysis with propensity and weighting methods
Participants
23,918 adults with presumed medical OHCA (383 bystander AED applications)
Main finding
Bystander AED application was associated with higher survival (aOR 1.60) and good neurological outcome (aOR 2.31) versus no prehospital defibrillation, comparable to EMS-delivered shock; yet AEDs were applied in only 8.5% of public-area versus 1.9% of non-public opportunity cases
Caveat
Only 383 bystander AED cases in nearly a decade, all from a single country's registry — the estimates are imprecise and system-specific
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Public-access AEDs are widespread in Australia but underused for the same reasons — this quantifies the cost of the deployment-utilisation gap rather than the devices themselves.

Learning goal

Strengthen my approach to promoting early bystander defibrillation when engaging communities about out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

    Resuscitation · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Danish national registry study of bystander-witnessed OHCA survivors asking whether longer time-to-ROSC means worse long-term outcomes — anoxic brain damage rose with time, but long-term survival, return to work and nursing-home admission did not, showing prolonged resuscitation doesn't preclude good recovery.

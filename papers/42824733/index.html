In-hospital arrests in resource-limited Somalia

In-hospital cardiac arrest in a resource-limited emergency department in Somalia: Outcomes and priorities for resuscitation system development
Resuscitation Plus · Added 22 September 2026

Prospective study of 240 in-hospital arrests in a Mogadishu ED — ROSC 5.8%, survival past 24 hours 1.3%, with CPR initiation documented in only 15% of cases, underlining system gaps rather than treatment questions

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective single-centre observational study
Participants
240 adult in-hospital cardiac arrests in a Mogadishu ED
Main finding
ROSC achieved in 5.8% and survival beyond 24 hours in 1.3%; CPR initiation documented in only 14.6% and emergency response activation in 22.6%
Caveat
Single resource-limited in-hospital setting with incomplete documentation; not generalisable and not prehospital
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). transferability — resource-limited hospital setting with a trauma-dominated case mix — little transfers to a well-resourced two-tier ambulance service

Learning goal

Understand how system gaps in emergency response activation affect resuscitation outcomes in resource-limited settings.

  • Dispatcher CPR barriers delay bystander CPR, cut survival

    Resuscitation · 24 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective cohort of 3,263 transcribed Swedish 112 calls linked to the national cardiac arrest registry quantified barriers to dispatcher-assisted CPR after the dispatcher recognised arrest — 43.7% of calls hit at least one barrier (commonest: inability to move the patient), bystander CPR reached 85% in barrier-free calls but stayed below 50% with three or more, and any barrier was associated with lower ROSC (adjusted RR 0.89) and 30-day survival (adjusted RR 0.57).

  • Post-arrest biomarker platforms don't agree

    Resuscitation Plus · 22 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Platform-comparison study of post-arrest HIBI biomarkers (GFAP, Nf-L, tau, UCH-L1) — lab-grade analyser results correlated highly with each other, but the point-of-care i-STAT agreed poorly, so prognostication thresholds can't be shared across devices

  • Smartphone CPR app: ROSC up, survival not

    Resuscitation Plus · 22 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Before–after study of 615 OHCA patients after the MySOS smartphone bystander-CPR app launched in Bangkok — post-implementation ROSC probability rose ~6 percentage points, but 24-hour and discharge survival showed no significant improvement

  • Bystander CPR benefits greatest in deprived areas

    Resuscitation Plus · 22 Sep 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 184,727 Korean OHCA cases found bystander CPR rates were lowest in the most deprived regions, yet its association with good neurological outcome was strongest there (aOR 1.56 vs 1.31–1.34 in better-off regions)

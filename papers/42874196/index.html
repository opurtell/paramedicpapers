Signs of life in traumatic OHCA

Signs of Life and Clinical Outcomes in Patients With Traumatic Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Nationwide Multicenter Observational Study
The Journal of Emergency Medicine · 2026-09-23 · Added 16 October 2026

Retrospective analysis of 462 adults with traumatic OHCA from a Japanese prospective registry found 4.8% survived beyond the ED, with the best survival where scene cardiac activity and spontaneous respiration coexisted (25%).

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective analysis of a prospective multicentre OHCA registry
Participants
462 adults with traumatic OHCA, no pulse at EMS arrival
Main finding
4.8% survived beyond the ED; 25% where cardiac activity and spontaneous respiration were both present at scene, 8.5% with cardiac activity alone, and none among the 318 patients (68.8%) with no signs of life
Caveat
Retrospective descriptive analysis with survival beyond the ED only — no discharge or neurological outcomes
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Scene prognostication in traumatic arrest is core paramedic decision-making everywhere, but this is a descriptive Japanese registry with survival beyond the ED as the endpoint rather than discharge or neurological outcome.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when signs of life at scene justify ongoing resuscitation in traumatic cardiac arrest.

    Physiology International · 15 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Nationwide Hungarian cohort of 3,674 shockable OHCAs found dispatcher-assisted CPR ongoing at ambulance arrival was associated with far better outcomes than crew-initiated CPR — 30-day survival 54.6% vs 7.1% and one-year survival 45.8% vs 6.2%.

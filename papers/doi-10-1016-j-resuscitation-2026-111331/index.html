Women's OHCA survival edge fades by discharge

Sex differences in epidemiology, care, and outcomes in patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in China
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 26 September 2026

A prospective Chinese multicentre cohort of 33,650 EMS-treated OHCA patients found women had better prehospital ROSC and survival to admission than men but no advantage at discharge, with the early benefit lost in older and shockable-rhythm patients.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective multicentre population-based observational cohort
Participants
33,650 EMS-treated OHCA patients (BASIC-OHCA, 2020)
Main finding
Women had higher odds of prehospital ROSC (aOR 1.21) and survival to admission (aOR 1.33) but no significant difference in survival to discharge or favourable neurological outcome
Caveat
Single-country cohort with large unmeasured differences in prehospital care pathways and male-female arrest aetiology
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A Chinese EMS system differs from Australian models in dispatcher coverage and bystander rates, but the sex-difference question is one Australian registries are also probing, so it informs rather than guides practice.

Learning goal

Recognise how sex influences resuscitation pathways and outcomes in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest care in Australia.

Stroke app speeds prehospital-to-ED handover

Digital Health-Enabled Prehospital Notification for Acute Stroke Using the Brain Saver Mobile App in Emergency Medical Transport in South Korea: Pilot Quasi-Experimental Study
JMIR Medical Informatics · 2026-10-08 · Added 10 October 2026

A South Korean pilot of a mobile app that streams stroke data from ambulances to EDs found shorter onset-to-door times in 34 app-supported transports versus 129 matched standard transports.

Study type
Other
Design
Pilot quasi-experimental study with matched comparison group
Participants
163 stroke patients (34 intervention, 129 comparison), 80 EMS personnel
Main finding
Intervention transports had shorter median onset-to-door time than matched controls; door-to-needle times were also compared post-match
Caveat
Small, non-randomised pilot in a single Korean city with a truncated abstract reporting limited detail on effect sizes
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital stroke notification is already standard Australian practice by radio and eMR — this adds a structured app workflow, so it informs how digital handover might evolve rather than change practice.

Learning goal

Understand how structured digital prehospital stroke notification could shorten onset-to-door times in Australian practice.

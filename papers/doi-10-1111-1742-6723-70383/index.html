Reported LOC didn't flag post-traumatic amnesia in ED

Post‐Traumatic Amnesia Testing for Suspected Concussion in the Emergency Department: A Retrospective Study
Emergency Medicine Australasia · 2026-11-01 · Added 3 November 2026

An Australian retrospective cohort of 6,259 adults tested with the Abbreviated Westmead PTA Scale after head injury found 91.8% passed, with post-traumatic amnesia associated with initial GCS 13–14 but not with reported loss of consciousness.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Single-centre retrospective cohort
Participants
6,259 adults presenting to ED with head injury
Main finding
91.8% passed the A-WPTAS; PTA was more frequent with GCS 13–14, in male patients, after low falls and after hours, but pass rates were similar with documented LOC (90.6%) and without (92%, p=0.13)
Caveat
ED population only, retrospective electronic records, and no link back to prehospital documentation
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The A-WPTAS is an ED tool rather than something ambulance crews administer, but crew handover observations — GCS and reported LOC — feed straight into this assessment, and LOC alone looks like a weak signaller.

Learning goal

Recognise post-traumatic amnesia in head injury patients even when loss of consciousness is not reported.

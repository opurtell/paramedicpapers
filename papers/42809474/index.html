Nearly half of paediatric doses off-protocol

Deviations From Recommended Drug Dosing in the Prehospital Treatment of Pediatric Patients
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-09-29 · Added 3 October 2026

US quality-registry cohort of 386 prehospital paediatric medication administrations found 46.4% deviated at least 20% from protocolised dosing — mostly underdoses — with ondansetron (aOR 36.9) and the intranasal route (aOR 7.3) the biggest drivers.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective quality-improvement registry cohort
Participants
386 medication administrations in 240 children across nine agencies
Main finding
46.4% of doses deviated ≥20% from recommended (79.9% underdoses); deviation was highest for ondansetron (85.3%) and via the intranasal route (69.7%); weight-estimation error was not associated
Caveat
Single US metropolitan system with uniform protocols, and registry-flagged deviations may capture deliberate titration within permitted ranges
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Weight-based paediatric dosing and intranasal drug delivery are standard Australian practice, so the error patterns translate directly to our trucks.

Learning goal

Update my approach to paediatric medication dosing, particularly intranasal and antiemetic administration, to reduce underdosing.

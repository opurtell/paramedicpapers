Direct geriatric admission depends on geography

Disparities in direct access to acute geriatric care - an observational analysis of geographic variations of ambulance conveyance
BMC Geriatrics · 2026-10-02 · Added 3 October 2026

Swedish register study of 8208 ambulance-transported patients aged ≥65 in Stockholm found only 7% went directly to a geriatric unit instead of an ED, with conveyance rates varying fourfold (4–21%) between hospital service areas and largely explained by distance, ownership and staffed-bed numbers.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional register-based study
Participants
8208 patients aged ≥65, Stockholm 2017–2019
Main finding
603 patients (7%) directly admitted to geriatric units; crude conveyance rates ranged 4–21% across service areas, with supply-side factors (proximity, ownership, bed numbers) explaining most between-area variation
Caveat
Single Swedish region with a geriatric-unit structure quite unlike Australian hospital systems
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Alternative destinations for older people are an active question for Australian services, but direct geriatric-unit admission isn't a pathway most Australian systems offer.

Learning goal

Recognise how geography and system factors shape older patients' access to acute care after ambulance assessment.

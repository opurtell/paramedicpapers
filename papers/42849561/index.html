ITDs in OHCA: early wins, late losses

Outcomes Following Impedance Threshold Device Use During Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest
Resuscitation · 2026-10 · Added 10 October 2026

A 97,439-patient US national EMS sample found impedance threshold device use was associated with better prehospital ROSC (aOR 1.09) but lower survival to discharge (23.9% vs 32.0%, aOR 0.68), with even more extreme trends when combined with active compression-decompression.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective national EMS registry analysis
Participants
97,439 adults with non-traumatic OHCA
Main finding
ITD-only: ROSC 26.7% vs 23.8% (aOR 1.09) but survival to discharge 23.9% vs 32.0% (aOR 0.68), with worse neurological outcomes; ITD+ACD showed more extreme versions of both trends
Caveat
Only 23.3% of patients had disposition outcomes available, and device use was not randomised
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Impedance threshold devices aren't carried by every Australian service, so this informs the device debate more than day-to-day practice — but the questions apply wherever they are used.

Learning goal

Weigh the risks and benefits of impedance threshold devices during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest resuscitation.

Related papers on Cardiac arrest

  • Unwitnessed arrests: mostly women, mostly at home

    Heart & Lung · 10 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    A systematic review of 22 studies (210,028 people) found female sex and arrest at home were the most consistent determinants of unwitnessed OHCA, with weaker, mostly unadjusted evidence for night or sleep arrests, deprivation and psychiatric illness.

  • Bystander CPR up but outcomes flat in the very old

    Resuscitation Plus · 9 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 322,943 bystander-witnessed OHCA patients aged ≥65 in the All-Japan Utstein Registry (2005–22) found bystander CPR rose over time but stalled near 50%, and 1-month favourable neurological outcome stayed around 1% in those aged ≥90 despite CPR rates near 80%.

  • CPR injuries common after long arrests

    Resuscitation Plus · 9 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre retrospective cohort of 136 OHCA patients found CPR-related injuries in 43% (16.9% clinically significant), with CPR duration over 20 minutes — not ECPR or mechanical CPR — independently associated with injury (OR 2.45).

  • Bystander AEDs work — when someone actually grabs one

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Korean multicentre OHCA registry analysis estimating bystander AED effectiveness against a no-defibrillation reference — AED application was associated with outcomes comparable to or better than EMS shocks, but was applied in only a fraction of eligible cases, especially at home.

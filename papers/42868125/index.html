First responders at suicide scenes: training gaps

First responders' experiences and training needs at the scene of a suspected suicide in Ireland: A mixed methods study
PloS One · 2026-10-13 · Added 15 October 2026

Mixed-methods study of 1,587 Irish first responders found positive attitudes towards suicide prevention but lower suicide literacy, postvention knowledge and preparedness, with prior training only weakly improving scores and focus groups describing a heavy emotional toll.

Study type
Other
Design
Mixed-methods national survey plus focus groups
Participants
1,587 survey respondents; 10 focus groups
Main finding
Lower scores for suicide literacy, postvention knowledge, organisational support and preparedness; prior suicide-related training associated with higher scores but small effect sizes
Caveat
Self-selected survey respondents from a single country's responder workforce
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The Irish responder mix and training structures differ, but the occupational experience of attending suspected suicides is directly relatable for Australian crews.

Learning goal

Strengthen my preparedness and postvention knowledge when responding to scenes of suspected suicide.

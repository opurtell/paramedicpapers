What the evidence says about ambulance-ED handover

The Impact of Coordination Between Ambulance Services and Emergency Departments on Healthcare Quality
Natural Resources for Human Health · 2026-10-05 · Added 17 November 2026

A narrative review of ambulance-ED coordination evidence found prehospital notification consistently associated with faster diagnosis and reperfusion in stroke and myocardial infarction, structured handover tools with better information transfer, and ambulance diversion with little crowding relief and links to higher mortality in time-sensitive conditions.

Main finding
Prehospital notification associated with faster diagnosis and reperfusion in stroke and myocardial infarction; structured handover tools improved completeness and efficiency; prolonged offload associated with harm and lost ambulance capacity; diversion offered little relief and was linked to higher mortality in time-sensitive conditions
Caveat
Narrative review by a single author in a low-profile journal - no systematic search protocol or pooled effect estimates
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Ramping, handover and prenotification are everyday pressure points for Australian crews, but this is a single-author narrative review rather than a systematic one, so treat it as a map of the evidence.

Learning goal

Understand how prehospital notification and structured handover shape patient outcomes during ambulance to emergency department transfers.

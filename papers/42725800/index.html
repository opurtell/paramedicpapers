CPR quality maintained across paramedic-to-ED handover

Cardiopulmonary resuscitation quality during the transition in care from paramedic services to emergency department teams
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 15 September 2026

Observational study of 35 OHCA transfers tracking CPR quality across the paramedic-to-ED handover — chest compression fraction (89–92%) and rate stayed guideline-compliant in every interval with no deterioration at transition, though compression depth was significantly deeper during paramedic intervals.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective observational study of defibrillator CPR metrics
Participants
35 adults with non-traumatic OHCA transported to a tertiary academic ED
Main finding
Chest compression fraction (89–92%) and rate (109.9–117.5/min) were guideline compliant in all four intervals with no significant differences; compression depth was deeper in ED intervals (significantly higher than paramedic 5.8–5.9 cm).
Caveat
Small sample from a single tertiary ED; retrospective design.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). transferability — direct evidence on paramedic-delivered CPR at handover, mapping to CMG 4 and everyday AP practice

Learning goal

Update my approach to maintaining compression quality during handover of cardiac arrest care at the emergency department.

  • DNAR orders missed in OHCA dispatches

    Resuscitation · 19 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    One-year regional study of 1,695 lights-and-sirens dispatches for suspected OHCA in Southern Denmark — 15.7% involved a patient with a physician-issued hospital DNAR order that dispatchers and crews couldn't access, one-day mortality was 90.6% in that group, and the authors call for DNAR orders to be visible to emergency dispatch and prehospital providers.

  • CPR-induced consciousness rising in OHCA

    Resuscitation · 18 Sep 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Ambulance Victoria registry study of 43,402 adult OHCA cases (2008–2024) identifying CPR-induced consciousness — 1,438 patients (3.3%), with annual incidence rising (IRR 1.083, 95% CI 1.07–1.10) across all arrest rhythms, and CPRIC associated with improved survival to hospital discharge.

  • Best height for standing compressions

    Resuscitation Plus · 17 Sep 2026 · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    Simulation study with 30 participants compressing at seven working heights found knee-height bed or stretcher position gave the best compressions (52.7 mm mean depth, 80.6% correct depth), with positions 15–20 cm above knee height significantly worse.

  • Mild hypercapnia during ECPR in pigs

    Resuscitation Plus · 16 Sep 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    Porcine refractory-VF arrest model (n=12) testing mild hypercapnia (PaCO2 50-55 mmHg) during ECPR — the hypercapnia group showed higher cerebral glucose (p=0.01) and lower caspase-8 expression, but no significant difference in brain tissue oxygen tension (49.9 vs 36.4 mmHg, p=0.13), intracranial pressure, EEG activity or serum brain-injury biomarkers.

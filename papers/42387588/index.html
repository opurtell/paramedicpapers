Managing ED care during civil unrest

Emergency department medical care during civil unrest: a narrative review
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 27 July 2026

Emergency clinicians should be familiar with the distinct injury profiles associated with crowd-control weapons and be prepared to manage them within the emergency care setting.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Narrative review of crowd-control weapon injuries and ED management
Participants
Not reported
Main finding
Reviews agent-specific injuries: conducted energy devices (low cardiac risk, no routine monitoring for alert asymptomatic patients), riot control agents (self-limiting, decontamination-focused care), kinetic impact projectiles (highest permanent disability burden, especially ocular), plus acoustic, water cannon and incendiary injuries; emphasises surge planning and mechanism-guided assessment.
Caveat
Narrative review without systematic methodology.
Bottom line
Context only

Learning goal

Understand how mechanism-guided assessment of crowd-control weapon injuries shapes my prehospital care and surge readiness.

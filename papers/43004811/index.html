Sydney COAST registry: trauma shock first year

Prospective Observational Study Assessing Patient Characteristics, Management and Early Outcomes of Major Trauma Patients With Prehospital Shock. A preliminary report of the Sydney COAST study
Emergency Medicine Australasia · 2026-11-09 · Added 11 November 2026

An Australian prospective multicentre registry enrolled 257 adults with prehospital trauma shock over 12 months in Sydney, finding hypotension drove 90% of shock definitions, 53% received prehospital blood, and mortality was 4.7% at 4 hours and 21% at 30 days.

Study type
Registry
Design
Prospective multicentre registry, first 12 months (prehospital medical teams)
Participants
257 adults with prehospital trauma shock
Main finding
Hypotension was the shock criterion in 90%; 53% received prehospital blood, 31% needed interventional haemorrhage control, 74% in-hospital transfusion; median ISS 27; mortality 4.7% at 4 hours, 9% at 24 hours, 21% at 30 days
Caveat
Preliminary descriptive report — no outcome comparisons, and not all shocked patients had haemorrhage
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian data, but the prehospital blood that half these patients got is limited to a few Australian services — the shock epidemiology travels nationally, the blood component doesn't yet

Learning goal

Strengthen my recognition of occult shock in major trauma patients who are not yet hypotensive.

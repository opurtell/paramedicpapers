Self-efficacy predicts bystander willingness for CPR

Factors influencing willingness to perform cardiopulmonary resuscitation and use an automated external defibrillator: a convergent parallel mixed-methods study
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-08-22 · Added 19 November 2026

A Malaysian mixed-methods study of 449 community members trained in CPR found self-efficacy the strongest predictor of willingness to perform CPR and use an AED, with attitude and perceived norms contributing far less.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Convergent parallel mixed-methods study (SEM plus thematic interviews)
Participants
449 survey participants and 10 stakeholder interviews
Main finding
Self-efficacy was the strongest predictor of intention (β 0.515, p<0.001, large effect), attitude smaller (β 0.206) and perceived norms negligible (β 0.094); intention mediated the path to behavioural willingness.
Caveat
Cross-sectional design measuring intention, not actual bystander action at real arrests.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The psychological levers behind bystander action are universal, though community norms and training contexts differ from Australia's — useful for anyone designing CPR awareness programs.

Learning goal

Understand how community self-efficacy shapes bystander CPR willingness so I can strengthen local responder education.

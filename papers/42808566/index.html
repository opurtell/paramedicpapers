Neonatal hyperammonaemia retrievals need early transfer

Hyperammonaemia in Neonatal Transport: A Retrospective Statewide Review of Neonates Retrieved With Elevated Ammonia
Journal of Paediatrics and Child Health · 2026-09-29 · Added 30 September 2026

A Victorian statewide review of 21 retrieved neonates with elevated ammonia found 76% had a confirmed inborn error of metabolism, over half needed haemofiltration, and 81% survived to discharge, supporting early retrieval to a quaternary centre.

Study type
Case series
Design
Retrospective chart review
Participants
21 neonates retrieved by the PIPER service (2016–2025)
Main finding
16 of 21 had a confirmed inborn error of metabolism, 12 required CVVHF, and median decision-to-dispatch time was 20 minutes with 81% survival to discharge
Caveat
Very small single-state sample, retrospective design, tertiary-centre case mix
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). An Australian retrieval service, but a 21-patient series of a rare metabolic condition — background for retrieval crews rather than something road paramedics will act on.

Learning goal

Recognise hyperammonaemia as a time-critical cause of neonatal deterioration requiring urgent specialist retrieval.

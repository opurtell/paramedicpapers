Five years of automated head-up CPR outcomes

Five-Year Experience with Automated Head Up Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation for Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-17 · Added 19 November 2026

A five-year US registry of 3,709 OHCAs treated with automated head-up CPR (head/thorax positioning plus active compression–decompression and an impedance threshold device) reported 10.5% survival to discharge with favourable neurology, 91% of survivors at CPC 1.

Study type
Registry
Design
Prospective observational multicentre registry study
Participants
3,709 adults with non-traumatic OHCA across 10 US EMS agencies serving 1.5 million people
Main finding
ROSC 29.5%, survival to discharge 13.1%, favourable neurological survival 10.5%; shockable rhythm 33.9% favourable survival vs 2.0% asystole; 91% of the 391 survivors had CPC 1.
Caveat
No concurrent control group — an observational registry cannot isolate the effect of head-up CPR from concurrent care changes.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Head-up CPR devices and active compression–decompression aren't standard Australian ambulance equipment — a large real-world dataset from a paramedic-model system, but adoption here would need equipment and guideline change.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and limitations of automated head-up CPR in my management of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

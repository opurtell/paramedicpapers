Co-dispatch modelled to cut OHCA response times

Modelled Response Times for Police and Fire Brigade Co-Dispatch to Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Geospatial Simulation Study in Vorarlberg, Austria
Journal of Clinical Medicine · 2026-10-22 · Added 15 November 2026

An Austrian geospatial simulation of 500 modelled OHCA locations found police and volunteer fire brigade co-dispatch cut modelled median first-unit response time from 9.81 to 5.41 minutes and lifted 10-minute coverage from 51.1% to 94.6%.

Study type
Modelling
Design
Geospatial routing simulation
Participants
500 modelled OHCA locations served by 18 EMS, 28 police and 121 volunteer fire stations
Main finding
Median first-unit response 9.81 min with EMS alone vs 5.45 min with police added, 7.36 min with fire added, 5.41 min with triple dispatch (paired median gain 4.34 min); locations reached within 10 min rose from 51.1% to 94.6%
Caveat
Purely modelled — fixed stations, assumed turnout times, continuous availability assumed, and the authors say operational validation is required
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A modelled European system with volunteer fire stations, but first-responder co-response to cardiac arrest is a live resourcing question for Australian services too.

Learning goal

Understand how co-dispatching first responders could reduce time to first unit in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

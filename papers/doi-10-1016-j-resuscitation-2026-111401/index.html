Choking arrests: ROSC common, survival still poor

Out-of-hospital cardiac arrests due to foreign body airway obstruction: A CanROC registry, retrospective cohort study
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 20 November 2026

A Canadian CanROC registry cohort of 105,178 EMS-treated out-of-hospital cardiac arrests (2005–2023) found 1,106 (1.1%) were obviously foreign-body-airway-obstruction-related: prehospital ROSC was far more frequent than in presumed cardiac arrests (62.7% vs 37.8%) but survival to discharge was similar (10.6% vs 9.0%).

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective registry cohort (CanROC, 2005–2023)
Participants
105,178 EMS-treated OHCAs, of which 1,106 obviously FBAO-related
Main finding
FBAO-related arrests achieved prehospital ROSC more often than presumed cardiac arrests (62.7% vs 37.8%, OR 2.77) but survival to discharge did not differ (10.6% vs 9.0%, p=0.094); within FBAO arrests, survival was 18.1% with prehospital ROSC and 0.8% without, and was associated with younger age (aOR 0.76 per decade), shockable rhythm (aOR 4.76) and shorter EMS response time (aOR 0.81 per minute).
Caveat
Exploratory retrospective design, and 'obviously FBAO-related' classification depends on registry coding rather than verified cause of arrest.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). A Canadian paramedic-model registry answering a question Australian crews face on every choking arrest. Bystander presence and shorter EMS response times drove outcomes — both central to how Australian systems are built, and the same arrest is managed the same way here.

Learning goal

Strengthen my recognition of airway obstruction as the reversible cause in cardiac arrest and prioritise early airway clearance.

Sepsis suspicion at dispatch, earlier antibiotics

Dispatch suspicion of sepsis and time from emergency department arrival to antibiotic administration: a Norwegian registry study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-13 · Added 14 November 2026

A Norwegian registry study of 527 sepsis admissions found dispatch suspicion of sepsis was associated with a 31-minute shorter adjusted time from ED arrival to antibiotic administration.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective registry-based cohort linking prehospital and hospital records
Participants
527 sepsis admissions (371 with emergency dispatch contact; 31 classified as suspected sepsis at dispatch)
Main finding
Dispatch suspicion of sepsis was associated with a 31-minute shorter adjusted time to antibiotics (95% CI −59.9 to −1.8 min)
Caveat
Only 31 patients carried the sepsis dispatch flag, so the estimate rests on a small subgroup
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Dispatch triage criteria and sepsis recognition are core ambulance functions in Australia, and the Nordic dispatch model is close to ours.

Learning goal

Recognise how early prehospital suspicion of sepsis can accelerate antibiotic administration for deteriorating patients.

    Single-centre Austrian cohort of 134 ED sepsis admissions found EMS documentation of sepsis suspicion was linked to antibiotics within 1 hour in 63.3% vs 19.2% of patients (aOR 5.77) and a median time-to-antibiotics of 32 vs 124.5 minutes.

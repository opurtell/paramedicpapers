Paediatric weight often undocumented before dosing

Documentation of Weight in Pediatric 9-1-1 Responses with Administration of Weight-Based Medications
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 12 September 2026

Analysis of 57,378 US paediatric 9-1-1 responses where weight-based medications were given found 73.1% had a documented weight (or length-based estimate), with documentation less likely for adolescents (OR 0.87), Black patients (OR 0.72) and lower-acuity cases (OR 0.59), and more likely with advanced life support crews (OR 2.02).

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Retrospective national EMS database analysis
Participants
57,378 US paediatric 9-1-1 responses with weight-based medications
Main finding
73.1% (41,963) had documented weight or length-based estimate; less likely in adolescents (OR 0.87), Black patients (OR 0.72), lower acuity (OR 0.59); more likely with ALS crews (OR 2.02)
Caveat
US national dataset; documentation may not reflect actual weight assessment practice
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Weight-based paediatric dosing is core AP practice under the ACTAS CMGs, so the document-the-weight habit transfers — but this is a US documentation/QI study, so the numbers are local to their system.

Learning goal

Strengthen my routine of obtaining and documenting a weight or length-based estimate before paediatric weight-based medication dosing.

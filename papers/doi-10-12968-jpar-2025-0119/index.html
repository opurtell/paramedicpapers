UK bystander CPR up, AED use flat

Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: comparing interventions across the UK
Journal of Paramedic Practice · 2026-11-02 · Added 11 November 2026

A UK literature review of eight articles found bystander CPR rates increasing in England and Scotland while AED use in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest has shown little improvement, with demographics and socioeconomic factors shaping prehospital intervention rates.

  • Longer collapse-to-contact times linked with ROSC

    Frontiers in Medicine · 12 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Chinese single-centre retrospective cohort of 152 OHCA cases found longer collapse-to-first-medical-contact intervals were independently associated with prehospital ROSC — a paradox the authors attribute to case-mix differences rather than a real benefit of slower response.

  • Big community arrest programme didn't change survival

    The New England Journal of Medicine · 10 Nov 2026 · RCT · Supports current practice

    A cluster-randomised trial across 55 North Carolina county clusters (22,459 patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest) found a multifaceted community and EMS programme did not improve survival with good neurological outcome (9.0% vs 9.4%).

  • Prehospital ECPR linked to doubled arrest survival

    The New England Journal of Medicine · 10 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A nationwide Dutch study compared HEMS with prehospital ECPR against conventional HEMS in adults 50 or younger with refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and found 40% survival to discharge with ECPR versus 20% without, with better neurological outcomes at 6 months.

  • PEEP during CPR linked with more ROSC

    The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · 9 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre Belgian cohort of intubated OHCA patients managed by a physician-staffed team found mechanical ventilation with PEEP during CPR was associated with higher odds of ROSC (adjusted OR 1.58) than manual ventilation without PEEP.

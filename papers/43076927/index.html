Where transfer delay hits paediatric testicular torsion

Where time is lost in pediatric testicular torsion: counterfactual estimates of reducing transfer-associated prehospital delay
World Journal of Urology · 2026-11-24 · Added 26 November 2026

Chinese referral-centre modelling of 311 boys with surgically confirmed testicular torsion estimated that eliminating transfer-associated excess prehospital delay would cut nonviability risk from 42.1% to 34.7% and orchiectomy from 33.4% to 27.3%.

Study type
Modelling
Design
Retrospective cohort with parametric g-computation counterfactual modelling
Participants
311 boys, 175 (56.3%) transferred between facilities
Main finding
Median prehospital time 28.5 h in transferred boys vs 9.5 h direct (adjusted time ratio 1.75, 95% CI 1.33–2.31); under a 100% reduction scenario nonviability fell 42.1%→34.7% and orchiectomy 33.4%→27.3%
Caveat
Single-centre retrospective data feeding modelled scenarios, not measured outcomes under an actual intervention
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Referral coordination and transfer delay are ambulance-system questions everywhere, though these counterfactual estimates come from a single Chinese paediatric centre.

Learning goal

Recognise testicular torsion as a time-critical paediatric emergency and weigh how transfer decisions affect outcomes.

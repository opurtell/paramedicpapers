Interprofessional simulation shapes student identity

‘Interprofessional simulation in paramedicine education: Student perceptions of role identity, communication and collaboration’
Paramedicine · 2026-10-19 · Added 20 October 2026

An Australian exploratory qualitative study of undergraduate paramedic, nursing and medical students found an interprofessional simulation event challenged assumptions about professional hierarchy and role identity, prompting students to reconsider advocacy, handover and assertive communication.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Exploratory descriptive qualitative study (single focus group, reflexive thematic analysis)
Participants
One focus group of undergraduate paramedic students
Main finding
Two themes emerged — Negotiating Professional Authority and Preparing for Collaborative Practice — with students reporting the simulation reshaped how they enacted the paramedic role, particularly around advocacy, handover and assertive communication
Caveat
Single focus group with a small sample at one institution — perspectives may not generalise
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Interprofessional simulation is well established in Australian paramedic programs, so this adds texture to what students take from it rather than pointing to anything new for services.

Learning goal

Understand how interprofessional learning shapes my role identity, advocacy and assertiveness during handover and collaborative practice.

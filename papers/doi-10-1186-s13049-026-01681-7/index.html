Few OHCA patients eligible for eCPR in Germany

Modelling eCPR eligibility after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a 10-year retrospective temporospatial analysis from a mixed urban-rural region in Germany
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 10 September 2026

Ten-year German registry study modelling eCPR eligibility after OHCA in a mixed urban-rural region — of 1,696 analysed arrests, only 348 (20.5%) met local eligibility criteria at ambulance arrival and just 149 (8.8%) met the stricter modified ELSO definition, with the study also testing whether hypothetical intra-arrest transport strategies could meet a 60-minute low-flow target.

Study type
Modelling
Design
Retrospective 10-year temporospatial registry cohort with hypothetical eCPR transport modelling
Participants
1,696 registry-documented OHCA cases (of 2,218) in Göttingen, Germany
Main finding
20.5% (348) met local eCPR criteria and 8.8% (149) modified ELSO criteria at EMS arrival; modelled on-scene eCPR gave median collapse-to-eCPR of 37 min (94.8% within 60 min) vs 75 min for intra-arrest transport (<1% within 60 min)
Caveat
Retrospective with hypothetical modelled scenarios; German mixed urban-rural system limits transferability
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Directly maps to CMG 4/4A cardiac arrest care and the intra-arrest transport debate, but eCPR isn't an ACTAS capability — useful benchmark for OHCA system design rather than practice change

Learning goal

Understand how geography and transport times shape eligibility for extracorporeal CPR in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

Out-of-hospital RSI practice across South African services

Out-of-hospital rapid sequence intubation practices by emergency care practitioners in selected provinces of South Africa: a retrospective cross-sectional review
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-09 · Added 12 November 2026

A South African multicentre record review of 354 out-of-hospital RSIs performed by emergency care practitioners across three provinces found trauma accounted for 67% of cases, ketamine was documented in 75% and rocuronium in 52%, with pre-oxygenation in 99% and capnography in 84% of cases.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Multicentre retrospective cross-sectional record review
Participants
354 RSI cases (from 535,479 patient care records screened)
Main finding
Trauma caused 238/354 (67.2%) of RSIs; ketamine documented in 74.9%, rocuronium in 52.3%, pre-oxygenation in 99.4%, end-tidal CO2 monitoring in 83.6% and post-intubation sedation/analgesia in 72%
Caveat
Retrospective documentation review — missing records may undercount care and adverse events
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Drug-assisted intubation is an intensive care paramedic skill in most Australian services, and rocuronium is not carried by all of them — but the drug choices, monitoring standards and adverse-event questions are the same debates.

Learning goal

Strengthen my documentation of post-intubation sedation, analgesia and capnography to support safer out-of-hospital RSI.

  • Transport ventilation during CPR feasible in pilot

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 28 Oct 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    A Thai exploratory pilot compared transport mechanical ventilation (HAMILTON-T1, volume-controlled) with bag-valve ventilation during CPR in adult OHCA and found no significant differences in PCO2, pH, pO2, sustained ROSC or 28-day survival, with mechanical ventilation operationally feasible.

  • Real-time feedback tidies BVM ventilation in arrest

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Paris before-and-after study of 163 arrests found real-time feedback during bag-valve-mask ventilation improved insufflated-volume adherence (68% vs 42% of breaths in the 400–600 mL range) and cut leak volume by 12%, although expired tidal volumes were unchanged.

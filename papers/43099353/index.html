Coagulopathy common after prehospital transfusion in long transports

Acute traumatic coagulopathy and hypofibrinogenemia in trauma patients who receive prehospital transfusion: a retrospective cohort study
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-09-04 · Added 28 November 2026

A Canadian retrospective cohort of 70 trauma patients transfused by Ornge air ambulance found 16% with INR > 1.5 and 20% with fibrinogen < 1.5 g/L at trauma centre arrival, with coagulopathy associated with higher mortality and massive haemorrhage protocol activation.

Retrospective cohort (air ambulance blood-on-board program, pilot for larger studies)
Participants

Main finding
16% had INR > 1.5 and 20% fibrinogen < 1.5 g/L on arrival; INR > 1.5 was associated with increased mortality (p = 0.04)
Caveat
Small retrospective pilot (70 patients) with mostly univariable analyses, and median out-of-hospital time of 131 minutes far exceeds typical Australian transport intervals.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is carried by a handful of Australian services and transport intervals like these (median 131 minutes) are rare here, so this informs the debate more than current practice.

Learning goal

Recognise which major trauma patients are most at risk of coagulopathy before hospital arrival in Australian settings.

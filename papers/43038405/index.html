Clinician smartphones linked to tripled interpreter use

A Lighter Approach to Language Access: Virtual Interpreter Utilization in Emergency Medical Services (EMS)
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-16 · Added 19 November 2026

A US pre-post cohort found issuing EMS clinicians lightweight smartphones (in place of heavy laptops) tripled virtual interpreter use, from 0.19 to 0.57 requests per 100 encounters.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective pre-post observational cohort
Participants
430 interpreter requests across pre- and post-implementation periods
Main finding
Requests rose from 104 (0.19 per 100 encounters) to 326 (0.57 per 100) after agency-issued smartphones (RR 3.04, 95% CI 2.44–3.79); audio requests tripled; most requested languages were Spanish (36%), Somali (34%) and Arabic (10%).
Caveat
Pre-post design — other workflow changes could contribute, and there are no patient outcome data.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Language barriers are a real problem on Australian crews, and the finding suggests device ergonomics and workflow, not clinician willingness, are the bottleneck.

Learning goal

Understand how portable technology shapes my use of interpreter services for patients with limited English proficiency.

VR adds little to CPR simulation training

Effectiveness of immersive virtual reality in undergraduate nursing students for cardiopulmonary resuscitation training: A randomized controlled trial
Nurse Education Today · Added 10 September 2026

RCT of 246 undergraduate nursing students testing immersive VR plus manikin simulation against manikin simulation alone for CPR group training — no significant difference in self-confidence or CPR group performance after cluster adjustment, though students rated VR higher on satisfaction and usability, and 33.6% reported dizziness and 19% headaches from headsets.

Study type
RCT
Design
Cluster-adjusted randomised controlled trial
Participants
246 undergraduate nursing students
Main finding
No significant difference in self-confidence (adjusted effect 0.02, p=0.974) or CPR group performance (adjusted effect 0.56, p=0.153) with immersive VR plus manikin vs manikin alone; 33.6% reported dizziness and 19% headaches
Caveat
Nursing students in group CPR training, not practising paramedics; 19% adverse symptoms
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Nursing-student education data, but directly relevant to ambulance service CPR training programs weighing up VR — the headline is hands-on sim alone did just as well

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and harms of immersive virtual reality when choosing resuscitation training methods for my professional development.

Drowning and trauma lead Swedish mountain deaths

Fatalities in Swedish mountains
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-18 · Added 19 November 2026

A Swedish study of mountain rescue fatalities (2018–2023) found drowning and traumatic injuries the leading causes of death among 26 autopsied cases, with half the victims alone when the fatal event occurred.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Descriptive retrospective study (search-and-rescue registry plus autopsy reports)
Participants
46 mountain fatalities; autopsy reports for 26
Main finding
Drowning (n=7) and traumatic injuries (n=7) were the leading causes of death, then suffocation (4), hypothermia (4), disease (3) and intoxication (1); 25 of 26 were male and 13 died alone.
Caveat
Autopsy reports were available for only 26 of 46 fatalities, so causes may be incomplete.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Alpine and wilderness rescue is a live issue for Australian services; the causes of death and the lone-victim pattern transfer even if the terrain and snowmobile context differ.

Learning goal

Recognise the patterns of preventable death among people recreating alone in remote Australian wilderness settings.

Video calls showed no clear resource savings

Prehospital Resource Utilization After Video-Assisted Versus Telephone-Only Emergency Calls: A Post Hoc Analysis of the CAM-VISION Cluster Randomized Clinical Trial
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-17 · Added 19 November 2026

A post hoc analysis of 18,745 calls from the Danish CAM-VISION cluster-randomised trial found video-assisted emergency calls produced no statistically significant reduction in prehospital resource use, though point estimates consistently favoured video.

Study type
Other
Design
Post hoc analysis of a cluster-randomised trial
Participants
18,745 emergency calls (8,124 video, 10,621 telephone-only)
Main finding
Initial dispatch 69.4% vs 73.9% (risk difference −4.5 pp, 95% CI −10.0 to 1.0); cumulative unit time −1.5 min (95% CI −6.2 to 3.1); no outcome reached statistical significance and later dispatch didn't offset the difference.
Caveat
Post hoc analysis not powered for resource outcomes, and the confidence intervals don't exclude meaningful differences.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Video triage on emergency calls is being trialled in Australian dispatch centres, and this is the first complete-pathway look at whether it actually saves resources.

Learning goal

Understand how video-assisted emergency calls shape dispatch decisions and prehospital resource use across systems.

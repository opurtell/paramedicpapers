In-flight events uncommon in international air repatriation

International Fixed-Wing Air Ambulance Repatriation: A Retrospective Observational Cohort Analysis With Regression Modeling of Fox Flight Air Ambulance Inc. Patient Repatriation Characteristics 2009 to 2024
Air Medical Journal · 2026-08-12 · Added 29 October 2026

A Canadian retrospective cohort of 3,905 international fixed-wing air ambulance repatriations (2009–2024) found in-flight events in 26.7% of transports but major events in only 3.9%, with independent predictors including age, trauma and neoplasm diagnoses, oxygen therapy and mechanical ventilation.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective observational cohort with multivariable regression
Participants
3,905 international fixed-wing repatriations, single Canadian provider, 2009–2024
Main finding
In-flight events in 26.7% of transports, major events 3.9%; independent predictors of events were age, orthopaedic/trauma and neoplasm diagnoses, oxygen therapy, invasive ventilation and transport year; acuity increased over time.
Caveat
Single private provider's charts; causality between team composition and events cannot be inferred from retrospective data.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). International repatriation is a small corner of Australian aeromedical practice, mostly fixed-wing contract work, but the event rates and crew-mix findings speak to the same transport medicine.

Learning goal

Understand which patient factors increase the risk of deterioration during long-haul aeromedical repatriation.

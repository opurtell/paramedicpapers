Bystander-attached AEDs linked with best OHCA survival

Survival after out of hospital cardiac arrest in relation to AED usage in a multi response system including AED equipped drones
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 19 November 2026

A Swedish registry cohort of 9,413 OHCAs in Västra Götaland found bystander-attached AEDs were associated with the highest 30-day survival (28% vs 9.0% when EMS attached first, OR 2.25), pointing to earlier defibrillation as the key lever.

Study type
Registry
Design
Registry-based cohort study (EMS, dispatch and registry linkage)
Participants
9,413 OHCAs, 2015–2024
Main finding
EMS attached the first AED in 72% of cases; 30-day survival was 28% with bystander attachment vs 9.0% EMS, 8.7% on-duty first responders and 8.6% community first responders; bystander attachment associated with higher 30-day survival (OR 2.25, 95% CI 1.77–2.85), rising to 62% in witnessed shockable arrests.
Caveat
Observational — bystander attachment likely flags arrests recognised and treated earlier, so attribution to the responder alone isn't possible.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Bystander defibrillation is a universal weak point in the OHCA chain; the message that earlier community access beats crew arrival applies to every Australian response system.

Learning goal

Understand how community AED access and drone-delivered defibrillation shape early bystander response in cardiac arrest.

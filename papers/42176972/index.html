Naloxone linked to better PEA arrest survival

Naloxone administration associated with improved survival in PEA out-of-hospital cardiac arrests
Resuscitation · Added 6 June 2026

Retrospective cohort (n=40,333) testing whether prehospital naloxone improves outcomes stratified by presenting rhythm, and found naloxone was associated with higher survival to discharge in PEA arrests but not in shockable rhythms or asystole.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective cohort with propensity-score matching
Participants
40,333 OHCA cases; 7,567 (18.8%) received naloxone
Main finding
In PEA arrests, naloxone was associated with higher survival to discharge (OR 1.46, 95% CI 1.11-1.92) but not ROSC (OR 1.09, 95% CI 0.90-1.31); no association in shockable rhythms or asystole
Caveat
Retrospective design; selection bias and resuscitation time bias not fully addressed, causality unproven
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Learning goal

Weigh the role of naloxone in PEA cardiac arrest management alongside my current resuscitation practice.

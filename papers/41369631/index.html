Overshooting BP targets worsens ICH outcomes

Early Intensive Blood Pressure Reduction After Intracerebral Hemorrhage Is Associated With Worse Functional Outcome: The Risk of Overshooting Blood Pressure Goals
Annals of Emergency Medicine · Added 1 May 2026

Retrospective cohort testing whether rapid systolic BP reduction ≤150 mm Hg after spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage improved outcomes — early target achievement and overshooting <120 mm Hg were associated with worse functional outcome.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort at two academic EDs
Participants
420 adults with spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage
Main finding
Achieving systolic BP ≤150 mmHg within 2 hours was associated with worse functional outcome (OR 2.32, 95% CI 1.17–4.57), as was overshooting below 120 mmHg within 6 hours (OR 2.55, 95% CI 1.27–5.13)
Caveat
Retrospective design with possible confounding by haemorrhage severity; in-hospital ED population
Bottom line
Context only

Learning goal

Weigh the risks of over-lowering blood pressure against target achievement when managing spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage.

No risky oxygen build-up at defibrillation site in CPR

Ambient oxygen enrichment during cardiopulmonary resuscitation: an overestimated risk? - Insights from a manikin study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 12 August 2026

Mannequin study testing whether removing oxygen-delivery devices reduces ambient oxygen concentration at defibrillation sites — it found no clinically relevant oxygen accumulation, and removing devices made no difference to oxygen levels.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Controlled mannequin-based simulation study
Participants
20 resuscitation scenarios; 6,555 oxygen measurements
Main finding
Median ambient oxygen 21.6% (IQR 21.3–22.1%); removing oxygen delivery devices >1 m did not lower oxygen concentrations; intubation and OxyDemand valve gave slightly lower fractions
Caveat
Mannequin simulation with limited scenarios; may not reflect real arrests with leaks or high-flow oxygen
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Directly challenges current ERC guideline (followed by Australian services) requiring oxygen device removal before defibrillation; could simplify CPR workflow if adopted.

Learning goal

Weigh the current evidence on disconnecting oxygen delivery during defibrillation against fire risk in resuscitation.

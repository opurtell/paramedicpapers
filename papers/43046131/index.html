Prehospital TXA lowers mortality in bleeding trauma

Prehospital tranexamic acid in trauma and traumatic brain injury: a systematic review with meta-analysis of randomized comparative evidence
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-08-22 · Added 19 November 2026

A systematic review of randomised evidence, authored in Saudi Arabia, pooled three trials of prehospital TXA in trauma and found lower 28–30-day mortality (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68–0.95) without a significant rise in thromboembolic events or seizures.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review with meta-analysis restricted to randomised trials (Cochrane RoB2)
Participants
21 reports; 3 randomised trials pooled for mortality (PATCH-Trauma, STAAMP, ROC-TXA)
Main finding
Lower 28–30-day mortality with prehospital TXA (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68–0.95); TBI mortality (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.64–1.20) and in-hospital mortality came from single trials; no statistically significant increase in thromboembolic events or seizures.
Caveat
The mortality pool rests on three trials, and the TBI and in-hospital estimates are single-study; thromboembolic point estimates need watching.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). TXA is part of standard Australian prehospital trauma care, so this pooled RCT evidence speaks directly to practice rather than a foreign model.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and thromboembolic risks of prehospital tranexamic acid for bleeding trauma and head injury in my practice.

Most prehospital tourniquets unjustified, few harmed

Prehospital Tourniquet Use in Rhode Island: High Rates of Unjustified Application with Low Complication Rates
Rhode Island Medical Journal · 2026-12-01 · Added 22 November 2026

US retrospective chart review of 232 prehospital tourniquet applications at a Rhode Island Level 1 trauma centre found 69.4% were unjustified, yet unjustified placement was not associated with higher mortality or complications.

Main finding
69.4% of tourniquets were unjustified; justified cases were more severely injured and more often needed surgery or transfusion, but unjustified placement showed no difference in mortality, compartment syndrome or nerve palsy
Caveat
Single US trauma centre, retrospective chart review, and 'justified' criteria were researcher-defined
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Tourniquets are standard kit on Australian ambulances, but this is a single-centre US chart review, so it informs the low-threshold debate rather than settling it.

Learning goal

Weigh the risks and benefits of prehospital tourniquet application when injury severity is uncertain.

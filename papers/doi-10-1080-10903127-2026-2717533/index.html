Capnometry with prehospital intubation cuts mortality

The use of Capnometry in Prehospital Intubation and Association with 30-Day Mortality: A Nationwide Registry Study
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 20 August 2026

Nationwide registry study examining capnometry use during prehospital intubation and its association with 30-day mortality.

Study type
Registry
Design
Nationwide retrospective registry cohort
Participants
8,625 patients (8,720 intubations) in Denmark 2016–2021
Main finding
Capnometry used in 80.3% of intubations, rising from 75.4% to 82.6%; 30-day mortality 62.6%; capnometry use independently associated with lower mortality (more frequent in survivors, 87% vs 76%)
Caveat
Observational registry design — association may reflect confounding by indication or case mix
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Directly relevant to Australian prehospital intubation practice; could inform guideline decisions on capnography/capnometry use.

Learning goal

Strengthen my practice of waveform capnometry during prehospital intubation to reduce mortality risk in critically ill patients.

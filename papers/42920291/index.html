Paramedics under-document neuro change over time

Self-Reported Completeness of Neurological Documentation in Prehospital Emergency Care: Associations With Neurological Training and Professional Experience
Medical Science Monitor · 2026-10-24 · Added 25 October 2026

A survey of 348 Polish paramedics found moderate self-reported neurological documentation completeness (mean 24.57 of a possible 35), worst for documenting temporal neurological change (only 9.2% consistent), with higher scores in those with neurological training or more experience.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional structured questionnaire, southeastern Poland (June 2025–March 2026)
Participants
348 paramedics
Main finding
Mean self-reported documentation completeness 24.57/35; level of consciousness was documented often (81.0%) but only 9.2% consistently documented temporal neurological changes; training was associated with higher scores (26.20 vs 23.24, P<0.001)
Caveat
Self-reported documentation with no chart audit to verify, single region
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Self-reported rather than audited, and from a Polish service, but under-documenting how neuro findings change over time is a known weak spot in prehospital records here too, and it directly affects stroke and TBI handover.

Learning goal

Refine my documentation of temporal neurological change to better capture patient deterioration over time.

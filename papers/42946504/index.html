Central venous access during arrest feasible in simulation

Feasibility of Prehospital Central Venous Cannulation During Cardiac Arrest in an Ambulance
Air Medical Journal · 2026-09-11 · Added 29 October 2026

A US simulation study in a standard ambulance found central venous cannulation technically feasible during simulated refractory shockable cardiac arrest, completed in all runs with left femoral access identified as the most practical site and ACLS interventions maintained throughout.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Simulation-based feasibility study
Participants
Repeated simulations with a multidisciplinary team (EMS clinicians plus a physician) in a standard ambulance
Main finding
Cannulation successful in all simulations via standard sterile technique; left femoral vein identified as the most practical site; main challenges were confined workspace and fixed stretcher height.
Caveat
Manikin simulation with a small expert team — no patient data, and real-world sterility and time costs are untested.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Central venous access is beyond current Australian paramedic scope — an intensive-care-tier idea at best, and this was a manikin study with a physician on the team.

Learning goal

Weigh whether prehospital central venous access offers practical advantages over intraosseous access during refractory cardiac arrest.

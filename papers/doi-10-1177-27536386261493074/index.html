Nurse-paramedics caught between two professions

The professional identity of dual-qualified nurse-paramedics: A descriptive phenomenological study
Paramedicine · 2026-09-25 · Added 26 September 2026

A phenomenological study of 13 Australian dual-qualified nurse-paramedics found they experienced a confused, fragmented professional identity — a constant 'tug of war' — driven by siloed governance structures and interprofessional rivalry.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative descriptive phenomenological study
Participants
13 dual-qualified nurse-paramedics across Australia
Main finding
Participants reported identity confusion, pressure to conform to one profession, and feeling invisible, disheartened and exhausted under governance structures that enforce strict professional boundaries
Caveat
Small qualitative sample, self-selected participants, no outcome measures
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). An Australian study of paramedics in jurisdictional services, speaking directly to workforce design, registration arrangements and staff wellbeing across the country.

Learning goal

Understand how dual professional identity affects my wellbeing and practice within Australia's regulated paramedic workforce.

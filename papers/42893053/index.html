Cardiac arrest systems not ready in rural Pakistan

Health system readiness for out-of-hospital-cardiac-arrest management in resource limited settings: A mixed-method study from rural and suburban Sindh, Pakistan
Resuscitation Plus · 2026-09-24 · Added 19 October 2026

Mixed-methods assessment of the OHCA chain of survival in rural and suburban Sindh found no functional defibrillator across seven hospitals, gaps in basic resuscitation equipment and ambulance availability, and poor community recognition of cardiac arrest.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Mixed-methods (exit interviews, facility readiness checklist, focus groups and interviews)
Participants
163 patient exit interviews and 7 hospitals
Main finding
Defibrillators present in 2 of 7 hospitals but neither functional, laryngeal mask airways unavailable in all facilities, and satisfaction lowest for communication (26.4%) and financial aspects (31.3%)
Caveat
Small facility sample in a very different low-resource setting.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). A low-resource health system with major infrastructure and access barriers — it describes a chain-of-survival gap, not anything comparable to Australian ambulance practice.

Learning goal

Recognise how equipment and system gaps across the chain of survival shape out-of-hospital cardiac arrest outcomes.

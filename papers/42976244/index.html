Electrical capture uncommon in prehospital transcutaneous pacing

Transcutaneous Pacing in the Prehospital Setting – A Retrospective Cohort Study of Electrical Capture
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-03 · Added 5 November 2026

A US EMS agency reviewed continuous monitor ECGs from 56 patients given prehospital transcutaneous pacing between 2017 and 2024 and found electrical capture in only 32%, often intermittent, with clinician-verbalised pulses coinciding with capture in just 15% of instances.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort with continuous ECG review
Participants
56 patients who received prehospital transcutaneous pacing
Main finding
Electrical capture in 18/56 (32%); among capturers, complete capture occurred only 27.2% of pacing time; median SBP change +22 mmHg with capture vs 0 mmHg without (p=0.031); clinician-verbalised pulse coincided with electrical capture in only 4/26 (15%) instances
Caveat
Single US agency, small sample, and capture judged from monitor recordings rather than patient-centred outcomes
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Transcutaneous pacing is carried by only a handful of Australian services, so this speaks most directly to the services that pace rather than day-to-day practice everywhere.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when prehospital transcutaneous pacing is likely to achieve electrical capture and meaningful perfusion.

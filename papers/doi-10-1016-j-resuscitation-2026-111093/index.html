Critical care attendance driven by distance, not deprivation

Geographic Inequalities in Prehospital Critical Care for Cardiac Arrest: A Spatial Analysis of Deprivation and Service Configuration
Resuscitation · Added 14 May 2026

This spatial analysis examined how deprivation levels and prehospital critical care service configuration affect cardiac arrest outcomes, revealing persistent geographic disparities in access and survival.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Spatial analysis of ambulance service data
Participants
57,601 OHCA patients across 9,639 English neighbourhoods
Main finding
Median neighbourhood prehospital critical care attendance was 25.0%; attendance clustered geographically and was associated with distance to nearest critical care base. Accounting for spatial dependence removed the association with deprivation.
Caveat
English ambulance services only; aggregated neighbourhood-level data rather than individual patient analysis.
Bottom line
Context only

Learning goal

Understand how service configuration and geography shape equitable access to prehospital critical care across Australian communities.

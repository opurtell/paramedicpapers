Whole blood on ambulances: first two years

Low-Titer O + Whole Blood Implementation in the EMS System for Metropolitan Oklahoma City and Tulsa: Significance, Success, & Sharing Lessons Learned
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-10-19 · Added 22 October 2026

The first two years of a prehospital low-titre O-positive whole blood program in Oklahoma (71.6% penetrating trauma) showed significantly improved prehospital vital signs after paramedic transfusion, with improved systolic blood pressure tied to higher survival to hospital arrival.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective descriptive observational study
Participants
Patients transfused low-titre O-positive whole blood by paramedics across two US cities, Dec 2022–Feb 2025
Main finding
Systolic blood pressure, heart rate and shock index improved significantly after transfusion, and improved systolic blood pressure carried 9.7-fold greater odds of survival to hospital arrival
Caveat
Descriptive before-after design with no control group, and a predominantly penetrating trauma mix that differs from Australian patterns
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is limited to a handful of Australian services, so this program report informs the should-we-carry-blood debate more than current practice.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when prehospital whole blood transfusion benefits trauma patients with haemorrhagic shock.

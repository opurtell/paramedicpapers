Post-ROSC PaCO2 doesn't predict paediatric OHCA outcome

Association between the first post-resuscitation partial pressure of arterial carbon dioxide and neurologic outcomes in children with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a multicenter retrospective cohort study
Resuscitation · Added 25 September 2026

Japanese multicentre registry study of 215 paediatric OHCA patients testing whether the first post-ROSC PaCO2 category predicts 30-day outcomes — no clear association with favourable neurologic outcome or survival for either hypocapnia (aOR 0.83) or hypercapnia (aOR 1.46), though estimates were imprecise with wide confidence intervals.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Multicentre retrospective registry cohort with propensity score overlap weighting
Participants
215 children with OHCA (27 hypocapnia, 39 normocapnia, 149 hypercapnia)
Main finding
Favourable neurologic outcome at 30 days occurred in 41 (19.1%) and 30-day survival in 92 (42.8%). No clear association between first post-ROSC PaCO2 category and neurologic outcome (hypocapnia aOR 0.83, 95% CI 0.13–5.14; hypercapnia aOR 1.46, 95% CI 0.25–8.68) or survival.
Caveat
Small sample with very imprecise estimates (wide confidence intervals)
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Post-ROSC ventilation and monitoring are within ACTAS scope (CMG 4A), but this is a small retrospective cohort that can't settle the target — hypothesis-generating only.

Learning goal

Weigh the evidence for targeting ventilation to avoid hypocapnia during post-ROSC care in paediatric patients.

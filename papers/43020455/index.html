EtCO2 trends predict ROSC cycle by cycle

Cycle-to-Cycle Prediction of Return of Spontaneous Circulation Using End-Tidal Carbon Dioxide Dynamics in Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 14 November 2026

A Taiwanese retrospective multicentre study of 1,480 patients with OHCA built a two-layer LSTM model on sequential EtCO2 that predicted ROSC well at event level (AUROC 0.876) but only 0.685 per two-minute cycle.

Study type
Modelling
Design
Multicentre retrospective study with LSTM prediction model, internally validated by 10-fold cross-validation
Participants
1,651 EtCO2 sequences from 1,480 adults with OHCA (583 ROSC events)
Main finding
Event-level AUROC 0.876 (Brier 0.140); cycle-level AUROC 0.685 across 17,029 two-minute prediction points, with systematic overestimation of ROSC probability (Brier 0.285)
Caveat
Retrospective, single-country dataset with internal validation only and poor cycle-level calibration
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). EtCO2 monitoring is routine on Australian cardiac arrests, but this is a retrospective modelling study needing prospective, real-time validation before it guides cycle decisions on the road.

Learning goal

Weigh the value of EtCO2 trend monitoring for anticipating return of spontaneous circulation during resuscitation.

  • Higher admission PaCO2 linked to worse post-arrest survival

    Journal of Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Chinese single-centre retrospective cohort of 617 adults resuscitated from OHCA found admission PaCO2 above 50 mmHg associated with higher one-month mortality in a stepwise gradient (each 10-mmHg increase aOR 1.45, 95% CI 1.24–1.70).

  • Night-time OHCA outcomes linked to bystander availability

    Journal of Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026 · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    A Czech-led scoping review of 18 studies found OHCA and STEMI incidence peaks in the morning with a secondary afternoon/evening peak, and night-time events were linked to lower ROSC and worse one-month and one-year survival, explained by limited bystander availability, less bystander CPR and defibrillation and longer times to hospital intervention rather than time of day itself.

  • NSW data linkage tracks sudden cardiac arrest

    Heart Rhythm · 13 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    An Australian data-linkage study identified 13,345 presumed sudden cardiac arrests aged 1–50 in NSW from 1.7 million linked records — 54% cardiac in aetiology and 83% fatal — and extrapolated roughly 370 cardiac SCA deaths a year among Australians aged 1–35.

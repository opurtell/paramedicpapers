Video laryngoscopes available but rarely first choice

Prehospital Videolaryngoscope Availability and Use in Belgium: A Nationwide Cross-Sectional Survey
Cureus · 2026-10-13 · Added 14 November 2026

A Belgian nationwide survey of mobile intensive care units found 91.4% of responding units had videolaryngoscope access, yet only 6.2% of physician users reached for it first-choice and 81.5% reserved it for failed direct laryngoscopy.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Nationwide cross-sectional survey of all Belgian MICUs (two questionnaires)
Participants
35 of 96 MICU department heads and 115 MICU physicians responded
Main finding
91.4% of responding MICUs reported videolaryngoscope availability; 70.4% of physician respondents used one, but only 6.2% as first-choice device and 81.5% used it after failed direct laryngoscopy
Caveat
36.5% unit response rate and self-reported use patterns
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Belgian crews are physician-staffed, but the airway-device question is one Australian advanced-tier crews face too — the availability-versus-use gap transfers even if the crew model doesn't.

Learning goal

Refine my judgement on when to choose videolaryngoscopy first-line rather than after failed direct laryngoscopy.

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 28 Oct 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

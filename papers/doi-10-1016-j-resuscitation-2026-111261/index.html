Intra-arrest TOE feasible but no survival gain

Transoesophageal echocardiography during cardiopulmonary resuscitation for cardiac arrest: a scoping review
Resuscitation · Added 3 September 2026

Scoping review of 22 studies (1,254 patients, 84% OHCA) mapping intra-arrest transoesophageal echocardiography — insertion succeeded in 80–100% of cases and TEE changed management in 31–48% of patients, but the two randomised trials found no difference in ROSC, survival, or favourable neurological outcome versus conventional CPR.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Scoping review of 22 studies
Participants
1,254 patients (84% OHCA)
Main finding
Successful TEE insertion 80–100%; TEE changed management in 31–48%; two RCTs found no difference in ROSC, survival or favourable neurological outcome versus conventional CPR
Caveat
Mostly single-centre observational studies without comparators; no long-term outcome data
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). TEE is an in-hospital, physician-level tool with no prehospital role in ACTAS — interesting for where resuscitation science is heading, but nothing changes on the road, and no patient-centred benefit has been shown.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and limitations of intra-arrest echocardiography for guiding resuscitation decisions in cardiac arrest.

    Resuscitation Plus · 8 Sep 2026 · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    Secondary analysis of 477 volunteers in prolonged manikin CPR — compression depth and chest recoil decayed within minutes, and a double-click metronome with separate compression and release cues preserved quality significantly longer than a standard metronome.

  • Community CPR training only helps public arrests

    Resuscitation · 3 Sep 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Difference-in-differences evaluation of a year-long community hands-only CPR training initiative across 20,414 OHCAs — overall bystander CPR rates rose from 37.4% to 42.5% but the adjusted intervention effect wasn't significant (+2.94 pp, 95% CI −0.95 to 6.83); only public-location arrests showed a significant increase (+8.59 pp), with no effect at home and no detectable survival or neurological benefit.

  • Racial gaps in bystander CPR for opioid OHCA

    Resuscitation · 2 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective cohort study using NEMSIS data (n=19,612) found Black patients with opioid-associated OHCA had significantly lower odds of receiving bystander CPR than White patients (AOR 0.70, 95% CI 0.63-0.77), with Hispanic patients also less likely (AOR 0.82).

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 1 Sep 2026 · Simulation · Context only

Updated paediatric traumatic arrest position statement

Prehospital Trauma Compendium: Prehospital Management of Pediatric Traumatic Out-of-Hospital Circulatory Arrest – A Joint Position Statement and Resource Document of NAEMSP, ACS-COT, ACEP, and AAP
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-19 · Added 20 November 2026

A US joint position statement and resource document from NAEMSP, ACS-COT, ACEP and AAP updates guidance on withholding or discontinuing resuscitation in paediatric traumatic out-of-hospital circulatory arrest, prioritising reversible causes, rapid haemorrhage control, least-invasive airway care and transport to a trauma centre when safe and appropriate.

Main finding
Recommendations: promptly identify children who may benefit from trauma-centre transport; treat reversible causes with direct pressure, wound packing and tourniquets, least-invasive airway management, and chest decompression only if thoracic trauma is suspected (no empiric bilateral decompression); compressions and ventilations have a larger role in paediatric than adult traumatic arrest.
Caveat
US consensus guidance, not an Australian guideline — withholding and termination decisions still follow local CPGs.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Every intervention it recommends — haemorrhage control, least-invasive airway care, chest decompression for suspected tension pneumothorax — is a core Australian paramedic skill; chest decompression is carried by most services but not all, so a couple will still need local guidance on the specifics. The withholding/termination framing aligns with how Australian major-trauma practice treats these cases.

Learning goal

Refine my judgement on identifying children with traumatic arrest who may benefit from rapid haemorrhage control and trauma-centre transport.

  • Prehospital TXA lowers mortality in bleeding trauma

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 19 Nov 2026 · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    A systematic review of randomised evidence, authored in Saudi Arabia, pooled three trials of prehospital TXA in trauma and found lower 28–30-day mortality (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68–0.95) without a significant rise in thromboembolic events or seizures.

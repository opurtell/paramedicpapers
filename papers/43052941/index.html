Shift work is rough on paramedics' teeth

Oral health behaviours and shift work barriers among Hungarian EMS personnel: a cross-sectional study
BMC Oral Health · 2026-08-25 · Added 20 November 2026

A Hungarian nationwide cross-sectional survey of 1,061 National Ambulance Service staff found over half (54.9%) reported current or recent dental or oral problems, and on-duty hygiene-kit availability was strongly associated with daily on-shift brushing (85% vs 21%) among 24-hour shift workers.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Nationwide cross-sectional online survey
Participants
1,061 Hungarian National Ambulance Service staff
Main finding
54.9% reported current or recent dental/oral problems, 20% had no dental check-up in two years, and hygiene-kit availability was strongly associated with daily or more frequent on-duty brushing among 24-hour staff (85% vs 21%); 88–90% were willing to undergo workplace screening.
Caveat
Self-reported cross-sectional survey — associations, not causation, and no outcome follow-up.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The 24-hour shift patterns mirror Australian ambulance rosters and the fix it points to — enabling on-duty hygiene and routine care — is an organisational one any service could adopt, but oral health is a small slice of workforce wellbeing and the study is self-reported survey data from a single national system.

Learning goal

Understand how shift work and workplace hygiene-kit access shape oral health for Australian paramedics.

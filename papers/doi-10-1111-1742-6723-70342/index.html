ED ultrasound archiving works in Australia

Ultrasound Archiving in the Emergency Department: Implementation, Governance and 4‐Year Outcomes at an Australian Tertiary Emergency Department
Emergency Medicine Australasia · Added 3 September 2026

Four-year implementation study of a middleware ED ultrasound archiving and QA workflow at Nepean Hospital — 7,281 studies archived across 15 modalities with zero data loss or failures, automated trainee logbooks saving each trainee ~2 h/week, and clinician time (not software) the dominant recurring cost.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Single-centre retrospective service implementation analysis
Participants
7,281 archived ED ultrasound studies across 15 modalities at one Australian tertiary ED
Main finding
No failures or data loss over four years; annual volume grew from 1,083 (2022) to 2,224 (2025); automated trainee logbooks save each trainee ~2 hours/week; dominant recurring cost was clinician time.
Caveat
Single-centre retrospective implementation report without comparison group
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). ED-based governance, not prehospital, but the archiving/QA/logbook template is directly relevant to any Australian ambulance service building an ultrasound QA or training program.

Learning goal

Understand how ultrasound image archiving and governance support quality assurance and training in prehospital practice.

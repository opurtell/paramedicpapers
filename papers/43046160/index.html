Chinese OHCA registry: low survival, low AED use

Epidemiology, outcomes, and associated factors of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in Zhejiang, China: a province-wide registry study
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-08-22 · Added 19 November 2026

A Chinese province-wide registry study of 17,646 EMS-treated OHCAs in Zhejiang found 3.0% survived to 30 days, with bystander CPR in 47.7% and bystander AED use just 1.8%.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective analysis of a prospective Utstein-style province-wide EMS registry
Participants
17,646 EMS-treated adult medical OHCAs, 2024–2025
Main finding
30-day survival 3.0%, favourable neurological outcome 2.0%; bystander CPR 47.7%, dispatcher-assisted CPR 20.0%, bystander AED use 1.8%; shockable rhythm the strongest factor for survival and neurological recovery; survival declined with longer EMS response times.
Caveat
Retrospective registry data from a community-response system far weaker than Australia's, limiting direct comparison.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A very different community-response system — the low bystander rates frame how much headroom community response still has, including in Australian systems with similar weak links.

Learning goal

Recognise the system factors that drive bystander CPR and AED use in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

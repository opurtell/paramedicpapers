Paramedic elevation doesn't help CPR in obesity simulation

Effect of Body Position on Chest Compression Quality During CPR in an Obesity Simulation Model: A Randomized Crossover Trial
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 1 September 2026

Randomised crossover simulation trial testing whether 10cm elevation of paramedics improves chest compression quality in obese manikins — it didn't, obesity still impaired compression depth by ~18mm despite increased rescuer effort

Study type
Simulation
Design
Randomised crossover simulation trial
Participants
60 paramedics
Main finding
Compression depth reduced ~18 mm with simulated obesity (adjusted mean differences 17.83 mm and 18.44 mm vs standard, both p<0.001); 10 cm paramedic elevation made no difference (p=0.523)
Caveat
Simulation with manikins, not real patients
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). transferability — Directly relevant to cardiac arrest care (CMG 4), practical ergonomic finding that doesn't require equipment change

Learning goal

Understand how patient obesity affects my ability to achieve effective compression depth during resuscitation.

  • Community CPR training only helps public arrests

    Resuscitation · 3 Sep 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Difference-in-differences evaluation of a year-long community hands-only CPR training initiative across 20,414 OHCAs — overall bystander CPR rates rose from 37.4% to 42.5% but the adjusted intervention effect wasn't significant (+2.94 pp, 95% CI −0.95 to 6.83); only public-location arrests showed a significant increase (+8.59 pp), with no effect at home and no detectable survival or neurological benefit.

  • Intra-arrest TOE feasible but no survival gain

    Resuscitation · 3 Sep 2026 · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    Scoping review of 22 studies (1,254 patients, 84% OHCA) mapping intra-arrest transoesophageal echocardiography — insertion succeeded in 80–100% of cases and TEE changed management in 31–48% of patients, but the two randomised trials found no difference in ROSC, survival, or favourable neurological outcome versus conventional CPR.

  • Racial gaps in bystander CPR for opioid OHCA

    Resuscitation · 2 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective cohort study using NEMSIS data (n=19,612) found Black patients with opioid-associated OHCA had significantly lower odds of receiving bystander CPR than White patients (AOR 0.70, 95% CI 0.63-0.77), with Hispanic patients also less likely (AOR 0.82).

  • Reversible causes: ED vs out-of-hospital arrest

    Resuscitation Plus · 28 Aug 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective cohort study comparing reversible causes in ED vs. out-of-hospital cardiac arrest — hypoxia was the most common cause overall but occurred less frequently in ED arrests (46.6% vs 57.2%), while hypovolemia (33.1% vs 23.2%) and cardiac tamponade (2.9% vs 0.6%) were significantly more common in ED.

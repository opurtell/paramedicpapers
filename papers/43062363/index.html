Community paramedicine programs can't agree on names

Terminology Without Consensus: A Thematic Analysis of Nomenclature Choice and Program Structure Among Expanded EMS Programs in a Single State
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-20 · Added 21 November 2026

A US qualitative study interviewing 23 expanded-EMS programs in Ohio found inconsistent use and interpretation of mobile integrated health and community paramedicine terminology, with one in four programs using alternative names.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews
Participants
23 of 58 identified expanded-EMS programs in Ohio
Main finding
Inconsistent use and interpretation of MIH, CP and MIH-CP terms; 1 in 4 programs used alternative terminology (e.g. neighbourhood paramedic, EMS outreach), attributed to variable definitions and limited stakeholder understanding
Caveat
Single US state with 23 of 58 programs participating — selection and generalisability limits
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A US tiered-response and community paramedicine model that only some Australian services run, but the naming-and-identity confusion it documents mirrors debates wherever ambulance services stretch beyond transport.

Learning goal

Understand how inconsistent terminology shapes how community paramedicine programs are understood and integrated in Australian settings.

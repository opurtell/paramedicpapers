HEMS vs road: guideline adherence in trauma

Guideline adherence in helicopter versus ground emergency medical services: an indication-based analysis of 129,783 patients from the TraumaRegister DGU®
European Journal of Trauma and Emergency Surgery · 2026-10-21 · Added 24 October 2026

Retrospective analysis of 129,783 severely injured patients in the German TraumaRegister DGU comparing guideline-recommended prehospital interventions between helicopter and ground transport — HEMS showed consistently higher intervention rates across indication-based subgroups, such as advanced airway in impaired consciousness (96.5% vs 82.9%) and chest drains (50.5% vs 40.9%).

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective multicentre registry cohort (TraumaRegister DGU, 2016–2024)
Participants
129,783 adults with severe trauma (25% HEMS)
Main finding
HEMS transport associated with higher intervention rates across all indication-based subgroups: advanced airway in impaired consciousness 96.5% vs 82.9%, chest tube placement 50.5% vs 40.9%, despite HEMS patients being younger and more severely injured
Caveat
Descriptive analysis with indication surrogates from registry variables and no adjustment for confounding by injury severity or scene factors
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A physician-staffed model, and chest drains sit beyond Australian paramedic scope while advanced airway is an intensive care paramedic skill in most services — but the helicopter-utilisation question is live here too.

Learning goal

Understand how transport mode and scene factors influence adherence to trauma care guidelines in Australia.

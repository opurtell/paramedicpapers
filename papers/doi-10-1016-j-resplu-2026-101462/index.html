Women post-OHCA get less angiography

Coronary angiography and reperfusion therapy following out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in women
Resuscitation Plus · Added 8 September 2026

Nationwide Swedish cohort of 8,641 post-OHCA admissions — women were less likely to undergo coronary angiography and PCI than men, yet intervention rates were even once angiographic findings were accounted for, and a third of angiographed women without shockable rhythm or ST elevation had significant coronary disease.

Study type
Registry
Design
Nationwide registry cohort
Participants
8,641 patients admitted alive after OHCA (29.4% women)
Main finding
Coronary angiography in 31.5% of women vs 51.0% of men; women less likely to undergo angiography (OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.66-0.90) and PCI (OR 0.76, 95% CI 0.64-0.90); after adjusting for angiographic findings intervention rates were similar (OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.80-1.18); significant coronary disease in 31% of angiographed women with non-shockable rhythm and no ST elevation
Caveat
Swedish registry data; residual confounding and no data on prehospital or out-of-hospital factors influencing angiography decisions
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — in-hospital post-ROSC care, but a useful reminder that OHCA without ST elevation can still mean obstructive disease

Learning goal

Recognise how sex-based bias may influence reperfusion decisions for women after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

    Resuscitation Plus · 8 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

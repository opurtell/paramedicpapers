Physician-directed ALS: no survival gain

Regional implementation of a prehospital advanced life support program with video-assisted physician direction and outcomes after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest
Resuscitation · 2026-10 · Added 22 October 2026

A 129,207-case Korean difference-in-differences study found regions adopting video physician-directed prehospital ALS for cardiac arrest had modestly higher prehospital ROSC (+1.35 percentage points) but no gain in survival or neurological outcome, with prehospital times stretching about 4 minutes.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective difference-in-differences cohort
Participants
129,207 adults with OHCA across South Korea
Main finding
SALS regions gained prehospital ROSC (+1.35 pp, 95% CI 0.56–2.15) but not survival to discharge (−0.35 pp) or favourable neurological outcome, while prehospital time rose 4.12 minutes
Caveat
Event-study analysis suggested differential pre-intervention trends, weakening the time-interval finding
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Real-time physician direction of paramedic ALS is not the Australian crew model, but the finding feeds the broader question of how much on-scene advanced care buys in cardiac arrest.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and system costs of physician-directed prehospital advanced life support in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

  • Fatigue common a year after cardiac arrest

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    A Victorian population-based cohort of 728 out-of-hospital cardiac arrest survivors found fatigue in 35.8%, anxiety in 22.6% and depression in 18.0% at 12 months, with heavier symptom burden linked to worse quality of life, function and return to work.

  • Pad application is the AED time bottleneck

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Timestamps from 8,115 remotely monitored Japanese AEDs showed pad application consumed a median 81% of the power-on-to-first-analysis interval, with completion inside about 50 seconds marking shocks delivered within 60 seconds of power-on.

  • Dispatcher training + volunteer app: more CPR, no outcome gain

    The Lancet. Public Health · 22 Oct 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial in French EMS agencies found dispatcher training in arrest recognition, telephone CPR and a geolocated volunteer responder app was associated with more bystander CPR (61.4% to 70.9%, adjusted OR 1.54), an effect that lost significance after adjusting for secular trends; clinical outcomes did not improve.

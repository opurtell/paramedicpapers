Ultrasound maps CPR hand position over LVOT not iAMC

Where should we compress? Ultrasound identification of the ideal area of maximal compression for CPR
Resuscitation · Added 9 August 2026

Prospective observational study using transthoracic echocardiography to map optimal chest compression location — guideline-recommended hand placement frequently overlies the left ventricular outflow tract rather than the ideal area of maximal compression.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Prospective observational echocardiographic mapping study
Participants
152 adult ED patients and volunteers
Main finding
Guideline hand position overlies the LVOT (median 1.5 cm from sternum) rather than the ideal area of maximal compression (median 4 cm lateral); LVOT within 1 cm of sternum in 50% vs 2.7% for iAMC
Caveat
Anatomical mapping in non-arrest subjects; no link shown to CPR outcomes
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Anatomically relevant to CPR technique, but requires ultrasound for application — the finding that standard compression points may not be optimal is worth noting, though practice change would need stronger outcome evidence.

Learning goal

Weigh whether current hand position guidance targets the best compression area during cardiac arrest resuscitation.

GCS-based triage misses seriously injured trauma patients

Concordance between prehospital Glasgow Coma Scale–based classification and diagnosis-based injury severity in destination decisions for suspected severe trauma: a linked EMS–hospital cohort study
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-10 · Added 13 November 2026

A South Korean linked EMS–hospital cohort of 5,841 suspected severe trauma patients found weak agreement between prehospital GCS classification and diagnosis-based injury severity (κ 0.192), with one in ten patients (10.0%) severely injured despite GCS 9–15 and destination undertriage of 33.6% (GCS-based) and 47.3% (diagnosis-based), roughly doubled in rural areas.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Linked EMS–hospital population-based cohort
Participants
5,841 suspected severe trauma patients aged ≥15 (6,179 analysed)
Main finding
GCS 3–8 detected diagnosis-based severe injury with 54.2% sensitivity (κ 0.192); one in ten patients (10.0%) met the anatomical severity criterion despite GCS 9–15; destination undertriage was 33.6% (GCS-based) and 47.3% (ICISS-based), with rural disparity (aOR 2.65 and 2.04).
Caveat
ICISS severity is a post-hoc, diagnosis-based measure, and undertriage definitions differ between systems.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). GCS-based trauma triage and destination decisions are core practice on every Australian ambulance crew, and the rural undertriage gap mirrors our own geography.

Learning goal

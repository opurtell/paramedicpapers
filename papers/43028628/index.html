Social support buffers the violence-to-burnout pathway

Workplace Violence Exposure and Burnout Among Fire-Service-Based Paramedics in South Korea: The Indirect Association Through Post-Traumatic Stress Symptoms and the Moderating Roles of Social Support and Resilience
Healthcare · 2026-10-24 · Added 15 November 2026

A South Korean cross-sectional study of 210 fire-service paramedics found workplace violence exposure was associated with burnout indirectly through post-traumatic stress symptoms, with social support weakening that pathway.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional moderated mediation survey
Participants
210 fire-service-based paramedics in Busan
Main finding
Workplace violence exposure associated with burnout indirectly through post-traumatic stress symptoms; social support moderated the violence–PTSS (p = 0.002) and violence–burnout (p = 0.015) associations; resilience findings did not survive correction
Caveat
Cross-sectional self-report, and some social-support findings depended on excluding one influential observation
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). South Korean fire-service paramedics, but violence exposure, PTSD symptoms and burnout are occupational realities for paramedics everywhere, and social support is something services can build.

Learning goal

Understand how social support buffers the impact of workplace violence on my wellbeing and burnout risk.

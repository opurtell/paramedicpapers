Physiology-based traumatic shock pathway rolled out

Focus Where It Matters: Design and Implementation of a Physiology‐Based Care Pathway for Traumatic Shock
Emergency Medicine Australasia · 2026-11-04 · Added 6 November 2026

An Australian Level 1 trauma centre implemented a physiology-based traumatic shock pathway (SBP ≤ 90 mmHg, shock index > 1, prehospital blood or senior clinician concern) and post-implementation staff surveys rated the equipment, interventions and roles highly, though clinical effectiveness wasn't assessed.

Study type
Other
Design
Single-centre quality-improvement implementation study with pre/post staff surveys
Participants
3,667 major trauma patients, of whom 324 (8.8%) met shocked trauma criteria
Main finding
Shocked patients had markedly higher ED mortality (6.8% vs 0.4%); favourable ratings of trauma bay equipment rose from about one-quarter to 87.0%, with 85.2–88.9% favourability for timely life-saving interventions, pathway embedding and role confidence.
Caveat
Evaluation was staff experience only — no clinical outcome assessment.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). An ED trauma-bay implementation study, and prehospital blood — one of its activation triggers — is limited to a few Australian services, so this informs the debate more than current practice.

Learning goal

Recognise early physiological indicators of traumatic shock to guide timely activation of blood-based resuscitation pathways.

  • Lab work targets haemolysis in stored whole blood

    Resuscitation Plus · 1 Nov 2026 · Other · Context only

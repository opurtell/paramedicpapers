Embedded low-acuity pathway improves rural ED performance

Embedded Low‐Acuity Pathway in the Emergency Department: An Alternative to Stand‐Alone Urgent Care Services
Emergency Medicine Australasia · Added 15 September 2026

Quasi-experimental study of an embedded ultra-low acuity pathway at Dubbo Base Hospital (n=4,798) — pathway availability halved did-not-wait rates (14.2%→6.9%), lifted seen-on-time and treatment performance, and cut mean ED length of stay by 51.7 minutes.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Single-centre pragmatic quasi-experimental study
Participants
4,798 ATS Category 4–5 presentations at Dubbo Base Hospital (ULAP 2,178 vs standard care 2,620)
Main finding
ULAP was associated with DNW falling from 14.2% to 6.9% (adjusted OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.35–0.53), seen-within-benchmark rising to 82.0% (OR 1.90, 1.64–2.20), ETP rising to 83.3% (OR 1.96, 1.69–2.28), and mean LOS 51.7 minutes shorter (95% CI −65.1 to −38.4).
Caveat
Single rural centre, quasi-experimental design with workforce-dependent exposure (risk of confounding).
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — system redesign rather than clinical care, but directly relevant to where low-acuity patients could be directed instead of transported

Learning goal

Understand how embedded low-acuity pathways reduce did-not-wait rates in rural emergency care.

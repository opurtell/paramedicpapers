Cardiac arrest

ROSC nomogram from a physician-led EMS

Influencing factors analysis and prediction model development for ROSC in adult non-traumatic OHCA: a retrospective study based on pre-hospital emergency settings
Annals of Medicine · 2026-10-14 · Added 16 October 2026

Chinese retrospective study of 9,386 non-traumatic OHCA cases built a ROSC prediction nomogram (AUC 0.775) from factors including shockable rhythm, intubation and — unique to physician-staffed systems — the attending physician's sex and seniority.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort with nomogram development
Participants
9,386 adult non-traumatic OHCA cases, Tianjin 2023–2024
Main finding
7.2% achieved sustained ROSC; independent predictors included shockable initial rhythm (aOR 2.85), intubation (aOR 2.29) and public location (aOR 1.29), with a nomogram AUC of 0.775
Caveat
Single-centre retrospective data from a physician-dispatch system, internally validated only
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). Physician-staffed Chinese EMS model — predictors like physician sex, title and years of practice simply don't exist in Australian paramedic-led systems.

Learning goal

Weigh how initial rhythm, airway management and arrest location shape my prognostication for return of spontaneous circulation in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

Related papers on Cardiac arrest

  • Signs of life in traumatic OHCA

    The Journal of Emergency Medicine · 16 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective analysis of 462 adults with traumatic OHCA from a Japanese prospective registry found 4.8% survived beyond the ED, with the best survival where scene cardiac activity and spontaneous respiration coexisted (25%).

  • Dispatcher CPR and survival in shockable OHCA

    Physiology International · 15 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Nationwide Hungarian cohort of 3,674 shockable OHCAs found dispatcher-assisted CPR ongoing at ambulance arrival was associated with far better outcomes than crew-initiated CPR — 30-day survival 54.6% vs 7.1% and one-year survival 45.8% vs 6.2%.

  • Three post-ROSC lab phenotypes predict outcome

    Resuscitation · 11 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Latent class analysis of 14 laboratory values drawn within six hours of ROSC in 1,514 resuscitated OHCA patients identified three phenotypes, with the multi-organ dysfunction group at roughly double the odds of poor neurological recovery compared with the others.

  • Opioid OHCA outcomes similar to other arrests

    Resuscitation · 11 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 10,786 EMS-treated OHCAs in a Canadian registry found opioid-associated arrests had similar outcomes to undifferentiated arrests (OR 1.02), but Utstein variables like initial rhythm and witnessed status predicted outcome differently in the opioid group.

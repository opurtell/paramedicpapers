Resuscitative thoracotomy: no survivors past 10 minutes

Traumatic Emergency Thoracotomy for Resuscitation in Shock (TETRiS): a national service evaluation of resuscitative thoracotomy practice outside the operating theatre
Resuscitation · 2026-10 · Added 30 October 2026

A prospective 12-month UK national service evaluation across 20 HEMS and 26 major trauma centres studied 116 resuscitative thoracotomies (82 prehospital) and found 43.1% achieved ROSC but only 6.9% survived to discharge, with no survivors beyond 10 minutes of traumatic cardiac arrest.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective national service evaluation (PHOTON, TERN, NaTRIC networks)
Participants
116 patients undergoing resuscitative thoracotomy (82 prehospital)
Main finding
43.1% achieved ROSC and 6.9% survived to discharge; outcomes were substantially better with cardiac tamponade than exsanguination, and there were no survivors beyond 10 minutes of traumatic cardiac arrest
Caveat
Prospective service evaluation without a comparator group — patient selection for thoracotomy drives the outcomes
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital thoracotomy is a doctor-delivered HEMS skill in the UK model, not an Australian paramedic intervention — but the 10-minute ceiling and the tamponade-versus-exsanguination split inform how trauma arrest patients are triaged to major trauma centres here.

Learning goal

Weigh patient selection for prehospital resuscitative thoracotomy, especially in traumatic arrest from exsanguination.

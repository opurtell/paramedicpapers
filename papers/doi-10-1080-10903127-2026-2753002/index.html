ECPR for OHCA: pump the brakes

Rapid Transport to Extracorporeal CPR: Urging Caution Prior to Implementation
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-10-22 · Added 25 October 2026

A Prehospital Emergency Care commentary reviewing the ARREST, INCEPTION and Prague OHCA trials argues the ECPR evidence is not strong enough for widespread prehospital adoption, because any benefit cannot be separated from the effect of the intra-arrest transport ECPR programs require.

Study type
Other
Design
Narrative review/commentary of three randomised ECPR trials
Participants
Three RCTs (ARREST n=30, INCEPTION, Prague OHCA)
Main finding
Only ARREST (30 patients) strongly favoured ECPR; INCEPTION and Prague OHCA were not statistically significant, and the authors argue intra-arrest transport effects cannot be excluded as a driver of reported benefit
Caveat
A commentary, not new data — interpretation of already-published trials
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). ECPR for OHCA depends on coordinated intra-arrest transport, so the implementation decision sits with ambulance systems here too; no Australian service runs routine ECPR for arrest, and this is a useful framing piece before anyone is asked to build one.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and harms of intra-arrest transport for extracorporeal CPR candidacy in refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

  • Hot nights linked to more OHCA

    International Journal of Biometeorology · 25 Oct 2026 · Case-control · Hypothesis-generating

    A nationwide time-stratified case-crossover study of 135,898 emergency hospitalisations for OHCA in Japan found hot nights (minimum temperature above the regional 92.5th percentile) increased OHCA hospitalisation risk by 9%, with the highest risks in women and people aged 75+.

  • Physician-directed ALS: no survival gain

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    A 129,207-case Korean difference-in-differences study found regions adopting video physician-directed prehospital ALS for cardiac arrest had modestly higher prehospital ROSC (+1.35 percentage points) but no gain in survival or neurological outcome, with prehospital times stretching about 4 minutes.

  • Pad application is the AED time bottleneck

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Timestamps from 8,115 remotely monitored Japanese AEDs showed pad application consumed a median 81% of the power-on-to-first-analysis interval, with completion inside about 50 seconds marking shocks delivered within 60 seconds of power-on.

  • Dispatcher training + volunteer app lifts bystander CPR

    The Lancet. Public Health · 22 Oct 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial across 14 French EMS agencies found dispatcher training in arrest recognition, telephone CPR and a geolocated volunteer responder app increased bystander CPR from 61.4% to 70.9% (adjusted OR 1.54), though survival did not improve.

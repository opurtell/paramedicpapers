Bystander CPR defines the prehospital OHCA profile

Latent Class Analysis of Pre-Hospital Chain-of-Survival Indicators and Outcomes After Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Nationwide Korean Registry Study, 2013–2024
Medicina · 2026-11-21 · Added 24 November 2026

A South Korean nationwide registry analysis of 78,226 adult OHCAs (2013–2024) found prehospital chain-of-survival indicators formed no meaningful profile beyond bystander CPR status, which was associated with better survival to discharge (aOR 1.61) and favourable neurological outcome (aOR 2.23).

Study type
Registry
Design
Nationwide registry cohort with latent class analysis
Participants
78,226 adults with OHCA, 2013–2024
Main finding
Bystander CPR was associated with survival to discharge (17.7% vs 10.0%; aOR 1.61, 95% CI 1.52–1.71), ED ROSC (aOR 1.11) and favourable neurological outcome (aOR 2.23, 95% CI 2.04–2.44); call-to-ED time ≤20 min did not separate the latent classes, and ED arrivals within 20 min fell from 28.8% to 5.6% while bystander CPR rose from 66.3% to 75.2%
Caveat
Latent classes built from three binary indicators collapsed onto bystander CPR status, with no CPR quality or interval causality — association, not effect
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Bystander CPR and transport intervals are the same questions in every Australian jurisdiction, and this is a 12-year national registry — the rate on the evidence, no system translation needed.

Learning goal

Strengthen my ability to recognise opportunities to coach bystander CPR and its impact on neurological outcomes.

  • Shockable rhythms decay fast in untreated arrest

    International Emergency Nursing · 26 Nov 2026 · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    Italian-led systematic review of 38 studies (1990–2025, more than 262,700 adult OHCA cases) found shockable rhythms decline progressively with increasing time-to-defibrillation, with earlier shock linked to higher defibrillation success, more ROSC and better survival.

    Resuscitation · 22 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

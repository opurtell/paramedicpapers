Admission hypocalcaemia predicts trauma mortality

Prognostic value of admission ionized calcium and acidosis in severely injured trauma patients
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 8 September 2026

Multicentre trauma-registry cohort of 977 severely injured patients — admission hypocalcaemia was independently associated with hospital mortality and trauma-induced coagulopathy, while apparently elevated ionised calcium mostly reflected profound acidosis.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective multicentre trauma registry cohort
Participants
977 adult trauma patients with admission ionised calcium (median ISS 25)
Main finding
Hypocalcaemia (iCa²⁺ <1.10 mmol/L) in 291 (30%); independently associated with hospital mortality (OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.02-2.85) and trauma-induced coagulopathy (OR 2.24, 95% CI 1.58-3.18); apparent hypercalcaemia (8%) largely attributable to profound acidosis
Caveat
Retrospective design; single level-1 trauma centre dataset despite multicentre registry
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — no prehospital ionised calcium testing, but good context for calcium shifts during major-trauma resus

Learning goal

Recognise hypocalcaemia as a marker of trauma-induced coagulopathy and mortality risk in major haemorrhage.

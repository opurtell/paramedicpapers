US naloxone use varies by region, not rurality

A national cross-sectional analysis of regional and rural-urban variation in emergency medical services naloxone administration for suspected opioid overdose
Journal of Substance Use and Addiction Treatment · 2026-11-12 · Added 14 November 2026

A US cross-sectional analysis of 110,261 EMS activations for suspected opioid overdose found documented naloxone administration in 33.9%, with regional variation (South aOR 2.10 vs Northeast) that persisted while the rural-urban difference attenuated to null after clinical adjustment.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
National cross-sectional analysis of the 2025 NEMSIS public-release dataset with multivariable logistic regression
Participants
110,261 EMS activations with suspected opioid involvement (37,412 naloxone administrations)
Main finding
Adjusted odds of EMS naloxone administration were higher in the South (aOR 2.10, 95% CI 2.01–2.18), Midwest (aOR 1.92) and West (aOR 1.31) versus the Northeast; rural-versus-urban odds were null after clinical adjustment (aOR 1.01, 95% CI 0.94–1.09)
Caveat
Administrative documentation of naloxone as a surveillance marker, not a clinical endpoint — variation may reflect documentation and case-mix differences
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Naloxone is carried by every Australian ambulance service, and the finding is about how documented practice varies with case mix rather than a foreign service model.

Learning goal

Understand how regional and rural factors shape naloxone use and documentation in suspected opioid overdose care.

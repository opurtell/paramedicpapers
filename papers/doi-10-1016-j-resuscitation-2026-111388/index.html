Systematic review of newborn PPV at birth

Positive pressure ventilation in newborns at birth: a systematic review of recipient characteristics and temporal trends
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 13 November 2026

This multinational systematic review of 42 studies (1984–2024) on positive pressure ventilation (PPV) at birth found PPV incidence of 3.0%–8.0% in population-based studies excluding CPAP, with higher rates in preterm infants, rising overall and facemask PPV over time, and stable or declining tracheal-tube PPV.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review (PROSPERO-registered, PRISMA 2020)
Participants
42 studies from 34 countries, 269 to 10,696,831 livebirths per study
Main finding
PPV incidence 3.0%–8.0% in nine population-based/large multicentre studies excluding CPAP; rates much higher among very and extremely preterm infants; overall and facemask PPV increasing over time while tracheal-tube PPV was stable or declining; lower incidence in low-resource and lower-level birthing centres; few studies reported indications or timing of PPV initiation.
Caveat
Marked heterogeneity in CPAP distinction, interface definitions and eligibility criteria precluded meta-analysis, and reporting of indications and timing was limited and inconsistent.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). PPV at birth is a core paramedic skill for births attended in the prehospital setting; understanding typical incidence, gestational-age patterns and reporting gaps can inform Australian practice expectations and documentation standards, though most included data come from hospital birthing settings rather than prehospital care.

Learning goal

Recognise which newborns at birth are most likely to need positive pressure ventilation and how practice patterns are changing.

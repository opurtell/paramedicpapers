Prehospital cricothyroidotomy: rare but often successful

Prehospital emergency cricothyroidotomy in Denmark: a nationwide cohort study, 2016–2023
Resuscitation · Added 3 September 2026

Nationwide Danish cohort of all 71 prehospital emergency cricothyroidotomies over 2016–2023 — overall procedural success 87.3% (83.3% in OHCA), aspiration and oropharyngeal bleeding the commonest indications, but the procedure was rare (0.12% of 39,885 OHCAs) and OHCA outcomes were poor (ROSC 25%, 30-day survival 4.5%, 1-year survival 2.3%).

Study type
Registry
Design
Nationwide registry-based observational cohort
Participants
71 patients undergoing prehospital emergency cricothyroidotomy (48 with OHCA), Denmark 2016–2023
Main finding
Procedural success 87.3% overall (83.3% in OHCA, 95.7% non-OHCA). Cricothyroidotomy performed in 48 of 39,885 OHCAs (0.12%). In OHCA patients, ROSC 25.0%, 30-day survival 4.5%, one-year survival 2.3%.
Caveat
Registry-based design in a physician-staffed Danish EMS system; very small numbers limit generalisability
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A failed-airway rescue skill relevant to ACTAS but ICP-only (CMG 3B intubation algorithm incl. surgical airway), and the data come from a physician-staffed system, so case mix and operator context differ from ours.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when surgical cricothyroidotomy is the right airway option in can't intubate, can't oxygenate situations.

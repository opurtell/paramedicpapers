Traumatic pneumothorax: most EDs don't drain early

Management of Traumatic Pneumothorax in the Emergency Department: A Two‐Centre Retrospective Cohort Study
Emergency Medicine Australasia · 2026-11-18 · Added 20 November 2026

An Australian two-centre retrospective cohort of 339 ED patients with traumatic pneumothorax found two-thirds were managed conservatively before leaving the ED, and of the 50 who arrived after a prehospital pleural intervention, 27 had chest drains inserted to formalise finger thoracostomy or needle decompression.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Two-centre retrospective cohort
Participants
339 patients aged 14 and over with traumatic pneumothorax
Main finding
231 patients (68.1%) were initially managed without drainage; pneumothorax size >35 mm (aOR 41.0, 95% CI 16.4–102) and haemothorax (aOR 6.3, 95% CI 2.5–15.8) predicted ED-initiated drainage; 22.8% of drained patients had a recorded complication and in-hospital mortality was 5.6%.
Caveat
Retrospective design at only two EDs, and the prehospital pleural-intervention subgroup is small (n=50).
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The endpoints are in-hospital, but the prehospital pleural-intervention subgroup speaks directly to how prehospital needle decompression and finger thoracostomy hold up downstream — a skill most Australian services carry and some don't, so the findings inform practice unevenly across the country.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when conservative management of traumatic pneumothorax is appropriate after prehospital pleural intervention.

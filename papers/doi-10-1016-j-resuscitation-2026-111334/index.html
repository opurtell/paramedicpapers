Post-ROSC ECG often misses occlusive MI

The post-ROSC ECG: morphological spectrum of non-ST-elevation occlusion and prognostic markers after cardiac arrest
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 26 September 2026

A retrospective single-centre study of 214 post-ROSC patients found nearly half of angiographically confirmed occlusive infarctions showed non-ST-elevation OMI patterns, and PR-segment prolongation independently predicted reduced 30-day survival.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective single-centre cohort
Participants
214 cardiac arrest survivors admitted to a tertiary ICU (101 with angiography-confirmed OMI)
Main finding
NST-OMI patterns made up 48% of confirmed OMI cases and underwent angiography less often (78% vs 98%); PR-segment prolongation was independently associated with reduced 30-day survival (aOR 0.977 per ms)
Caveat
Single-centre, hospital-side ECGs only, retrospective design with angiography as reference
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The post-ROSC ECG is taken prehospital, so recognising NST-OMI patterns could shape cath-lab activation, but the analysis was done on ICU-admitted survivors and needs prehospital validation.

Learning goal

Strengthen my interpretation of subtle post-ROSC ECG patterns so I recognise occlusive infarction without ST elevation.

VR leads mass-casualty training but frameworks lag

Technology-enhanced training for prehospital mass-casualty incident preparedness: A scoping review
PloS One · 2026-11-12 · Added 14 November 2026

A scoping review of 28 studies on technology-enhanced prehospital mass-casualty training found virtual reality was the most common modality (43%) and 56% of completed studies demonstrated applied learning, but 75% lacked an explicit training design framework.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR) of 28 studies, 2015–2025, with modified Kirkpatrick outcome mapping
Participants
28 studies of technology-enhanced prehospital MCI training
Main finding
Virtual reality was the most common modality (43%, n = 12), 50% of studies used dual-purpose training-and-assessment platforms, and 75% lacked an explicit training design framework; 56% of completed studies showed applied learning
Caveat
Scoping design with no outcome pooling, and no studies from low- or lower-middle-income countries
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). MCI preparedness training is a live issue for Australian services expanding simulation, though the mapped literature comes mostly from higher-resourced systems abroad.

Learning goal

Understand how simulation-based training can strengthen my preparedness for mass-casualty incident response.

Drone AI for mass-casualty triage

A Drone-Integrated Artificial Intelligence-Based System for Mass-Casualty Triage and Remote Monitoring
Prehospital and Disaster Medicine · 2026 · Added 20 October 2026

A research team built and pilot-tested a drone-integrated AI system that measures heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and temperature from drone-captured video and thermal imaging, assigns triage categories, and streams casualty data to dispatch — feasible under controlled indoor conditions.

Study type
Other
Design
System development with pilot accuracy testing
Participants
Not reported (pilot under controlled indoor conditions)
Main finding
The system measured four vital signs from drone imagery via video PPG, thermal imaging and neural networks, generated triage decision support, and transmitted casualty location, vitals and triage category to response teams and dispatch
Caveat
Controlled indoor pilot only — accuracy on real MCI scenes with smoke, crowds and weather is untested
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Drone and remote-triage programs are emerging in Australia but nothing like this is in operational use — it signals where MCI triage tech is heading rather than changing anything crews do today.

Learning goal

Understand how drone and AI triage technologies could support mass-casualty response across Australian contexts.

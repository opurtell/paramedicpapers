Layperson naloxone linked to fewer EMS interventions

Layperson naloxone administration and subsequent emergency medical services care for opioid overdose
Drug and Alcohol Dependence · 2026-10-31 · Added 6 November 2026

In a US cross-sectional study of 26,087 EMS activations for opioid overdose, layperson-administered naloxone (30.7% of cases) was associated with less subsequent EMS naloxone (24.4% vs 34.8%) and lower odds of transport (aOR 0.78).

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional analysis of a national EMS registry
Participants
26,087 EMS activations for opioid overdose (adults 16 and over)
Main finding
Layperson naloxone preceded 30.7% of activations; any subsequent EMS naloxone 24.4% vs 34.8% (aOR 0.73, 95% CI 0.68–0.78) and transport 79.4% vs 84.4% (aOR 0.78, 95% CI 0.73–0.84); advanced airway attempts (1.6% vs 1.4%) and cardiac arrest (6.6% vs 6.0%) uncommon in both groups.
Caveat
Registry cross-section — why transports were lower (refusal versus genuine recovery) isn't known.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Community and take-home naloxone programs run across Australia, and bystander response before EMS arrival is the same question everywhere.

Learning goal

Weigh how prehospital naloxone given by bystanders affects my assessment and transport decisions in opioid overdose.

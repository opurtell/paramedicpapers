SGA vs intubation in OHCA: ROSC lower with SGA

Supraglottic airway versus endotracheal intubation in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Resuscitation Plus · Added 25 September 2026

Systematic review and meta-analysis comparing supraglottic airway devices with endotracheal intubation in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. Abstract not yet available (ahead-of-print).

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis (random-effects, GRADE)
Participants
13 studies, 246,659 adults with OHCA
Main finding
Survival to hospital discharge did not differ (RR 0.96, 95% CI 0.82–1.12). SGA was associated with lower ROSC than tracheal intubation (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77–0.96; prehospital RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.71–0.95), but in the two RCTs SGA was associated with higher ROSC (RR 1.09, 95% CI 1.03–1.16). Favourable neurologic outcome did not differ overall (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.88–1.11); exploratory ≥1-month subgroup favoured TI (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.81–0.99).
Caveat
Mostly observational evidence with substantial heterogeneity (I² up to 96%) and reversal of direction in RCTs
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). Title-only rating — findings can't be assessed without the abstract, though the topic is core ACTAS territory: LMA Supreme is AP scope (CSM A09) and intubation is ICP-only (CMG 3B).

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and harms of supraglottic airway versus tracheal intubation during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest management.

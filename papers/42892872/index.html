Why patients decline hypoglycaemia call-back programs

"It's Scary, but I Handle It": A Qualitative Study on Severe Hypoglycemia Experiences and Reasons for Declining to Participate in a Community Paramedicine Trial to Prevent Hypoglycemia Among People With Diabetes
Diabetes, Obesity, and Cardiometabolic CARE · 2026-07-31 · Added 19 October 2026

Qualitative interviews with adults who declined a community paramedicine hypoglycaemia-prevention program found they normalised severe episodes within their own management routines — time constraints (56%), perceived lack of benefit (25%) and privacy concerns about home visits (25%) kept them out.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative interview study (thematic analysis)
Participants
16 adults with diabetes after EMS-attended severe hypoglycaemia
Main finding
Non-participation reasons: time constraints 56%, perceived lack of benefit 25%, privacy concerns or discomfort with home visits 25%
Caveat
Small US sample of program decliners only — no perspective from those who enrolled.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Community paramedicine programs vary widely across Australia, but the recruitment and privacy barriers are the kind any extended-care or call-back model here would hit.

Learning goal

Understand why patients decline community paramedicine hypoglycaemia follow-up and how privacy and trust shape engagement.

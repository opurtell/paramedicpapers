Drone AEDs arrived first in two-thirds of arrests

Mapping the research landscape of UAV- and eVTOL-enabled emergency medical services: a bibliometric analysis, scoping review, and policy tool assessment
Frontiers in Health Services · 2026-10-15 · Added 31 October 2026

A Chinese-led bibliometric and scoping review of 168 drone and eVTOL emergency medical services studies found drone-delivered AEDs arrived before ambulances in 67% of cases (median lead 3 min 14 s) and drone blood delivery cut transport time by 61%.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Bibliometric analysis, scoping review and policy document analysis
Participants
168 studies reviewed; 62 Chinese policy documents analysed
Main finding
Drone AEDs arrived before ambulances in 67% of cases (median lead time 3 min 14 s); drone blood delivery reduced transport time 61%; modelled networks were cost-effective with OHCA survival gains of 21–46% under model assumptions
Caveat
Synthesis plus Chinese policy analysis, and the survival figures come from models rather than measured outcomes
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Drone-delivered AEDs and blood logistics remain trial-stage technologies; the pooled numbers sketch what a network could do, not current practice.

Learning goal

Understand how drone-delivered AEDs and blood products could integrate with Australian emergency medical response systems.

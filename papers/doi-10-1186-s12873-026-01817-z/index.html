Rural self-harm deaths before ambulance arrival

Factors associated with death before EMS arrival among emergency medical services-attended self-directed violence cases in southern Iran: a cross-sectional study
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-10-17 · Added 19 October 2026

An Iranian cross-sectional study of 2,280 EMS-attended self-directed violence cases found 5.4% died before EMS arrival, with higher odds in rural locations (aOR 2.08), older patients (aOR 1.03/year) and morning calls (aOR 2.14 vs evening).

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional analysis of EMS registry records with multivariable logistic regression
Participants
2,280 EMS-attended self-directed violence cases (124 dead on arrival)
Main finding
Death before EMS arrival associated with rural location (aOR 2.075, 95% CI 1.208–3.564), increasing age (aOR 1.028) and morning (aOR 2.140) and afternoon (aOR 1.882) calls versus evening
Caveat
Registry-only data from one Iranian province with no clinical detail on mechanism, lethality or bystander response
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). From a different system with variable ambulance access, but the pattern — rural cases and time-of-day delays in reaching live patients — speaks to response-interval equity issues Australian services also face.

Learning goal

Recognise which patients experiencing self-directed violence face higher risk of death before ambulance arrival.

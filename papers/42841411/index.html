Ice-water immersion wins for drug hyperthermia

Comparison of cooling methods for drug-induced hyperthermia in a pre-hospital and emergency department setting: a systematic review
Clinical Toxicology · 2026-10-07 · Added 9 October 2026

A systematic review of 71 studies (1,932 patients) comparing cooling methods for drug-induced (and exercise-induced) hyperthermia found ice-water and cold-water immersion fastest at ~0.16–0.17 °C/min and clearly superior to passive cooling.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review with meta-analysis (GRADE)
Participants
71 studies, 1,932 patients, 12 cooling modalities
Main finding
Ice-water immersion cooled at 0.169 ± 0.027 °C/min and cold-water immersion at 0.162 ± 0.037 °C/min — the fastest modalities; ice-water-soaked sheets/towels 0.121 ± 0.030 °C/min; meta-analysis confirmed immersion beats passive cooling
Caveat
Many included studies cover exercise-induced rather than drug-induced hyperthermia, and practicality varies by setting
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Stimulant toxicity and heat illness with hyperthermia are everyday presentations across Australia, and aggressive active cooling is achievable prehospital — the ranking of methods is directly actionable.

Learning goal

Weigh the practicality of immersion cooling for drug-induced hyperthermia in my prehospital practice.

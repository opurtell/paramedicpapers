Flight simulation training: engagement shown, evidence thin

Enhancing High-Fidelity Simulation Flight-Based Training for Critical Care Paramedics in EMS Aviation: A Systematic Review
Air Medical Journal · 2026-09-21 · Added 29 October 2026

A Norwegian-affiliated systematic review of 7 articles found high-fidelity simulation feasible and engaging for critical care paramedic flight training, but insufficient evidence on whether it improves self-awareness or real prehospital performance.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review (CASP appraisal of 7 articles)
Participants
7 studies of high-fidelity simulation for critical care paramedics
Main finding
Learners reported improved expertise, engagement, motivation, responsiveness and mood; evidence was insufficient on self-awareness and on performance of trained crews in the prehospital setting; authors recommend standardised training and assessment.
Caveat
Only 7 heterogeneous studies, none showing a link to real-world prehospital performance.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian aeromedical services use simulation-based training for critical care crews, and the review's core message — learner enthusiasm is well shown, performance benefit is not — applies to any service buying sim time.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on whether simulation training skills transfer to real prehospital critical care performance.

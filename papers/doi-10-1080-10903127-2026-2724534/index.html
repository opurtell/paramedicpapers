Public accepts drone-delivered AEDs

Public Acceptance, Trust, and Self-Efficacy Related to Drone-Assisted Emergency Medical Response in Louisiana
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 1 September 2026

Cross-sectional survey of 586 Louisiana adults on drone-assisted emergency response — high acceptance (mean 4.1/5), trust (3.8), and self-efficacy (4.0), with telecommunicator guidance significantly increasing self-efficacy for both family and stranger scenarios

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional online survey
Participants
586 Louisiana adults
Main finding
Mean acceptance 4.1/5, trust 3.8/5, self-efficacy 4.0/5; trust and self-efficacy strongest predictors of acceptance (R²=.57); telecommunicator guidance increased self-efficacy (Cohen's d 0.41–0.44)
Caveat
Single US state, online panel sample; attitudes may not predict real-world use
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — Forward-looking innovation relevant to future service models, but ACTAS doesn't currently use drones for medical delivery

Learning goal

Understand how public trust and telecommunicator guidance shape community acceptance of drone-delivered AEDs.

Hot nights linked to more OHCA

Association between nighttime heat exposure and emergency hospitalization for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: A time-stratified case-crossover study in Japan
International Journal of Biometeorology · 2026-10-23 · Added 25 October 2026

A nationwide time-stratified case-crossover study of 135,898 emergency hospitalisations for OHCA in Japan found hot nights (minimum temperature above the regional 92.5th percentile) increased OHCA hospitalisation risk by 9%, with the highest risks in women and people aged 75+.

Study type
Case-control
Design
Time-stratified case-crossover, nationwide administrative database (2011–2021)
Participants
135,898 emergency hospitalisations for OHCA
Main finding
Nighttime heat exposure increased OHCA hospitalisation (RR 1.090, 95% CI 1.047–1.135); RR rose to 1.295 in people ≥75 and 1.352 in women in northern regions, and risk was higher where air-conditioning penetration was low
Caveat
Hospitalisation-based — arrests not transported or dead at scene are invisible, and daily minimum temperature is a crude exposure measure
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian summers are trending the same way and our population is ageing identically — this is a hazard pattern crews will meet on night shift, though it is a climate exposure signal, not an intervention trial.

Learning goal

Recognise hotter nighttime temperatures as a driver of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest risk in older and female patients.

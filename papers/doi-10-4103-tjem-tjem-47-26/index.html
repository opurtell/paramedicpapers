Two paramedic attitudes to paediatric pain

Paramedic attitudinal profiles regarding pediatric pain management: A latent class analysis in a standardized emergency medical services system
Turkish Journal of Emergency Medicine · Added 13 October 2026

Cross-sectional survey of 129 paramedics in a standardised Turkish EMS system used latent class analysis to identify two paediatric analgesia attitudinal profiles — proactive/system-oriented (57.4%) and conservative/low-engagement (42.6%) — unrelated to age, sex or experience.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Prospective cross-sectional survey with latent class analysis
Participants
129 paramedics
Main finding
Two profiles: proactive/system-oriented (57.4%, higher endorsement of dosing apps, analgesia algorithms, post-analgesia monitoring and non-pharmacologic adjuncts) vs conservative/low-engagement (42.6%); profile membership not associated with demographics or experience
Caveat
Self-reported attitudes only — whether profiles translate into actual analgesia delivery is unvalidated
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Paediatric analgesia is a universal weak spot and the attitudinal split will feel familiar, but self-reported attitudes in a different system limit direct transfer.

Learning goal

Recognise how my own attitudes towards paediatric analgesia shape my assessment and treatment decisions.

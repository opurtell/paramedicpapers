Japanese mobile EMS app rollout data

Population-based implementation of a mobile emergency medical service platform in Hiroshima, Japan
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 1 September 2026

Population-based descriptive study of a Japanese mobile EMS app capturing 147,213 transport cases over 15 months — 90% were emergency transports, OCR used in 74% of cases, hospital acceptance on first request in 66% with median 5.2 min negotiation time

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Population-based descriptive study of a mobile EMS application
Participants
147,213 EMS transport cases in Hiroshima Prefecture over 15 months
Main finding
90% were emergency transports; hospital acceptance on first request in 66% with median negotiation time 5.2 min; OCR used in 74% of cases; 1,988 cardiac arrest cases captured
Caveat
Descriptive, single prefecture in a different EMS system; no outcome impact assessed
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — Operational innovation relevant to digitising prehospital data and hospital coordination, but Japanese system context differs from ACTAS

Learning goal

Understand how digital platforms for hospital coordination could shape ambulance dispatch and handover practice in Australia.

Prehospital ECPR linked to doubled arrest survival

Prehospital Extracorporeal Life Support in Refractory Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest
The New England Journal of Medicine · 2026-11-07 · Added 10 November 2026

A nationwide Dutch study compared HEMS with prehospital ECPR against conventional HEMS in adults 50 or younger with refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and found 40% survival to discharge with ECPR versus 20% without, with better neurological outcomes at 6 months.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Nationwide nonrandomised comparative cohort (OnScene, Netherlands)
Participants
231 adults ≤ 50 with refractory OHCA (144 ECPR-capable HEMS vs 87 conventional)
Main finding
Survival to discharge 40% with ECPR vs 20% without (OR 2.72, 95% CI 1.59–4.66); favourable neurological outcome at 6 months 36% vs 13% (OR 4.22, 95% CI 2.82–6.32); 32 adverse events with ECPR vs 11
Caveat
Not randomised — patients attended by ECPR-capable HEMS may differ systematically, and the ECPR group had nearly three times the adverse events
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital ECPR runs from physician-staffed helicopter services — an intensive care tier well beyond Australian road practice — so this informs where the evidence is heading rather than what crews do today.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and harms of prehospital ECPR for young adults in refractory cardiac arrest.

  • Big community arrest programme didn't change survival

    The New England Journal of Medicine · 10 Nov 2026 · RCT · Supports current practice

    A cluster-randomised trial across 55 North Carolina county clusters (22,459 patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest) found a multifaceted community and EMS programme did not improve survival with good neurological outcome (9.0% vs 9.4%).

  • PEEP during CPR linked with more ROSC

    The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · 9 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre Belgian cohort of intubated OHCA patients managed by a physician-staffed team found mechanical ventilation with PEEP during CPR was associated with higher odds of ROSC (adjusted OR 1.58) than manual ventilation without PEEP.

  • Community first responders trained but rarely dispatched

    Resuscitation Plus · 8 Nov 2026 · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    A cross-sectional survey of 2,704 registered community first responders in two Swedish regions before local system launch found most were CPR-trained and felt prepared (97.3%) but real dispatch experience was low (15.1% ever dispatched, 1.9% performed CPR).

RhD-positive prehospital blood OK for women

RhD-Positive Transfusion in Females of Childbearing Potential in Hemorrhagic Shock: Risk, Reality, and Policy
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 2 September 2026

Narrative review synthesising evidence on D-alloimmunization and HDFN risk found modeling studies estimate fetal death due to anti-D-mediated HDFN would occur in fewer than 0.1% of females of childbearing potential exposed to RhD-positive blood products in trauma, supporting prehospital RhD-positive transfusion when RhD-negative unavailable.

Main finding
Modelling studies estimate fetal death from anti-D-mediated HDFN in fewer than 0.1% of females of childbearing potential exposed to RhD-positive blood products in trauma, supporting RhD-positive transfusion when RhD-negative products unavailable
Caveat
Narrative review reliant largely on modelling studies rather than direct outcome data
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Directly relevant to haemorrhagic shock management but ACTAS does not carry blood products — would require guideline change and formulary expansion to adopt.

Learning goal

Weigh the risks and benefits of RhD-positive blood transfusion for females of childbearing potential in haemorrhagic shock.

Lactate drives prehospital mSOFA risk

Deconstructing the Modified Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (mSOFA) Score: A Decision Tree Analysis Reveals the Pathophysiological Hierarchy of Organ Dysfunction Predictors in Prehospital Critical Care
QJM · 2026-10-12 · Added 13 October 2026

Prospective Spanish multicentre study of 12,212 high-priority patients deconstructed the prehospital mSOFA score with a decision tree, finding lactate ≥3.8 mmol/L was the primary discriminator for 2-day in-hospital mortality, ahead of SaFi, GCS and creatinine.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective multicentre observational cohort
Participants
12,212 adults transferred at high priority across three Spanish EMS
Main finding
Cohort mortality was 5.0%; the decision tree's first split was lactate at 3.8 mmol/L, followed by SaFi, GCS and creatinine (order depending on lactate level)
Caveat
Predicts 2-day in-hospital mortality only, and the decision tree hasn't been externally validated outside Spain
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Point-of-care lactate sits with advanced-care crews in a handful of Australian services, most don't carry it — so this informs critical care practice and score design rather than routine protocols.

Learning goal

Recognise how elevated lactate can sharpen my prehospital risk stratification of critically unwell adults.

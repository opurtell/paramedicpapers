Paediatric prehospital fentanyl: serious adverse effects rare

Is prehospital opioid administration safe in children? A register-based study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-05 · Added 7 November 2026

A Danish register study of 1,531 children given prehospital opioids (99.7% fentanyl) found 15.1% had at least one clinical sign compatible with opioid adverse effects, serious signs were uncommon (bradycardia and respiratory depression 0.7% each), and 63.6% were still in pain at hospital arrival.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective register-based cohort with medical record review
Participants
1,531 children aged 0–14 given prehospital opioids, Denmark 2017–2022
Main finding
15.1% had at least one clinical sign compatible with opioid-related adverse effects; bradycardia 0.7%, respiratory depression 0.7%; no opioid antidote given; 63.6% in pain at arrival and 51% received further in-hospital analgesia.
Caveat
Retrospective register review — nonspecific signs such as nausea and drowsiness can't be firmly attributed to opioids (no other explanation identified in 9.2%).
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Fentanyl is standard prehospital analgesia across Australian services, and a national registry like this speaks directly to how crews dose paediatric pain relief.

Learning goal

Refine my assessment and monitoring of prehospital opioid analgesia in paediatric patients to reduce under-treatment of pain.

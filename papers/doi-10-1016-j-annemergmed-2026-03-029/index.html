IV magnesium adds little for ED headache

Intravenous Magnesium Sulfate for Acute Nontraumatic Headache in the Emergency Department
Annals of Emergency Medicine · Added 25 August 2026

RCT of 1,028 ED patients with acute nontraumatic headache found IV magnesium sulfate plus paracetamol achieved more frequent treatment success (78.9% vs 65.1%) but pain score differences were below clinical importance thresholds, with less rescue analgesia needed but more mild side effects.

Study type
RCT
Design
Randomised double-blind placebo-controlled trial
Participants
1,028 adults with acute nontraumatic headache
Main finding
MgSO4 plus paracetamol: treatment success 78.9% vs 65.1% (difference 13.8%, 95% CI 8–19), but pain score differences below 1.3-point clinical importance threshold; less rescue analgesia (7.1% vs 15.3%) and more adverse events (15.4% vs 11.1%)
Caveat
Benefits below accepted thresholds for clinical importance; ED-based, single-centre
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). ED-based analgesia trial — magnesium showed statistical but not clinically meaningful benefit for prehospital use

Learning goal

Weigh the clinical importance of intravenous magnesium as adjunct analgesia for acute nontraumatic headache in my practice.

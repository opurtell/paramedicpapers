Smartwatch cardiac alerts feasible but poorly worn

Operational Feasibility of a Smartwatch-Based Photoplethysmography Alert System Integrated with Emergency Medical Services
Acta Cardiologica Sinica · 2026-11 · Added 28 November 2026

A Taiwanese feasibility study of 96 participants (75 high-risk cardiovascular patients) tested smartwatch photoplethysmography alerts triggering EMS dispatch or case-manager follow-up, with 3 of 16 critical alerts leading to EMS dispatch and median wearing time of only 0.7 days in the high-risk group.

Study type
Other
Design
Prospective feasibility study with cloud-based EMS dispatch and case-manager workflow
Participants
96 participants (75 high-risk cardiovascular patients)
Main finding
All 3 EMS activations came from the high-risk group; 3 of 16 critical alerts (18.8%) led to EMS dispatch and 13 led to expedited outpatient review; median wearing time 0.7 days vs 4.6 days in controls
Caveat
Tiny sample, very poor adherence, no systematic ECG confirmation and no confirmed EMS-treated arrhythmias.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Wearable-triggered EMS activation is not part of Australian dispatch, so this is a look at where consumer devices and ambulance call-taking may head rather than current practice.

Learning goal

Understand how wearable cardiac alert systems integrate with EMS dispatch and the barriers to patient adherence.

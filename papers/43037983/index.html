Most fatal brain bleeds die before neurosurgery

Sudden Deaths From Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage and Proximity to Neurosurgical Tertiary Care
JAMA Network Open · 2026-11-02 · Added 18 November 2026

A Finnish–New Zealand population cohort of 12,912 patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage found 70.5% of 30-day deaths occurred without neurosurgical unit admission, with higher proportions in districts farther from a neurosurgical centre.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective nationwide cohort from two administrative data collections
Participants
12,912 patients with aneurysmal SAH across 41 hospital districts in Finland and New Zealand
Main finding
3,819 of 5,418 SAH deaths (70.5%) occurred without neurosurgical unit admission — 70.7% in Finland, 70.1% in New Zealand — and the proportion rose with distance from a neurosurgical centre (age- and sex-adjusted RR 1.07 close-distance, higher farther out)
Caveat
Observational administrative data, so distance itself is associated with, not proven to cause, death without admission
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Registry epidemiology from Finland and New Zealand — most SAH deaths happen before neurosurgical care ever starts, which speaks to prehospital recognition and destination decisions rather than any treatment paramedics deliver.

Learning goal

Strengthen my recognition of sudden deterioration suggestive of aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage in rural and remote patients.

Only a third of arrests get bystander CPR

Global Rate of Bystander Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation in Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Heart, Lung & Circulation · 2026-10-06 · Added 8 October 2026

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 229 studies quantifying global bystander CPR rates in OHCA — just under a third of patients get bystander CPR, with arrests at home 28% less likely to receive it than public-location arrests.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis
Participants
229 studies, 4,421,570 OHCA patients across 45 countries
Main finding
Pooled bystander CPR prevalence was 32.1% (95% CI 29.7-34.5%), higher in high-income countries (34.9% vs 13.9%) and for paediatric arrests (49.2% vs 31.4%), with residential arrests 28% less likely to receive CPR than public ones (RR 0.72)
Caveat
Extreme heterogeneity (I² = 99.9%) — the pooled figure smooths over wildly different systems, registries and definitions
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Bystander CPR rates are the ceiling on outcomes for every Australian chain of survival, and the home-arrest gap shown here is the same gap community resus programs target.

Learning goal

Understand the system and community factors that limit bystander CPR before ambulance arrival, especially for residential arrests.

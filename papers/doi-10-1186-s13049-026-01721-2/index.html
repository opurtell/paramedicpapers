Simulated EMS care held up at altitude

Quality of prehospital emergency care at high altitude (3,450 m) by non-acclimatised EMS physicians: a randomised assessor-blinded simulation crossover study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-10-30 · Added 31 October 2026

In a Swiss randomised crossover simulation study, 20 non-acclimatised EMS physicians showed no significant drop in overall performance at 3,450 m altitude (composite 82% at baseline vs 74% acute exposure, p=0.082).

Study type
Simulation
Design
Assessor-blinded randomised crossover simulation study (physician-staffed crews)
Participants
20 non-acclimatised EMS physicians (17 analysed)
Main finding
Median composite performance 82% at baseline, 74% during acute exposure to 3,450 m, 82% with supplemental oxygen at 4 L/min, and 83% after 20 hours at altitude; overall difference not significant (p=0.082)
Caveat
Simulation only, 17 of 20 physicians completed all conditions, and the study crewed doctors rather than paramedics
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A physician-staffed Swiss model, but hypobaric hypoxia affects any crew working helicopter rescue or alpine retrieval, which parts of Australia do.

Learning goal

Recognise how acute altitude exposure affects my own clinical performance and when supplemental oxygen protects decision-making.

Prehospital stroke triage: high under- and overtriage rates

Performance of the Lausanne Ischemic Stroke Pathway prehospital triage system in identifying acute reperfusion candidates: a quality-control study
Swiss Medical Weekly · 2026-10-29 · Added 3 November 2026

A Swiss single-centre quality-control study of a prehospital stroke triage pathway found EMS undertriaged 21% and overtriaged 63% of acute reperfusion candidates, with both rates falling when a coordinated medical team (including a neurologist) was phone-consulted.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective single-centre quality-control study
Participants
1,071 patients with suspected acute ischaemic stroke
Main finding
41% were eligible for reperfusion; EMS undertriage 21%, overtriage 63%; the physician-led call-team step reduced undertriage to 11% and overtriage to 57%
Caveat
Physician-staffed call-team model is not how Australian dispatch works, and single-centre retrospective design
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The second-assessment step rests on a physician-staffed call team Australian services don't run, but the under- and overtriage rates are a useful benchmark for our own stroke screens.

Learning goal

Refine my prehospital stroke assessment to better identify patients suitable for acute reperfusion and reduce undertriage.

  • Why rural stroke care lags: system drivers

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 25 Oct 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    A qualitative study of 11 stakeholders in coastal eastern England found rural and coastal distance becomes inequity-producing when combined with limited mobility, complex extrication and Category 2 demand, shaping delays in stroke recognition, pre-alerting and transfer to stroke-capable care.

  • Scene-to-door time and stroke thrombectomy

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 25 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre Japanese cohort of 121 stroke thrombectomy patients transported directly from scene found scene-to-door time was only associated with outcome in patients with pre-stroke disability, where longer intervals were linked to lower odds of a favourable 90-day outcome (aOR 0.91; ROC cut-off 14 minutes in that subgroup).

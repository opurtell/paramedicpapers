Paramedics feel weakest in neonatal and paediatric mental health

Finnish Paramedics' Self-Assessed Competence in Paediatric and Neonatal Care: A Nationwide Survey of Education, Training and Development Needs
Acta Paediatrica (Oslo, Norway · 2026-10-08 · Added 10 October 2026

A survey of 253 Finnish paramedic graduates found moderate self-rated paediatric competence (mean 2.92/5) but low neonatal competence (2.04), with paediatric mental health emergencies rated lowest (1.30) and limited simulation or placement opportunities reported.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional survey
Participants
253 paramedics
Main finding
Self-rated competence 2.92 (paediatric) and 2.04 (neonatal); paediatric behavioural and mental health emergencies lowest at 1.30; simulation and clinical placement opportunities limited, especially in neonatal care
Caveat
Self-assessed competence in a single country's graduates, recruited via social media
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A different education system, but the pattern — minimal paediatric and neonatal case volume, weakest in mental health presentations — will feel familiar to educators here.

Learning goal

Understand how limited paediatric and neonatal clinical exposure shapes my competence in behavioural and mental health emergencies.

Stroke triage app adopted but not embedded

Implementation and Evaluation of the Stroke Triage App Among Paramedics in Prehospital Emergency Care: Sequential Mixed Methods Study
Journal of Medical Internet Research · 2026-11-03 · Added 6 November 2026

A Dutch mixed-methods evaluation of the Stroke Triage App across two EMS regions found good initial adoption (median 120 app triages/month) but inconsistent routine integration, with workflow fit and limited perceived added value the main barriers.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Sequential mixed-methods implementation evaluation (usage data, survey, interviews)
Participants
246 paramedics with app accounts; 76 surveyed, 9 interviewed
Main finding
Median 120 triages/month (IQR 107–131) and median 7 uses per paramedic (IQR 3–13); the app was well accepted and supported structured neurological assessment, but clinical urgency, poor workflow integration and limited perceived added value hampered routine use.
Caveat
Only 31% of account holders responded, and usage data can't show whether triage advice changed decisions.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). A Dutch paramedic service, but the adoption-versus-integration gap for clinical decision-support apps is the same one Australian services face.

Learning goal

Understand how digital decision support tools fit into prehospital stroke workflows and where barriers to routine use arise.

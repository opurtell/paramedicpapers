Co-design of urine culture stewardship in ED

Co‐Design of a Theory‐Informed, Multicomponent, Intervention for Diagnostic Stewardship in a Major‐Referral Emergency Department: A Qualitative Descriptive Study
Emergency Medicine Australasia · Added 29 September 2026

Qualitative co-design study at Townsville ED (7 workshops, 35 emergency physicians) building a theory-informed intervention against low-value urine cultures — culture, complexity and efficiency drove over-ordering, with education, nudges, clinical champions and audit/feedback chosen as the fix.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative descriptive study using co-design workshops
Participants
35 emergency physicians across 7 workshops
Main finding
Culture, complexity and efficiency drove low-value urine cultures; education, nudges, clinical champions and audit/feedback were selected as intervention components, with workflow inefficiency and credibility of change seen as challenges.
Caveat
Single ED, qualitative design; no effect or outcome data.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — ED pathology ordering rather than prehospital practice; the transferable part is the co-design-plus-behavioural-theory method for ambulance QI work

Learning goal

Understand how workplace culture and workflow pressures drive low-value pathology ordering in my practice.

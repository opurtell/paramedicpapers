Kidney biomarkers predict outcomes after OHCA

Prognostic performance of plasma and urinary biomarkers of kidney injury and function after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest
Resuscitation · Added 25 August 2026

Prospective study of 82 comatose OHCA patients found kidney-specific biomarkers (plasma and urinary NGAL and KIM-1, cystatin C) were superior to serum creatinine for early AKI detection and prediction of poor neurological outcomes at discharge.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective observational biomarker study
Participants
82 comatose adults with OHCA
Main finding
Kidney-specific biomarkers (plasma and urinary NGAL, KIM-1, cystatin C) outperformed serum creatinine for early AKI detection and predicting poor neurological outcome; AKI in 65.9%, CPC 3–5 in 54.9%
Caveat
Small single-cohort observational study; biomarkers measured post-ROSC, limited relevance to prehospital care
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Hospital-based biomarker research — relevant for post-arrest care but not prehospital decision-making

Learning goal

Build my understanding of how kidney injury after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest influences post-ROSC care and prognostic conversations.

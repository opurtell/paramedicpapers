No benefit from prophylaxis for opioid nausea

Prophylaxis of opioid-induced nausea in helicopter emergency medical service: a prospective quasi-experimental study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 7 May 2026

Prospective study of 205 HEMS patients comparing ondansetron prophylaxis, aromatherapy, and no prophylaxis for opioid-induced nausea — neither intervention showed benefit compared to no prophylaxis in unselected patients.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective quasi-experimental rotating-schedule study across six HEMS bases
Participants
205 patients >16 given opioids for traumatic pain (86 no prophylaxis, 64 ondansetron, 55 aromatherapy)
Main finding
Median NRS change 0 in all groups; NRS increase in 15%, 14%, 15%; NRS>3 in 14% vs 11% vs 11%; vomiting 4%, 2%, 0%. Neither ondansetron nor aromatherapy showed benefit over no prophylaxis.
Caveat
Quasi-experimental rotating allocation, single HEMS system, modest sample size
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Learning goal

Weigh whether routine antiemetic prophylaxis is warranted when giving opioids for traumatic pain in the prehospital setting.

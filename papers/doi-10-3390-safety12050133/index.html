Child visitor injuries in Queensland

Injury Ambulance Attendances Among Child Visitors to Queensland, Australia: A Descriptive Study, 2009–2018
Safety · Added 13 October 2026

Descriptive study of 3,802 Queensland Ambulance Service attendances for injuries in child visitors aged ≤15 (2009–18) found interstate visitors accounted for 85.8% of cases, with falls and transport incidents the leading mechanisms and burns third in 0–4-year-olds.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective descriptive geospatial analysis of ambulance records
Participants
3,802 ambulance attendances, non-Queensland residents ≤15 years, 2009–2018
Main finding
Interstate visitors 85.8% of cases; falls and transport incidents leading mechanisms; burns third-leading mechanism for interstate visitors aged 0–4; coastal locations most common
Caveat
Purely descriptive with a decade-old ending date (2018) — pick-up location used as a proxy for injury location
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian ambulance data, but descriptive tourism-injury epidemiology that informs prevention messaging rather than clinical practice.

Learning goal

Understand how travel and unfamiliar environments shape paediatric injury patterns at tourist destinations.

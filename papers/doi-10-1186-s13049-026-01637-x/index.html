Prehospital physicians underconfident in paediatrics

Challenges faced by prehospital emergency physicians providing emergency care to pediatric patients
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 31 May 2026

They surveyed emergency physicians and analysed 9,409 protocols to understand pediatric emergency preparedness, and found pediatric cases represent only 6.5% of prehospital physician deployments but physicians reported low confidence in neonatal care, intoxication, and pediatric polytrauma.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Mixed-methods: retrospective protocol analysis plus physician survey
Participants
9,409 prehospital physician protocols (606 paediatric cases) and 437 surveyed physicians
Main finding
Paediatric cases were 6.45% of missions; high confidence for seizures, anaphylaxis and hypoglycaemia but markedly lower confidence for neonatal care, paediatric polytrauma and CPR; >70% supported mandatory simulation-based paediatric training.
Caveat
Self-perceived competence (not measured performance) and physician-staffed system not directly comparable to Australian paramedic practice
Bottom line
Context only

Learning goal

Build my confidence in managing low-frequency paediatric emergencies such as neonatal resuscitation and polytrauma.

ECPR timing in refractory OHCA: survival gap persists

Earlier Versus Later Extracorporeal Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation in Refractory Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Nationwide Sequential Landmark Target Trial Emulation
European Heart Journal. Acute Cardiovascular Care · 2026-10-25 · Added 26 October 2026

A target trial emulation in 58,668 patients with refractory OHCA from the Korean nationwide registry found ECPR was associated with higher 30-day survival at 15, 30 and 60 minutes after ED arrival (+15.0, +14.4 and +7.7 percentage points); an exploratory analysis found the benefit narrowed but remained significant to 120 minutes.

Study type
Registry
Design
Nationwide registry target trial emulation (sequential landmark)
Participants
58,668 patients with refractory OHCA (909 received ECPR)
Main finding
Weighted 30-day survival with ECPR vs conventional CPR was 31.0% vs 16.0%, 21.6% vs 7.2% and 10.9% vs 3.2% at 15, 30 and 60 minutes after ED arrival; an exploratory analysis found benefit narrowed but stayed significant to 120 minutes
Caveat
Propensity-adjusted observational design, not randomised — residual selection bias about who gets ECMO is likely
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). ECPR is an in-hospital tool — Australian crews don't deliver it — but the refractory-arrest transport and destination decisions that get a patient to an ECMO centre are ours, and the finding questions the 60-minute cut-off many systems apply.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and uncertainties of ECPR for refractory cardiac arrest beyond the conventional sixty-minute window.

  • Hot nights linked to more OHCA

    A nationwide time-stratified case-crossover study of 135,898 emergency hospitalisations for OHCA in Japan found hot nights (minimum temperature above the regional 92.5th percentile) were associated with a 9% higher OHCA hospitalisation risk, with the highest risks in people aged 75+ and women in northern regions.

  • Dispatcher training + volunteer app: more CPR, no outcome gain

    The Lancet. Public Health · 22 Oct 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial in French EMS agencies found dispatcher training in arrest recognition, telephone CPR and a geolocated volunteer responder app was associated with more bystander CPR (61.4% to 70.9%, adjusted OR 1.54), an effect that lost significance after adjusting for secular trends; clinical outcomes did not improve.

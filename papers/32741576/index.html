Mechanical vs manual CPR shows no survival difference

The Effect of Lights and Sirens on Critical Care Transport Time
The Journal of Emergency Medicine · Added 23 April 2026

They measured whether lights and sirens meaningfully shorten critical care transport, and found a statistically significant but modest time saving of about 8 minutes.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort of ED CPR following prehospital-initiated resuscitation
Participants
818 non-traumatic OHCA patients (345 manual, 473 mechanical CPR)
Main finding
No significant difference in 1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month mortality (12-month p=0.034); favourable neurological outcome (mRS ≤3 at 1 year) 3.5% vs 4.0% (p=0.501)
Caveat
Retrospective design in the ED setting; findings may not apply to prehospital compression choice
Bottom line
Context only

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and risks of lights and sirens transport for patients already receiving prehospital resuscitation.

Longer on-scene intervals linked to 24-hour mortality

Association between On-Scene Interval and 24-Hour Mortality among Code Red Emergency Medical Services Patients: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Archives of Academic Emergency Medicine · 2026-09-10 · Added 28 October 2026

A Thai retrospective cohort of Code Red ALS activations found on-scene intervals of 21-25 minutes were associated with higher odds of 24-hour mortality compared with 5 minutes or less (adjusted OR 5.61), and intervals of 20 minutes or longer came with 321.7 fewer expected survival minutes.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort, ALS dispatch, restricted cubic splines
Participants
2,321 Code Red EMS activations, Saraburi Province
Main finding
On-scene interval of 21-25 minutes was associated with higher odds of 24-hour mortality versus 5 minutes or less (aOR 5.61, 95% CI 1.58-19.97); non-survivors had a longer median on-scene interval (12 vs 9 minutes)
Caveat
Prolonged scene time may reflect necessary on-scene care in sicker patients, and spline estimates at longer intervals were imprecise
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Scene-time benchmarks are an operations question for any ambulance service, but a Thai physician-supported ALS dispatch model and the observational design mean sicker patients likely drive some of the long scene times.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on which on-scene interventions justify extended scene time in critically unwell patients.

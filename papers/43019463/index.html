Advanced airway not linked to traumatic arrest survival

Prehospital Advanced Airway Management in Traumatic Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Nationwide Cohort Study Using Time-Dependent Propensity Score Sequential Matching
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-12 · Added 14 November 2026

A Japanese nationwide registry cohort of 7,217 adults with traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest found successful prehospital advanced airway management was not significantly associated with better 30-day survival or neurological outcome when resuscitation-time bias was corrected.

Study type
Registry
Design
Nationwide retrospective registry cohort with time-dependent propensity score sequential matching
Participants
7,217 adults with traumatic OHCA (2,653 received advanced airway management)
Main finding
Thirty-day survival 1.8% with advanced airway vs 1.4% matched controls (corrected RD 0.6%, 95% CI −0.1 to 1.2); favourable neurological outcome 0.2% vs 0.4% (corrected RD −0.2%, 95% CI −0.6 to 0.1) — no significant timing-dependent association
Caveat
Observational design with extremely low event rates, so even a real effect could hide in the confidence intervals
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Supraglottic airways are standard Australian cardiac arrest care and intubation is an advanced-tier skill in most services, so the question is squarely ours; the physician-free Japanese EMS model resembles ours more than Europe's.

Learning goal

Weigh the value of advanced airway management during traumatic cardiac arrest against prioritising early ventilation and circulation.

  • EtCO2 trends predict ROSC cycle by cycle

    Resuscitation · 14 Nov 2026 · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    A Taiwanese retrospective multicentre study of 1,480 patients with OHCA built a two-layer LSTM model on sequential EtCO2 that predicted ROSC well at event level (AUROC 0.876) but only 0.685 per two-minute cycle.

  • Higher admission PaCO2 linked to worse post-arrest survival

    Journal of Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Chinese single-centre retrospective cohort of 617 adults resuscitated from OHCA found admission PaCO2 above 50 mmHg associated with higher one-month mortality in a stepwise gradient (each 10-mmHg increase aOR 1.45, 95% CI 1.24–1.70).

  • Night-time OHCA outcomes linked to bystander availability

    Journal of Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026 · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    A Czech-led scoping review of 18 studies found OHCA and STEMI incidence peaks in the morning with a secondary afternoon/evening peak, and night-time events were linked to lower ROSC and worse one-month and one-year survival, explained by limited bystander availability, less bystander CPR and defibrillation and longer times to hospital intervention rather than time of day itself.

  • NSW data linkage tracks sudden cardiac arrest

    Heart Rhythm · 13 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    An Australian data-linkage study identified 13,345 presumed sudden cardiac arrests aged 1–50 in NSW from 1.7 million linked records — 54% cardiac in aetiology and 83% fatal — and extrapolated roughly 370 cardiac SCA deaths a year among Australians aged 1–35.

Unwitnessed arrests: mostly women, mostly at home

Determinants of unwitnessed out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review (HL-D-26-00618)
Heart & Lung · 2026-10-08 · Added 10 October 2026

A systematic review of 22 studies (210,028 people) found female sex and arrest at home were the most consistent determinants of unwitnessed OHCA, with weaker, mostly unadjusted evidence for night or sleep arrests, deprivation and psychiatric illness.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review
Participants
22 studies, 210,028 adults
Main finding
Female sex and home location most consistently associated with unwitnessed arrest; limited evidence for arrests at night or during sleep, low socioeconomic status, type I diabetes and psychiatric illness
Caveat
Much of the included evidence came from unadjusted analyses with heterogeneous definitions of witnessed status
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). The determinants are population and household factors, not features of a particular EMS system, so the pattern travels directly to Australian communities.

Learning goal

Recognise which patients are most likely to sustain an unwitnessed cardiac arrest and how this shapes response priorities.

    Resuscitation Plus · 9 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 322,943 bystander-witnessed OHCA patients aged ≥65 in the All-Japan Utstein Registry (2005–22) found bystander CPR rose over time but stalled near 50%, and 1-month favourable neurological outcome stayed around 1% in those aged ≥90 despite CPR rates near 80%.

    A single-centre retrospective cohort of 136 OHCA patients found CPR-related injuries in 43% (16.9% clinically significant), with CPR duration over 20 minutes — not ECPR or mechanical CPR — independently associated with injury (OR 2.45).

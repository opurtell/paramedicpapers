Extrication after road collisions: an evidence map

Rescue and extrication characteristics associated with morbidity and mortality following road traffic collisions: a scoping review
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-18 · Added 19 November 2026

An international scoping review mapped the rescue and extrication literature after road traffic collisions and found fragmented definitions, inconsistent time intervals and heterogeneous outcomes, with no clear effectiveness evidence for any extrication approach.

Main finding
Mapped rescue characteristics studied against morbidity, mortality and physiological deterioration; found inconsistent definitions of entrapment, extrication method and time, and heterogenous outcome reporting limiting synthesis and system-level learning.
Caveat
A scoping review maps the evidence without evaluating whether any extrication approach works.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Extrication decisions are made on Australian roads every shift alongside fire and rescue crews; the review's finding that the evidence base can't settle self-extrication vs rapid extrication is directly relevant to those joint decisions.

Learning goal

Understand how inconsistent definitions of entrapment and extrication affect patient outcomes and system-level learning.

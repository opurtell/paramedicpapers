Whole blood in paediatric trauma: early program data

Prehospital Whole Blood Resuscitation in Pediatric Patients: A San Antonio Cohort Descriptive
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-10 · Added 12 November 2026

A US retrospective cohort from San Antonio Fire Department's mature prehospital whole-blood program described 73 paediatric recipients (4.3% of 1,703 transfusions, 2018–2025): mostly adolescent males with penetrating trauma, 45% with an elevated age-adjusted shock index and 50% surviving to discharge.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective observational cohort
Participants
73 paediatric whole-blood recipients (of 1,703 total transfusions)
Main finding
Trauma caused 96% of paediatric transfusions (74% gunshot, 84% all penetrating); 45% had elevated age-adjusted SIPA, 20% were pulseless; survival 50%, died 39%, unknown 11%; younger children showed the largest drop in shock index after resuscitation
Caveat
Descriptive single-program cohort with no comparator, and 11% of outcomes unknown
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is limited to a few Australian services, so this informs the debate more than current practice — but TXA and intraosseous access are standard, and paediatric trauma physiology is universal.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on recognising paediatric haemorrhagic shock and when prehospital blood transfusion is indicated.

    A US national registry study (CARES, 2013–2023) developed and internally validated paediatric OHCA risk scores from prehospital variables, classifying 26,895 children into low/moderate/high survival groups with AUROC 0.76–0.82 and average discharge-survival probabilities from 3% to 48%.

  • Paediatric prehospital fentanyl: serious adverse effects rare

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 7 Nov 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    A Danish register study of 1,531 children given prehospital opioids (99.7% fentanyl) found 15.1% had at least one clinical sign compatible with opioid adverse effects, serious signs were uncommon (bradycardia and respiratory depression 0.7% each), and 63.6% were still in pain at hospital arrival.

  • Paediatric transport ventilation: nCPAP first, transfers dominate

    South African Medical Journal · 3 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    A South African retrospective review of 1,548 ventilated under-fives in Western Cape EMS found 99.4% were interfacility transfers, mostly for respiratory distress, with non-invasive support dominating (nCPAP 66.5%, high-flow 14.5%) over invasive ventilation (18.9%).

  • Adrenaline timing not tied to paediatric OHCA survival

    Pediatric Critical Care Medicine · 31 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese national registry study of 259 witnessed paediatric out-of-hospital cardiac arrests given adrenaline, using time-dependent propensity matching to address resuscitation-time bias, found no adrenaline timing window was associated with better one-month survival or neurological outcome.

Fibrinogen concentrate supply grows in Queensland

Supply of Fibrinogen Concentrate Across Queensland Health
Emergency Medicine Australasia · 2026-11-09 · Added 11 November 2026

A ten-year Queensland Health supply analysis of 24,717 g of fibrinogen concentrate found 97% went to 54 hospitals and only 1% to ambulance services, while population-adjusted supply rose from 8.19 to 59.2 g per 100,000 between 2016 and 2025.

Study type
Other
Design
Retrospective statewide supply analysis
Participants
24,717 g of fibrinogen concentrate supplied across Queensland, 2016–2026
Main finding
97.0% of supply went to 54 hospitals, 2.0% to retrieval services and 1.0% to ambulance services; 66.2% went to major trauma services and 92.9% to viscoelastic-assay-capable hospitals; population-adjusted supply rose from 8.19 to 59.2 g per 100,000 (2016–2025)
Caveat
Supply data only — no patient outcomes, no link to bleeding control or mortality
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Fibrinogen concentrate isn't carried by Australian ambulances, so this is supply-chain context for the bleeding debate rather than current practice

Learning goal

Understand the supply pathways and access barriers that shape prehospital availability of fibrinogen concentrate in Australia.

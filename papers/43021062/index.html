3% of callers drive a fifth of all calls

Frequent Callers to Emergency Medical Services: an observational cohort study from Region Zealand, Denmark (2018-2025)
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-08-17 · Added 14 November 2026

A Danish observational cohort of 443,977 emergency calls found 3.0% of callers (6,415 people) generated 19.9% of all calls, with post-threshold frequent callers seeing more telephone resolution (23.8%) and non-conveyance (27.4%).

Study type
Cohort
Design
Observational cohort of emergency medical dispatch centre calls, 2018–2025
Participants
214,091 individuals making 443,977 calls in Region Zealand, Denmark
Main finding
Frequent callers (5+ calls in 12 months) were 3.0% of callers but 19.9% of calls; alcohol/intoxication/overdose and suicidal presentations were top contact causes only in the highest-volume post-threshold groups
Caveat
Single-region data and a dispatch-centre definition of frequent calling that may not match other systems' operational definitions
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Frequent-caller patterns and dispatch load are universal ambulance problems, and the Danish single-tier system maps well onto Australian operations.

Learning goal

Understand the system factors that drive frequent calling to ambulance services and how coordinated responses reduce demand.

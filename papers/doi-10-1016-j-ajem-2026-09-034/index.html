Calcium with prehospital blood products for trauma

Calcium during prehospital trauma blood-product resuscitation: A meta-analysis
The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · Added 25 September 2026

Meta-analysis of calcium supplementation during prehospital blood-product trauma resuscitation. Abstract not yet available (ahead-of-print).

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of nonrandomised comparative and prevalence studies
Participants
17 studies of adult trauma patients
Main finding
Calcium-exposed patients had lower hypocalcaemia (military cohort RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.27–0.61; civilian air-transport RR 0.51, 95% CI 0.32–0.82; exploratory pooled RR 0.45, 95% CI 0.33–0.61); hypocalcaemia was frequent after prehospital blood-product resuscitation
Caveat
Only two clinically dissimilar nonrandomised studies; threshold variation, uncertain sampling relative to in-hospital treatment and residual confounding preclude causal inference
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). Title-only rating, and calcium's relevance here sits with blood-product resuscitation — blood products are not in the extracted ACTAS formulary, so adoption would need a prehospital blood program.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and limitations of routine calcium supplementation during prehospital blood-product trauma resuscitation.

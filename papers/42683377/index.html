Paralysing post-ROSC patients prehospital

Advanced Paramedic Paralysis in ROSC (APPiR) study
British Paramedic Journal · 2026-09-01 · Added 29 August 2026

UK cohort of advanced paramedics in critical care giving neuromuscular blockade to post-ROSC patients poorly tolerating the tube — 99 patients over a year, all PGD indications met, with no adverse physiological trends in sats, end-tidal CO2, MAP or heart rate.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort study
Participants
99 patients (85.9% male, mean age 57.6)
Main finding
All PGD indications were met; median time to NMB was 71 minutes from OHCA and 40 minutes post ROSC; all intubations within two attempts; no statistically significant adverse physiological trends.
Caveat
Small single-service descriptive cohort with no control group — safety signals are reassuring but say nothing about effect on outcomes.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Drug-assisted intubation and post-ROSC ventilatory control are intensive care paramedic skills in most Australian services rather than routine paramedic practice.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and risks of prehospital neuromuscular blockade for post-ROSC patients before transport.

    Resuscitation · 3 Sep 2026 · Registry · Context only

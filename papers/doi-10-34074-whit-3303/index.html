NZ paramedics breached patient rights more often than nurses

Patient Rights Breaches by Aotearoa New Zealand Paramedics and Nurses: An Exploratory Study
Whitireia Journal of Nursing, Health and Social Services · 2026 · Added 1 November 2026

A New Zealand descriptive analysis of Health and Disability Commissioner decisions from 2014–2024 found nine paramedic and 100 nurse Code of Rights breaches, but per 1,000 practitioners paramedics were three times more likely to breach than nurses and at higher severity, with Right 4 (appropriate standard of care) most common in both professions.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Descriptive statistical analysis of published regulatory breach decisions
Participants
109 breach decisions (9 paramedics, 100 nurses), NZ, 2014–2024
Main finding
Per 1,000 practitioners, paramedics were three times more likely than nurses to breach the Code and breached at a higher severity rate; Right 4 was the most prevalent breach in both professions
Caveat
Small paramedic numbers make the per-1,000 comparison fragile, and published decisions capture only reported and upheld breaches in one country
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). An Aotearoa NZ paramedic workforce studied under a statutory complaints framework closely mirrors Australian regulation, and error-reporting culture is a live issue across Australian services too.

Learning goal

Understand the professional conduct risks that lead paramedics to breach patients' rights to appropriate standards of care.

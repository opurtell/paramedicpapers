TBI in first responders needs occupational pathways

Traumatic Brain Injury in First Responders: From Hidden Burden to Occupational Health Action
The Journal of Head Trauma Rehabilitation · 2026-10-20 · Added 21 October 2026

Introduction to a themed issue on TBI in first responders, finding a substantial but underrecognised burden with no consistent pathways for recognition, rehabilitation or return to duty, and presenting a 28-recommendation multidisciplinary consensus statement.

Study type
Guideline / statement
Design
Consensus statement / thematic review
Participants
28 consensus recommendations across a multidisciplinary group
Main finding
Substantial TBI burden in law enforcement, fire and EMS with persistent gaps in diagnosis, rehabilitation and duty-readiness; 28 recommendations spanning recognition, early assessment, return to duty and organisational policy
Caveat
Narrative synthesis of a themed issue — the authors concede first responder-specific evidence remains limited
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Return-to-duty and occupational health structures differ across Australian services, but the underrecognition of frontline TBI and its overlap with mental health is just as live a gap here.

Learning goal

Understand how occupational traumatic brain injury in paramedics affects recognition, return to duty and organisational support.

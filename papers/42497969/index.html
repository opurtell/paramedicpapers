Prehospital magnesium before thrombolysis: no overall benefit

Prehospital neuroprotectant in combination with post-arrival thrombolytic therapy for acute ischemic stroke. Secondary analysis of the FAST-MAG prehospital trial
Journal of Stroke and Cerebrovascular Diseases · Added 31 July 2026

Secondary analysis testing whether prehospital magnesium sulfate followed by thrombolysis improves 90-day outcomes — feasible and safe but no significant overall clinical benefit, though severe stroke patients (NIHSS >15) showed better outcomes in hypothesis-generating subgroup analysis.

Study type
RCT
Design
Secondary analysis of the FAST-MAG phase 3 RCT
Participants
401 patients receiving thrombolysis (214 magnesium, 187 placebo)
Main finding
No significant overall 90-day mRS benefit from prehospital magnesium plus thrombolysis; in severe strokes (NIHSS >15) the magnesium group had higher rates of favourable 90-day outcome (mRS 0-2); median last-known-well to prehospital infusion was 46 min and 93 min pre-thrombolysis exposure
Caveat
Secondary/subgroup analysis of a null trial; subgroup findings hypothesis-generating
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Learning goal

Weigh the role of prehospital neuroprotective therapy in the early management of acute ischaemic stroke.

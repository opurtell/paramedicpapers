Real-time BVM feedback linked to better arrest ventilation

Ventilation Monitoring in Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest Care
Resuscitation · 2026-10 · Added 31 October 2026

A US prospective before/after study in Milwaukee found real-time BVM feedback (Real BVM Help) was associated with the share of OHCA ventilations meeting both rate and tidal-volume targets rising from 7.5% to 39.0%.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective before/after quality assurance study
Participants
278 adults with OHCA (148 before / 130 after)
Main finding
Ventilations in-target for both rate and tidal volume rose from 7.5% to 39.0% with real-time feedback; with ETCO2 monitoring alone only 6.8% met both, and 58.2% of ventilations delivered a lower-than-target tidal volume
Caveat
Before/after design without randomisation, in a single US county system
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Bag-valve-mask ventilation and ETCO2 monitoring are core skills on every Australian ambulance, and most crews ventilate arrests with a BVM and little more than ETCO2 to go on.

Learning goal

Strengthen my ventilation technique during cardiac arrest resuscitation by using feedback to improve rate and tidal volume.

  • ECG signs appear minutes before post-ROSC rearrest

    Resuscitation · 31 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A US retrospective cohort of 1,298 post-ROSC OHCA patients found routine ECG features — wider QRS, lower heart rate, higher heart-rate variability — were associated with rearrest, with prediction best one minute before it (AUROC 0.74).

  • AED coverage depends on how responders travel

    Frontiers in Public Health · 30 Oct 2026 · Modelling · Context only

    A Chinese spatial optimisation study of 17,842 OHCA dispatch records in Wuhan modelled AED coverage under different responder travel modes and found demand-weighted coverage ranged from 85% (driving) to 32% (walking), with universal walking coverage needing 521 extra AEDs versus 68 by car.

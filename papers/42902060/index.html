POC troponin on ambulances: fewer ED visits

Cost-effectiveness of prehospital high-sensitivity troponin i testing versus standard emergency department care in patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome in France
Health Economics Review · 2026-07-25 · Added 22 October 2026

A French economic model built on 737 patients found prehospital point-of-care high-sensitivity troponin reduced ED attendance (90.9% vs 96.7%) and ED length of stay (1.63 vs 2.76 hours) versus standard care for suspected acute coronary syndrome.

Study type
Modelling
Design
Decision-analytic semi-Markov model
Participants
737 patients with suspected ACS from the French VALIDATE cohort
Main finding
ED attendance fell 5.8 percentage points (90.9% vs 96.7%, p<0.001) and mean ED stay dropped from 2.76 to 1.63 hours with prehospital hs-cTnI
Caveat
A 24-hour horizon that excluded inpatient, ICU and procedural costs, so only short-term economics were captured
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital troponin testing is not part of Australian ambulance practice, and this models a physician-dispatched French system, but it sketches what a POC-troponin pathway could buy if trialled here.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and limitations of prehospital point-of-care troponin testing in my assessment of suspected chest pain.

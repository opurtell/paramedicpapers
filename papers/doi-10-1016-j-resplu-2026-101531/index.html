Every minute counts: early bystander CPR

Decreased Early Bystander CPR Initiation Despite Stable Overall Rates: A Nationwide Time-to-Event Analysis During the COVID-19 Pandemic
Resuscitation Plus · 2026-10 · Added 17 October 2026

A time-to-event analysis of the All-Japan Utstein registry found overall bystander CPR rates stayed stable through the pandemic, but initiation within 10 minutes slipped — and earlier starts sharply improved neurological outcomes (aOR 3.27 for 0–3 min vs >10 min).

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective nationwide registry cohort with time-to-event analysis
Participants
56,082 layperson-witnessed non-traumatic OHCAs
Main finding
Overall bystander CPR was stable (58.7% vs 58.6%), but early initiation fell during the pandemic (aHR 0.96); CPR started at 0–3 min carried aOR 3.27 for favourable neurological outcome at 1 month versus >10 min.
Caveat
Pandemic-era comparison; the timing–outcome link is associative and residual confounding (dispatch, case mix) can't be excluded.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Bystander CPR timing sits at the front of every Australian chain of survival, and this is one of the largest OHCA registries anywhere, so the timing–outcome gradient travels directly.

Learning goal

Strengthen my dispatch coaching techniques so bystanders start CPR earlier in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

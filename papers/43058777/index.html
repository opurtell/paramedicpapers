Debriefing works, but nobody reports it the same way

Debriefing in prehospital simulation-based education: a scoping review of ambulance nurse and paramedic literature
BMC Nursing · 2026-08-27 · Added 21 November 2026

A scoping review of 18 studies (2005–2025) on debriefing after prehospital simulation for practising paramedics and ambulance nurses found debriefing was associated with better technical performance, communication and teamwork, but how it was run and reported varied widely.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Scoping review (Arksey and O'Malley framework, PRISMA-ScR)
Participants
18 studies from 974 records, mostly US and quantitative
Main finding
Guided debriefing in 15 studies and tool/technology-assisted in 3; debriefing associated with improvements in technical performance, communication, teamwork, clinical reasoning and reflective practice, with inconsistent reporting of facilitation, structure and outcomes
Caveat
Mostly self-reported educational outcomes, so debriefing's specific contribution can't be separated from simulation itself
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Simulation debriefing is standard in Australian services' training, so the gap this review names — inconsistent structure, facilitation and outcome reporting — is directly actionable for clinical education units here.

Learning goal

Strengthen my debriefing practice after simulation and clinical cases to improve teamwork and reflective judgement.

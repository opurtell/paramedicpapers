LVOT VTI unreliable for mild hypovolaemia

Changes in LVOT velocity–time integral during whole blood donation: implications for early detection of hypovolemia in spontaneously breathing patients
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 8 September 2026

Prospective study measuring LVOT VTI in 63 volunteers before and after donating about 6–7 mL/kg of blood — VTI fell 11.5% on average but with marked inter-individual variability, so it can't reliably flag mild hypovolaemia on its own.

Study type
Other
Design
Prospective physiological study of volunteers before and after blood donation
Participants
63 healthy adult volunteers donating ~6-7 mL/kg blood
Main finding
LVOT VTI fell 11.5% (95% CI -15.3 to -7.7) immediately after donation and remained 6.1% below baseline at 15 minutes; a ≥10% reduction occurred in only 50% (95% CI 37-63) of participants
Caveat
Healthy volunteers with controlled donation, not actual patients; marked inter-individual variability
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — prehospital ultrasound isn't in the ACTAS skills corpus

Learning goal

Weigh the value of point-of-care ultrasound measures like LVOT VTI when assessing suspected early hypovolaemia.

Street layout linked to EMS response times

Built environment and emergency medical services response time in a high-density megacity: multilevel evidence from urban-suburban
Frontiers in Public Health · 2026-10-30 · Added 16 November 2026

A Chinese multilevel regression of 2024 Beijing ambulance dispatch data found denser arterial and local road networks were associated with shorter EMS response times citywide, while denser residential development was linked to slower responses in the central city.

Not reported (2024 Beijing Emergency Center dispatch data)
Main finding
Lower residential facility density and denser arterial and local road networks were associated with shorter response times; high residential density was associated with slower responses in central urban areas, while local-road density mattered most in suburbs
Caveat
Single megacity with a very different urban form from Australian cities, and associations only — no causal claim
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A high-density megacity's built form differs from Australian cities, but the road-density-versus-residential-density signal is useful for response-time planning in our own growth corridors.

Learning goal

Understand how urban street layout and residential density shape ambulance response times in Australian cities.

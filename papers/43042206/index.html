Centralised call centres linked to leaner ambulance operations

The effects of common emergency call system applications in pre-hospital emergency medical services from a lean management perspective: The case of Ordu province, Turkiye
Pakistan Journal of Medical Sciences · 2026-10 · Added 18 November 2026

A retrospective before–after study of 2,346,774 emergency calls in Ordu province, Türkiye found a centralised emergency call centre was associated with a 49% drop in calls per incident and a 36.9% fall in ambulance response time.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective longitudinal before–after study framed on lean management principles
Participants
2,346,774 emergency calls and 721,897 cases over 84 months
Main finding
Calls per incident fell 49.0% (4.63 to 2.36), ambulance response time fell 36.9% and mobilisation time 23.7%; on-time arrival improved (urban 93.9% to 98.4%, rural 82.2% to 89.2%)
Caveat
Before–after design with no adjustment for secular trends or other system changes over eight years
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A centralised dispatch model in Türkiye — the operational question of call handling, mobilisation and response times is the same here, though a single province's case mix and geography limit direct comparison.

Learning goal

Understand how centralised call-taking and dispatch systems shape ambulance response times across urban and rural Australia.

Ambulance contamination hotspots: cuffs and sats probes

Evaluation of Surface Contamination in Prehospital Emergency Units: Identifying Hotspots and Hygiene Priorities
Clinical Epidemiology and Global Health · Added 27 October 2026

An Iranian cross-sectional study sampling 23 high-touch sites in ambulances (1,932 ATP tests) found the worst contamination on patient-contact kit — pulse oximeter and blood pressure cuff — and showed handwashing cut paramedic hand contamination nearly tenfold.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional ATP bioluminescence sampling
Participants
23 high-touch sites per vehicle, 1,932 total tests
Main finding
Pulse oximeter (>1,100 RLU) and blood pressure cuff exceeded the very-high contamination threshold; paramedic hand RLU fell from 349.79 to 34.79 after handwashing
Caveat
Single Iranian university-affiliated fleet; ATP RLU thresholds are proxies, not infection outcomes
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Vehicle cleaning between patients and hand hygiene are universal practice, and the hotspot equipment sits in every Australian ambulance — though this is one low-resource Iranian fleet.

Learning goal

Recognise which shared patient-contact equipment poses the highest infection risk and strengthen my hygiene practices between cases.

  • Prehospital qSOFA adds a little prognostic signal

    Journal of Clinical Medicine · 17 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Turkish retrospective cohort tested whether the prehospital qSOFA already recorded on the chart adds prognostic information for ambulance-transported suspected infection — it predicted 30-day mortality within every ED triage stratum but added almost nothing to a full arrival model (ΔAUC 0.007).

  • EMS sepsis suspicion speeds up ED antibiotics

    Wiener Klinische Wochenschrift · 15 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Single-centre Austrian cohort of 134 ED sepsis admissions found EMS documentation of sepsis suspicion was linked to antibiotics within 1 hour in 63.3% vs 19.2% of patients (aOR 5.77) and a median time-to-antibiotics of 32 vs 124.5 minutes.

  • Sepsis intubations: haemodynamic trouble common

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 9 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    A retrospective analysis of the ANZEDAR registry (79 hospitals, 2010–26) covering 280 sepsis intubations found haemodynamic complications in 27.9% and peri-intubation cardiac arrest in 4.6%, with less waveform capnography use than non-sepsis intubations.

Doppler beats pulse check after tourniquet

Comparison of Pulse Palpation and Paramedic-Performed Doppler Ultrasonography for Assessing Distal Circulation After Tourniquet Application: A Randomized Crossover Study
Healthcare · Added 13 October 2026

Randomised crossover study of 41 practising paramedics assessing distal circulation after tourniquet application found paramedic-performed Doppler ultrasonography beat pulse palpation on sensitivity (88.9% vs 66.7%) and accuracy (90.2% vs 78.0%) against an assessor-Doppler reference.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Prospective randomised crossover
Participants
41 actively practising paramedics, upper-extremity tourniquet application
Main finding
Pulse palpation sensitivity 66.7%, specificity 87.0%, accuracy 78.0%; paramedic Doppler sensitivity 88.9%, specificity 91.3%, accuracy 90.2%
Caveat
Laboratory-style setup on healthy volunteers with a simulated injury model — not bleeding patients in the field
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Tourniquets are standard kit Australia-wide, but handheld Doppler isn't routine prehospital equipment, so this describes a possible adjunct rather than current practice.

Learning goal

Weigh pulse palpation against handheld Doppler for confirming distal circulation after tourniquet application in trauma care.

AI voice assistants in EMS: survey

A multinational cross-sectional survey on the use of AI-based voice assistance systems in emergency medical services
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 16 September 2026

Multinational survey of 401 EMS clinicians in Germany, Norway and Switzerland exploring how AI-based voice assistance systems could be designed for prehospital care — respondents were technically proficient and already using digital tools mainly for documentation, knowledge access, hospital pre-notification and occupancy checks, with the study mapping requirements, expectations and implementation obstacles rather than testing clinical outcomes.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Multinational cross-sectional online survey (Germany, Norway, Switzerland)
Participants
401 EMS clinicians (587 responses, 186 excluded)
Main finding
Digital tools used mainly for documentation, knowledge access, hospital pre-notification and occupancy checks; positive attitude towards voice assistants in missions; desired features include translation, patient history summarisation and documentation; key barriers are staff acceptance, funding and data protection.
Caveat
Survey of attitudes and requirements only — no clinical outcomes tested; European respondents may not generalise to Australian EMS.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Acceptance/requirements survey only; no outcome data and no such system deployed in ACTAS, but it speaks directly to documentation and clinical-support workflows ACTAS shares.

Learning goal

Understand the barriers and enablers shaping how AI voice assistance could support Australian paramedic documentation and communication.

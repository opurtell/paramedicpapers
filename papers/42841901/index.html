Field fluids before ROSC in crush arrests

Pre-hospital fluid resuscitation and return of spontaneous circulation in earthquake victims with cardiac arrest during the first 24 hours after the 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes: Implications for disaster risk management
Ulusal Travma Ve Acil Cerrahi Dergisi = Turkish Journal of Trauma & Emergency Surgery · 2026-10 · Added 9 October 2026

A single-centre retrospective study of 37 earthquake victims in cardiac arrest after the 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes found ROSC in 27% — exclusively in the 25 patients who received pre-hospital fluids (mean ~1.5 L) — with better pH, lactate, and potassium on arrival.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Single-centre retrospective cohort
Participants
37 adults in arrest within 24 hours of the earthquakes
Main finding
ROSC in 10/37 (27.0%), all of whom had received pre-hospital fluids (mean 1,540 ± 611 mL; p=0.010); fluid recipients had higher pH and lower lactate, potassium, and base excess on arrival
Caveat
Tiny retrospective sample with major confounding by indication — the sickest patients may simply not have received fluids, so causality cannot be inferred
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Field fluids for suspected crush injury are within the scope of Australian paramedic practice, but earthquake mass-casualty arrests of this kind are rare here, so this informs disaster planning more than day-to-day work.

Learning goal

Weigh the role of early fluid resuscitation in traumatic cardiac arrest from crush injury in disaster settings.

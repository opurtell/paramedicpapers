Simulated prehospital eCPR shows large time savings

Quantifying Time Saved through Prehospital Extracorporeal Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation: A Simulation-Based Analysis in Real-World Cardiac Arrest Patients
Prehospital and Disaster Medicine · 2026 · Added 26 November 2026

Austrian simulation study comparing 23 simulated prehospital eCPR activations with 25 real in-hospital eCPR patients found the prehospital pathway reached the cannulation decision far sooner (18 vs 49 minutes from call), shortening estimated call-to-ECMO-flow time.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Prospective observational simulation study within a hospital-based eCPR programme
Participants
23 simulated prehospital cases vs 25 in-hospital eCPR patients
Main finding
Call-to-decision 18 (SD 5) vs 49 minutes; estimated call-to-ECMO-flow shorter in the prehospital arm, assuming 20 minutes of on-scene cannulation
Caveat
Simulation only, with cannulation duration presumed and flow times estimated rather than measured; no patient outcomes
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). eCPR is not something Australian ambulances carry — adoption would need a guideline change — but the time savings it demonstrates matter for refractory-arrest pathway planning.

Learning goal

Weigh the role of prehospital eCPR activation in my transport decisions for refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

  • Shockable rhythms decay fast in untreated arrest

    International Emergency Nursing · 26 Nov 2026 · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    Italian-led systematic review of 38 studies (1990–2025, more than 262,700 adult OHCA cases) found shockable rhythms decline progressively with increasing time-to-defibrillation, with earlier shock linked to higher defibrillation success, more ROSC and better survival.

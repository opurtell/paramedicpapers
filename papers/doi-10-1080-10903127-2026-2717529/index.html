Research priorities for prehospital older adult care

Research and Knowledge Translation Priorities for the Prehospital Care of Older Adults: A Modified Delphi Study
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 25 August 2026

Modified Delphi study identified five high-priority research themes for older adult prehospital care, with falls assessment, prevention, and clinician training ranking highest across research and knowledge translation domains.

Study type
Other
Design
Modified Delphi study with multidisciplinary stakeholder panel
Participants
28 stakeholders (EMS clinicians, geriatricians, medical directors, educators and others)
Main finding
145 candidate priorities consolidated into 30 themes; 17 advanced to final ranking; five high-priority, high-consensus topics identified in both research and knowledge translation domains, four shared, centring on assessment after perceived low-acuity falls, fall prevention, clinician training, and assessment tools
Caveat
Consensus priorities reflect stakeholder opinion, not evidence of effect, and likely reflect the participating health system
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). transferability — Older adults represent growing EMS demographic — findings should guide research and practice priorities for this population

Learning goal

Understand how evidence gaps in prehospital older adult care shape my assessment of falls presented as low acuity.

    BMC Geriatrics · 3 Oct 2026 · Cross-sectional · Context only

    Swedish register study of 8208 ambulance-transported patients aged ≥65 in Stockholm found only 7% went directly to a geriatric unit instead of an ED, with conveyance rates varying fourfold (4–21%) between hospital service areas and largely explained by distance, ownership and staffed-bed numbers.

  • Rising ED boarding for older adults

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 19 Sep 2026 · Cross-sectional · Context only

    US national survey analysis of 4.45 million admissions of adults aged ≥65 (2015–2022) — 85% boarded ≥2 hours in the ED, mean boarding time rose from 138 minutes in 2018 to 343 minutes in 2022 and roughly 501 minutes for patients with Alzheimer's-related dementia, with the worst growth in metropolitan areas and among those ≥75.

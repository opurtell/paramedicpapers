Bystanders describe heavy emotional toll of doing CPR

Experiences and coping strategies of bystanders involved in out-of-hospital cardiopulmonary resuscitation: A qualitative study
Resuscitation Plus · 2026-10 · Added 1 November 2026

A Scottish qualitative study of 11 bystanders who performed CPR in an OHCA found a substantial emotional impact — worst when the patient died or was known to them — with themes of being compelled to help but unprepared, and identified better emotional preparation in CPR training and access to a knowledgeable independent person as ways to reduce it.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative — semi-structured interviews, reflexive thematic analysis
Participants
11 bystanders (9 knew the patient), recruited via social media, Scotland
Main finding
Four themes: emotional impact; managing multiple roles; compelled to help but unprepared; talking to the right person
Caveat
Small, self-selected sample recruited through social media advertisements
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Bystander experience after CPR is the same in any system, and CPR training and post-event support are things Australian ambulance services directly shape.

Learning goal

Understand the emotional impact on bystanders performing CPR and how dispatcher support can better prepare them.

    Resuscitation · 31 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

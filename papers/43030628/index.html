Numbers for when on-scene ROSC stops happening

On-Scene Time-Dependent Probabilities of Return of Spontaneous Circulation and Favorable Neurological Outcome in Cases of Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest with an Initial Shockable Rhythm: A Retrospective Study Using the National Utstein Data in Japan
Journal of Clinical Medicine · 2026-10-31 · Added 15 November 2026

A Japanese national Utstein cohort of 22,383 adults with shockable cardiogenic OHCA found the probability of subsequent on-scene ROSC fell from 6.97% at 10 minutes to 0.50% at 20 minutes of resuscitation, with favourable neurological outcome in 50.0% of under-75s achieving on-scene ROSC at or after 10 minutes.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective nationwide Utstein descriptive cohort, 2017–2022
Participants
22,383 adults with cardiogenic OHCA and an initial shockable rhythm
Main finding
Probability of subsequent on-scene ROSC 6.97% at 10 min and 0.50% at 20 min after EMS contact; favourable neurological outcome (CPC 1–2 at 1 month) in 50.0% of patients <75 and 20.5% of those ≥75 with on-scene ROSC at or after 10 min
Caveat
Descriptive Japanese EMS data — no causal claims, and the authors state it does not establish age-based criteria for prolonged resuscitation or transport
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Shockable-rhythm resuscitation duration and transport decisions are made by paramedics across Australia, and this is a 22,383-patient national registry over six years.

Learning goal

Weigh when ongoing on-scene resuscitation in shockable cardiac arrest remains worthwhile against early transport decisions.

Harder ambulance transports, higher pneumonia mortality

Impact of the COVID ‐19 Pandemic on Ambulance Transport for Suspected Bacterial Pneumonia and In‐Hospital Mortality: A Population‐Based ORION Study
Acute Medicine & Surgery · 2026-01 · Added 17 November 2026

A Japanese population registry study of 78,720 patients transported for suspected bacterial pneumonia in Osaka found difficult ambulance transport (four or more hospital selections and 30+ minutes on scene) rose from 2.7% pre-pandemic to 10.3% during COVID-19 and was associated with higher in-hospital mortality (adjusted OR 1.14).

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective population-based registry study (Osaka ORION), 2018-2023
Participants
78,720 patients transported for suspected bacterial pneumonia (65,510 physician-diagnosed)
Main finding
Difficult transport rose from 2.7% (2018-2019) to 10.3% (2020-2022) and 9.3% (2023); difficult transport associated with higher in-hospital mortality (adjusted OR 1.14, 95% CI 1.03-1.25); transport rates fell from 166.3 to 117.3 per 100,000 residents then returned to 167.0 by 2023
Caveat
Japanese registry with EMT-based diagnosis coding and a pandemic-era case mix that shifted alongside the transport changes
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Hospital refusal of ambulances and prolonged transports are live pressures here too, though the Japanese hospital-acceptance system and EMT diagnosis coding differ from Australian arrangements.

Learning goal

Understand how prolonged hospital selection during transport affects outcomes for patients with suspected pneumonia.

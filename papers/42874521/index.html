HEMS overtriage in burn injuries

Burn injury management by Dutch physician-staffed HEMS: Findings from the HEMS-BURNS cohort and National Trauma Registry
Burns · 2026-08-17 · Added 16 October 2026

Nine-year Dutch HEMS burn cohort found nearly half of physician-staffed helicopter dispatches for burns were cancelled, mostly for minor injuries, and HEMS-specific interventions were performed in only 33.6% of completed missions.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Descriptive retrospective cohort
Participants
976 HEMS dispatches for suspected burns plus 879 registry patients, Rotterdam 2015–2023
Main finding
46.6% of dispatches were cancelled (63.3% for minor injury); interventions occurred in 33.6% of non-cancelled dispatches, lower in children (14.2%) and scald burns (14.6%) than adults (43.5%) and flame burns (45.6%)
Caveat
Descriptive single-HEMS study; intervention rates were used as a proxy for benefit without a comparison group
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). Physician-staffed HEMS model — most Australian burn patients are handled by road crews and adult/paediatric retrieval services, so the dispatch-criteria debate reads across only loosely.

Learning goal

Weigh the value of specialist retrieval dispatch for burn injuries against overtriage and cancelled missions.

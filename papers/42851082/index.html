AR training keeps teen AED skills fresh

Effectiveness and retention of learning the use of an automated external defibrillator in adolescents: a comparative analysis of three teaching methods
Health Education Research · 2026-10 · Added 15 October 2026

Quasi-experimental study of 193 secondary students found all three AED teaching methods produced safe, effective shocks initially, but the augmented-reality group retained a 100% effective shock rate at 3 months while the traditionally trained group declined significantly.

Study type
Other
Design
Quasi-experimental, three teaching groups, 3-month follow-up
Participants
193 untrained students aged 13–15
Main finding
100% effective shock rate retained at 3 months in the augmented-reality group vs a significant decrease in the traditional training group
Caveat
Quasi-experimental allocation, manikin assessment only, no real-world defibrillation
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). School AED education sits under public-access defibrillation programs, which vary in scale and funding across Australia.

Learning goal

Strengthen my approach to teaching AED use to young people so skills are retained beyond the initial session.

What paramedics want from voice-to-text ePCRs

Canadian Paramedic User Requirements for Speech Recognition-Aided Documentation
Applied Clinical Informatics · 2026-09-29 · Added 3 October 2026

A mixed-methods survey of 323 Canadian paramedics plus 14 interviews identified and ranked the features paramedics want in speech-recognition documentation — 86% viewed it positively, with accuracy, patient interaction and compliance rated most important.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Mixed-methods (questionnaire plus interviews)
Participants
323 paramedic survey respondents, 14 interviews
Main finding
86% (277/323) had positive perceptions of speech recognition; top-rated features were patient interaction, compliance, support, durability, training, accuracy, continuous improvement, training support, dictation and editing
Caveat
Opt-in self-selected respondents may over-represent tech-enthusiasts; no working-system evaluation
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A Canadian paramedic service, but the documentation burden and ePCR context are the same here, and Australian services are actively exploring dictation tools.

Learning goal

Understand how speech recognition documentation could affect patient interaction and record quality in my practice.

Lights and sirens save only ~3 minutes

The Estimated Benefit of Lights and Sirens During Ambulance Transport
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 26 April 2026

This study used a modern transport model to estimate whether lights and sirens save time, and it found a median gain of only 3.0 to 3.3 minutes per trip, which likely does not justify the known driving risks except in selected cases.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Observational study comparing actual emergent trips with modelled non-emergent trip durations
Participants
25,902 incidents, 21.0% transported with lights and sirens
Main finding
Lights and sirens were associated with a median trip duration saving of 3.0 min (95% CI 2.9–3.1), or 3.3 min in spatially aggregated analysis, and 0.3 min/km faster pace
Caveat
Modelled non-emergent comparisons in a single US EMS system; median benefit may hide variation by case type
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Learning goal

Understand when the modest time saved by lights and sirens transport justifies the added road safety risk.

No sex differences in OHCA with confirmed CAD

Sex differences in management and outcomes after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest with angiographically confirmed obstructive coronary artery disease
Resuscitation Plus · Added 29 September 2026

Post hoc analysis of a prospective European OHCA registry (2012–2023) testing whether sex-based differences in care persist when obstructive CAD is angiographically confirmed as the arrest cause — among 580 patients (15.3% women), PCI use, procedural success, targeted temperature management and ECPR were similar between sexes, 30-day survival was 66.2% in women vs 61.5% in men (p=0.33), favourable neurological outcome 55.1% vs 53.8% (p=0.82), and female sex was not independently associated with 30-day survival (adjusted OR 0.75, not significant).

Study type
Registry
Design
Post hoc analysis of a prospective European OHCA registry
Participants
580 adults with OHCA and angiographically confirmed obstructive CAD (15.3% women)
Main finding
30-day survival 66.2% (women) vs 61.5% (men), p=0.33; favourable neurological outcome 55.1% vs 53.8%, p=0.82; sex not associated with 30-day survival (adjusted OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.39–1.41).
Caveat
Post hoc analysis of a selected cohort with only 15.3% women; in-hospital subgroup of OHCA patients.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Registry data on in-hospital post-ROSC care rather than a prehospital intervention — useful context for the post-arrest pathways ACTAS feeds into (CMG 4 Cardiac Arrest: Adult, CMG 16 Suspected ACS), but nothing changes on the road tomorrow.

Learning goal

Recognise how sex bias may delay recognition of obstructive coronary disease in women after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

  • Early adrenaline + early airway in OHCA

    The Journal of Emergency Medicine · 2 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    Korean nationwide registry cohort of 20,662 OHCA patients testing whether early adrenaline (≤10 min) combined with early advanced airway management (≤6 min) improves outcomes — the combination roughly doubled odds of good neurological recovery (aOR 2.44) and survival to discharge (aOR 2.18) versus late/late, and early adrenaline with late airway showed no benefit, but the interaction attenuated in sensitivity analyses addressing resuscitation time bias, so it is hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

  • PIHCA vs GO-FAR 2 for IHCA prediction

    Resuscitation Plus · 30 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    External validation of the PIHCA and GO-FAR 2 pre-arrest scores across 501 Swedish in-hospital cardiac arrests (28% survived with favourable neurological outcome) found comparable discrimination for favourable outcome (AUROC 0.73 vs 0.70, difference not significant) and adequate calibration for both scores.

  • Post-OHCA outcomes at Victorian Heart Hospital

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 29 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Audit of 121 post-arrest patients at the Victorian Heart Hospital — 50.4% survived to discharge with 92.6% prehospital ROSC and 61.2% shockable rhythms, and 24% needed interhospital transfer, mostly for non-cardiac causes.

  • No evidence on screening arrest survivors' psychology

    Resuscitation · 26 Sep 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    Systematic review of structured identification and assessment of psychological, emotional or cognitive problems in cardiac arrest survivors — of 1,605 records screened, none met eligibility, so there is no evidence base for which screening instruments, thresholds, timing or referral pathways should be used.

Supporting bystanders after failed resuscitation

Bystanders and Close Ones in Unsuccessful Resuscitation: An Overlooked Population in Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest
Nursing Reports · Added 27 October 2026

An Italian perspective paper argues bystanders and relatives involved in unsuccessful out-of-hospital resuscitation are an overlooked group at risk of lasting psychological distress, and sketches supportive follow-up and referral roles for emergency clinicians.

Study type
Other
Design
Perspective/review
Participants
Not reported
Main finding
Unsuccessful resuscitation involvement can cause emotional distress, intrusive memories, guilt and PTSD symptoms; supportive follow-up contact and referral pathways are proposed over formal debriefing
Caveat
A perspective paper — the evidence on supportive follow-up effectiveness remains preliminary
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). As community responder schemes grow, more Australians will perform CPR that fails — but this is a perspective piece with preliminary evidence, framed around nursing roles.

Learning goal

Understand my role in recognising and supporting bystander psychological distress after unsuccessful resuscitation.

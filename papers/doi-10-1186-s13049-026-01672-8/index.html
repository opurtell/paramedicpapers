Who makes non-urgent ED visits

Non-urgent emergency department utilization in adults: a systematic review of associated factors across diverse health systems
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 18 August 2026

Systematic review of 32 studies across 21 countries identified factors associated with non-urgent ED utilisation, with frequent prior ED visits (aOR up to 82) and patient perception of non-serious illness (aOR up to 21) being the strongest predictors

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review of cross-sectional, cohort and case-control studies
Participants
32 studies, 21 countries
Main finding
Non-urgent visit proportion 1–82.4%; strongest associations with frequent prior ED visits (aOR up to 82.13) and patient perception of non-serious illness (aOR up to 21.03)
Caveat
Substantial heterogeneity in definitions of non-urgent visits; narrative synthesis only
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — Provides operational context for understanding ED utilisation patterns, though in-hospital focus

Learning goal

Understand the patient and system factors that drive non-urgent emergency department presentations in Australian primary care context.

Public-access defibrillation location effects in Yokohama

Location-resolved performance of public-access defibrillation in an urban EMS system: a population-based study in Yokohama, Japan
Resuscitation Plus · Added 11 August 2026

Population-based study of public-access defibrillation performance across different locations in urban Yokohama, Japan

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective population-based registry study with propensity score matching
Participants
15,345 OHCA patients in Yokohama, Japan, 2021–2024 (272 received PAD shock)
Main finding
PAD shock associated with favourable 30-day neurological outcome (OR 3.3; 95% CI 1.9–5.7) and 30-day survival (OR 3.7; 95% CI 2.2–6.1); shock rates varied by location from 34% at sports facilities to 0.06% at private residences
Caveat
Japanese urban setting; very few PAD shocks overall, and residual confounding possible despite matching
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Relevant to PAD deployment strategies but abstract not available for detailed assessment

Learning goal

Understand how location influences public-access defibrillation use and outcomes in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

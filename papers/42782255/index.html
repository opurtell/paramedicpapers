Prehospital minithoracostomy vs chest drain

Prehospital Minithoracostomy versus Chest Drainage for Emergency Pleural Decompression in Major Trauma
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 29 September 2026

Retrospective physician-led HEMS cohort (94 trauma patients) comparing prehospital minithoracostomy with chest drainage — minithoracostomy carried no meaningful scene-time penalty and fewer recorded complications, but non-random allocation and few events make it hypothesis-generating only.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective physician-led HEMS cohort with Bayesian analysis
Participants
94 major trauma adults (66 minithoracostomy, 28 chest drainage)
Main finding
Posterior median scene-time difference −5.9 minutes (95% CrI −13.8 to 1.7); recorded complications 0/50 evaluable minithoracostomy vs 6/10 evaluable chest drainage (risk difference −58.7 percentage points, 95% CrI −83.9 to −29.3); no clear survival differences at 1, 30 or 90 days.
Caveat
Non-randomised allocation by clinical judgment, complication ascertainment differed between groups, and very few events.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — surgical pleural decompression isn't in the extracted ACTAS guidelines (CMG 17 covers needle decompression, ICP-flagged); would need a guideline change to adopt

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and limitations of finger thoracostomy compared with chest drainage for traumatic pleural decompression in my practice.

OHCA cause attribution shifts with definitions

Cardiac cause attribution in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: linkage of a provincial resuscitation registry with the death-registration system in Zhejiang, China
BMC Public Health · 2026-11-16 · Added 19 November 2026

A Chinese registry–death-registration linkage study in Zhejiang showed cardiac cause attribution in OHCA ranged from 20.7% to 42.3% depending on attribution rules and eligibility definitions used.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective registry linkage study (bootstrap cluster CIs)
Participants
55,720 registry records; 11,427-record linked-death cohort
Main finding
Cardiac attribution estimates ranged from 20.74% to 42.32% across the definition matrix; in paired records, registry-recorded cardiac attribution was 22.70% vs 27.82% under a broader rule (difference 5.12 pp, 95% CI 0.96–8.51).
Caveat
Three-quarters of death-query outcomes were unknown because of identification-document barriers.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A registry-methods question that matters to how OHCA epidemiology is counted and compared, less to the road — useful when reading any 'proportion cardiac' claim.

Learning goal

Recognise how differing cardiac cause attribution definitions shape reported outcomes and epidemiology of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

Cardiogenic shock: prehospital recognition and routing

Cardiogenic Shock Systems of Care Best Practices. Part 1: Prehospital and Emergency Department Evaluation and Management
US Cardiology · 2026-09-22 · Added 15 October 2026

Best-practice review of cardiogenic shock systems of care covering prehospital and ED evaluation argues early identification and direct routing to shock centres prevents irreversible organ damage, while conceding prehospital identification remains difficult.

Study type
Guideline / statement
Design
Narrative best-practice review (Part 1 of a series)
Participants
Not applicable — expert review
Main finding
Emphasises early prehospital/ED identification and immediate transfer to a cardiogenic shock centre; no new outcome data
Caveat
Expert opinion without systematic evidence appraisal or comparative data
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Cardiogenic shock centres and mechanical support capacity are concentrated in a handful of Australian hospitals, and paramedic pathways to them vary by service.

Learning goal

Recognise early signs of cardiogenic shock and refine my judgement on when direct routing to a specialised cardiac centre is appropriate.

Mapping emergency transport in remote Indigenous communities

Emergency Response and Patient Transport in Remote Indigenous Communities: A Scoping Review
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-09 · Added 12 November 2026

A global scoping review of 76 publications on prehospital emergency response and transport for rural and remote Indigenous communities found air retrieval dominated the literature (71% of publications) while community first response was rarely evaluated — only 16% of publications used an evaluative design.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Scoping review (PubMed, Embase, Scopus; March 2026 search)
Participants
76 publications reviewed
Main finding
Air transport/medical evacuation described in 71% of publications and ground transport in 51%; explicit paramedic/EMS detail in 24% and dispatch in 12%; top barriers were infrastructure and communications (92%), cost and jurisdiction (87%), distance to definitive care (83%); only 16% of publications evaluated a pathway
Caveat
Descriptive scoping review of mostly unevaluated literature — no outcome data to guide practice
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Directly relevant to remote Australian communities, where long retrieval distances and infrastructure gaps are everyday reality; the review's gaps mirror how little of this pathway has been formally evaluated here.

Learning goal

Understand the system barriers shaping emergency transport and retrieval for patients in remote Indigenous communities.

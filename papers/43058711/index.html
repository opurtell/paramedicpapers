Flipped classroom beats lectures for ECG skills

Comparing flipped classroom and lecture-based education on clinical competency in electrocardiogram interpretation among prehospital emergency personnel
BMC Medical Education · 2026-08-26 · Added 21 November 2026

An Iranian quasi-experimental pre-post study comparing flipped-classroom with lecture-based teaching found the flipped group had significantly higher ECG interpretation competency scores at two weeks and one month after training among prehospital emergency personnel.

Prehospital emergency personnel in Iran, 2025 (sample size not reported in abstract)
Main finding
Flipped-classroom group scored significantly higher at two weeks (mean 6.84, p=0.002) and one month after intervention; groups were equivalent at baseline (p=0.10)
Caveat
Quasi-experimental pre-post design with no long-term follow-up and sample details incomplete in the abstract
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Done with Iranian prehospital personnel in a different training system, but flipped-classroom design is a live question for Australian paramedic education and ECG competency is universal.

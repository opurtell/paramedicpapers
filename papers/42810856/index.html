Risk-score-guided conveyance safe in chest pain

PreHEART score for emergency medical services triage of chest pain: a randomised trial
Heart · 2026-09-29 · Added 3 October 2026

A Dutch pragmatic RCT testing whether a prehospital risk score could guide ambulance conveyance in 5170 adults with undifferentiated chest pain — it was non-inferior to usual care for 3-day serious adverse events while increasing non-conveyance, cutting 30-day ED presentations and lowering costs.

Study type
RCT
Design
Pragmatic randomised open-label blinded-endpoint non-inferiority trial
Participants
5170 adults with undifferentiated chest pain
Main finding
PreHEART-guided conveyance non-inferior for 3-day unexpected serious adverse events (win ratio 1.01, 95% CI 0.89–1.16); non-conveyance rose to 34.6% vs 24.5%, 30-day emergency presentations fell (RR 0.88), costs −€345, and no deaths within 3 days among 1526 non-conveyed patients
Caveat
Open-label trial in a Dutch EMS system with its own consult-and-convey structure; the signals for increased ACS detection in women were nominal and didn't survive multiplicity correction
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Non-conveyance pathways exist across Australian services, but validated chest-pain decision tools aren't routine on Australian ambulances — this is the strongest evidence yet that they're safe, and it directly informs the keep-at-home debate.

Learning goal

Weigh the safety of risk-score-guided non-conveyance when triaging adults with undifferentiated chest pain.

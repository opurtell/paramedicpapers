Prehospital variables can't pick pelvic binder benefit

Prehospital Variables Do Not Reliably Identify Pelvic Fracture Morphologies Amenable to Pelvic Binder Application: A Retrospective Imaging Review
The American Surgeon · 2026-10-10 · Added 11 October 2026

Blinded imaging review of 298 CT-confirmed pelvic ring injuries found binder application would be potentially mechanically unfavourable in 56% of cases, and no prehospital variable (hypotension, shock index, mechanism, GCS) discriminated favourable morphology well.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Retrospective imaging review at a tertiary trauma centre
Participants
298 adults with CT-confirmed pelvic ring injury
Main finding
Binder application would be favourable in only 17% (95% CI 13–22%), neutral in 28% and potentially unfavourable in 56%; the best-performing variable (ISS) reached just AUC 0.65, all others below 0.60
Caveat
Single-centre surgeon-classified imaging review, and binder position on CT may not reflect how it sat in the field
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Pelvic binders are near-universal across Australian services and the empiric-application decision comes up on every major trauma job.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when prehospital pelvic binder application is likely to benefit or harm pelvic ring injuries.

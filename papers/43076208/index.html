Liberal ambulance fluids did not reduce sepsis mortality

Prehospital Fluid Resuscitation for Patients With Suspected Sepsis: The PITSTOP-Fluids Factorial Randomized Clinical Trial
JAMA · 2026-11-24 · Added 25 November 2026

A Canadian multicentre factorial RCT testing liberal versus usual-care prehospital fluid resuscitation in suspected sepsis found hospital mortality was essentially identical at 18.0% versus 18.3%.

Study type
RCT
Design
Multicentre 2×2 factorial randomised controlled trial (4 Ontario paramedic services)
Participants
1646 adults with suspected sepsis (median age 76)
Main finding
Hospital mortality 18.0% with liberal fluids (median 1500 mL) versus 18.3% with usual care (median 500 mL), RR 0.98 (95% CI 0.80–1.20); all 17 secondary outcomes similar.
Caveat
Suspected sepsis criteria (fever plus SBP <100 mmHg) excluded the sickest patients, and even usual care gave most a bolus.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Normal saline and fluid boluses are standard Australian ambulance practice, and this trial tested the strategy itself rather than a new drug or device.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and harms of liberal prehospital fluid boluses for suspected sepsis before intravenous access and antibiotics.

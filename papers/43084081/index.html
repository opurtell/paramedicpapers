Deprived areas wait longest for major trauma access

Localised geographic and socioeconomic inequalities in prehospital trauma care access for road traffic collisions in England: a spatial autocorrelation analysis
European Journal of Trauma and Emergency Surgery · 2026-11-25 · Added 27 November 2026

An English spatial analysis of 33,755 small areas modelled drive and flight times to major trauma centres and HEMS bases against deprivation and serious-collision rates — no national deprivation gradient, but 1,101 areas were both deprived and access-poor.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Spatial autocorrelation analysis of national area-level data
Participants
33,755 English small areas (LSOAs), 2022–2024 collision data
Main finding
No national deprivation–access gradient, but 1,101 areas (3.4%) were simultaneously more deprived and access-poor, with median drive times of 56.8 minutes to a major trauma centre and higher killed-or-seriously-injured collision rates.
Caveat
Modelled drive and flight times rather than actual ambulance response, and England-only road and deprivation data.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The geography and HEMS network are England's, but the method for finding access-poor, high-collision areas is one Australian services could run on their own data.

Learning goal

Understand how geographic and socioeconomic access to major trauma care shapes prehospital decision-making and retrieval pathways.

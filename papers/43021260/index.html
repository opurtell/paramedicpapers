Weekly simulation linked to better student exams

The impact of simulation with debriefing on developing clinical competence and performance in paramedicine students
BMC Medical Education · 2026-08-18 · Added 14 November 2026

An Australian retrospective analysis of 305 paramedicine students across 1,243 simulated scenarios found weekly practice frequency was positively correlated with summative exam performance (r = 0.257, p < 0.001), with competence scores rising across the trimester.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Retrospective analysis of performance-based assessment data (2019–2024) from a single university programme
Participants
305 second-year paramedicine students and 1,243 practice scenarios
Main finding
Practice frequency correlated with exam performance (r = 0.257, p < 0.001), strongest in the final trimester tertile (r = 0.229), and a 2020 performance dip during reduced COVID-19 simulation exposure highlighted the role of consistent practice
Caveat
Single-institution retrospective correlation, not a causal test of simulation dosage
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). It's an Australian paramedicine programme, so the finding is directly about local education — but a modest correlation from one university's assessment data doesn't settle how programmes should schedule simulation.

Learning goal

Strengthen my commitment to regular simulation practice and structured debriefing to sustain clinical competence across the year.

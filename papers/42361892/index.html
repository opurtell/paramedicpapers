Bystander rescue breathing no benefit in suffocation OHCA

Association of rescue breathing with outcomes in adult suffocation-related cardiac arrest
Resuscitation · Added 3 August 2026

This retrospective cohort of 76,774 Japanese patients with witnessed suffocation-related OHCA found no bystander CPR was associated with higher favourable neurological outcomes compared with compression-only CPR (OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.04-1.38), while CCV-CPR showed no significant difference versus compression-only.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective nationwide registry cohort with IPTW adjustment
Participants
76,774 adults with witnessed suffocation-related OHCA
Main finding
No bystander CPR associated with better 1-month favourable neurological outcome than compression-only CPR (adjusted OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.04-1.38); CCV-CPR no significant difference (OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.78-1.32)
Caveat
Retrospective registry data with possible residual confounding; Japanese setting with different bystander CPR practices
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Japanese-specific context and counterintuitive findings that require local validation before changing practice guidance

Learning goal

Weigh the role of rescue breathing in bystander CPR guidance for suffocation-related cardiac arrest.

    Resuscitation · 3 Aug 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    This registry study of 436 OHCA patients compared refractory VF (all three shocks failed to terminate, 5% of cases) versus recurrent VF, finding refractory VF patients had lower ROSC (41% vs 59%) and 30-day survival (23% vs 46%) with predictors including no CPR/AED before EMS, longer delay to first shock, and lower amplitude spectral area.

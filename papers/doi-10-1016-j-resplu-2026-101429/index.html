Loss of pulsatility predicts death after ECPR

Incidence and impact of loss of pulsatility on outcomes after extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation – A retrospective cohort study
Resuscitation Plus · Added 18 August 2026

Retrospective cohort of 221 ECPR patients found loss of pulsatility (zero pulse pressure) occurred in 49% of cases and was associated with significantly higher in-hospital mortality (74% vs 48%), with each additional hour of non-pulsatility increasing mortality risk by 17%

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective single-centre cohort
Participants
221 ECPR patients
Main finding
Loss of pulsatility occurred in 49% (108/221); in-hospital mortality 74% vs 48% (p=0.01); each additional hour of non-pulsatility increased mortality risk (OR 1.17, 95% CI 1.05–1.30)
Caveat
Retrospective, single high-volume ECMO centre; loss of pulsatility group had more OHCA and longer arrest-to-ECMO times
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — Highly specialized ECMO center study with limited direct prehospital relevance, though useful for understanding ECPR prognostication

Learning goal

Recognise the risks of loss of pulsatility during resuscitation and how prolonged non-pulsatility shapes outcomes after refractory arrest.

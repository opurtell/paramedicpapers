Paediatric OHCA: half had warning symptoms

Cardiac warning symptoms in paediatric out-of-hospital ventricular fibrillation cardiac arrest survivors
Open Heart · 2026-07 · Added 6 October 2026

UK single-centre review of 58 consecutive paediatric out-of-hospital VF arrest survivors without a pre-existing diagnosis found 50.9% had reported cardiac symptoms beforehand — syncope in 23.6%, half the arrests during exertion — and of 13 children with prior syncope only three had sought medical attention and none had follow-up arranged, pointing to missed opportunities to diagnose inherited cardiac conditions before arrest.

Study type
Case series
Design
Single-centre retrospective review of consecutive paediatric OHCA survivors at a UK tertiary referral centre (1996–2025)
Participants
58 survivors of paediatric OHCA aged 0–18
Main finding
50.9% had prior cardiac symptoms (syncope 23.6%); 50% of arrests occurred on exertion; 72.4% were later diagnosed with an inherited cardiac condition, most commonly hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; of 13 children with prior syncope only three sought medical attention and none had follow-up arranged
Caveat
Single tertiary centre, survivors only (so no data on children who died at first presentation), and symptom history via retrospective record review
Bottom line
Practice prompt

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). red-flag symptoms in children — exertional syncope in particular — are exactly what paramedics screen for, and this quantifies how often those warning signs slip through before a devastating presentation

Learning goal

Strengthen my recognition of warning symptoms and exertional syncope as red flags for inherited cardiac conditions in children.

Neonatal chest compressions: most survive a month

Survival after neonatal chest compressions at birth: Temporal trends and prognostic factors in a population-based cohort, Queensland, Australia
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 19 November 2026

An Australian population cohort of 3,077 Queensland infants who received chest compressions at birth found 89.0% survived to 28 days, with survival improving from 82% in 2008 to 89% in 2024.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective population-based cohort (perinatal data collection)
Participants
3,077 liveborn chest-compression recipients, Queensland 2008–2024
Main finding
70.5% established regular breathing within 60 minutes and 89.0% survived to 28 days; adjusted survival odds fell with gestational age (aOR 0.17 under 28 weeks), though the term vs moderate-preterm gap attenuated after adjusting for cord pH or adrenaline.
Caveat
Administrative data lack compression indications and resuscitation quality measures.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian data on a resuscitation paramedics perform at field births, but the cohort is dominated by in-hospital births, so it informs context and counselling more than on-road technique.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on prognostic factors influencing newborn survival after birth compressions.

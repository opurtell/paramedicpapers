Breathlessness self-management linked to fewer ambulance calls

The Australian Capital Territory breathlessness intervention service (ABIS): a co-designed feasibility study
BMC Pulmonary Medicine · 2026-08-20 · Added 18 November 2026

An Australian co-designed feasibility study of a home-based breathlessness intervention service found 140 completers over 22 months, with 90% reporting improvement in daily activities and 21% reporting they had avoided calling an ambulance for breathlessness.

Study type
Other
Design
Co-designed feasibility study with mixed-methods evaluation
Participants
140 patients completing the programme over 22 months
Main finding
90% reported improvement on activities of daily living, 70% on 'worst' breathlessness and 68% on mastery; 21% reported avoiding an ambulance call using the strategies they learned
Caveat
Single-arm feasibility study without a control group; outcomes were self-reported and the 100-patient 12-month recruitment goal was missed
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). An Australian community service rather than an ambulance pathway, but its ambulance-avoidance outcome is directly relevant to how frequent breathlessness callers could be supported elsewhere in the system.

Learning goal

Understand how non-pharmacological breathlessness self-management strategies can reduce urgent ambulance demand for chronic respiratory patients.

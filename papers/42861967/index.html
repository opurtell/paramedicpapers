Who calls an ambulance for low-acuity problems

Factors Associated with Low-Acuity EMS Transport to the Emergency Department: An Observational Study Guided by the Andersen Behavioral Model
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-10-12 · Added 13 October 2026

Retrospective study of 41,761 low-acuity ED visits in the southeastern US found only 7.7% arrived by ambulance, with the highest odds among retired or disabled (aOR 3.60) and unemployed (aOR 2.26) patients and the lowest among those with an established GP (aOR 0.57).

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective multicentre observational cohort (secondary EHR analysis)
Participants
41,761 low-acuity ED encounters across 21 EDs
Main finding
3,232 of 41,761 discharged low-acuity patients (7.7%) arrived by EMS; nighttime presentation (aOR 1.63) and mental health diagnosis (aOR 1.76) also raised the odds
Caveat
Captured only visits ending in ED discharge in one US region, so admitted low-acuity transports and the fully transported caseload aren't represented
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Low-acuity demand and alternative referral pathways are live issues for Australian ambulance services, though the US insurance-driven ED setting limits direct comparison.

Learning goal

Understand the access and social factors that lead Australian patients to call an ambulance for low-acuity problems.

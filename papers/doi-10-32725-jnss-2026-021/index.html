Paediatric IO sim success beat IV in students

Comparison of intravenous and intraosseous access performed by paramedic students on two paediatric simulation models: a within-subject simulation study
Journal of Nursing, Social Studies, Public Health and Rehabilitation · 2026-11-05 · Added 17 November 2026

A Czech within-subject simulation study had 50 paramedic students attempt paediatric IV on an arm trainer and intraosseous insertion on a high-fidelity manikin, finding IO completed with verified patency in 50 of 50 attempts versus IV in 4 of 50.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Within-subject simulation study with fixed task order and a single non-blinded assessor
Participants
50 second- and third-year paramedic students
Main finding
IO insertion with verified patency 50/50 attempts (100%, mean 118.1 s); IV catheterisation 4/50 (8%, mean 288.9 s among successes); 92% of students rated IV more demanding and 88% found the models unrealistic or only partly realistic
Caveat
Two different simulator models in fixed order - the authors state the findings describe simulator performance and cannot be extrapolated to clinical practice
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). IO access is standard kit across Australian services and paediatric vascular access failures are real, but the students rated the models unrealistic and the authors state nothing can be extrapolated to patients.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when to escalate from paediatric intravenous cannulation to intraosseous access in the prehospital setting.

Autonomy and support, not workload, drive flourishing

Emergency medical services personnel's work capability sets: associations with job demands, job resources, and flourishing at work
Frontiers in Psychology · 2026-10-26 · Added 11 November 2026

A South African cross-sectional survey of 197 EMS personnel found emotional demands were associated with lower work capability and flourishing, while autonomy and co-worker support — not job demands — distinguished the strongest capability group.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional survey with latent class analysis
Participants
197 EMS personnel
Main finding
Three capability sets emerged (weak 48%, constrained 30%, robust 22%); emotional demands correlated negatively with capability and flourishing, while autonomy and co-worker support differentiated the groups and job demands did not
Caveat
Cross-sectional self-report in one country — no causal claims possible
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). South African EMS, but the demands-resources pattern echoes what Australian paramedic wellbeing studies keep finding

Learning goal

Recognise how autonomy and co-worker support shape my work capability and flourishing under emotionally demanding conditions.

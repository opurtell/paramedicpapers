Forecast: prehospital transfusion growth and HDFN risk

Forecasting the rate of hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn in the United States based on recent trends in prehospital transfusion
Transfusion · 2026-11-05 · Added 7 November 2026

US researchers forecast that growing prehospital transfusion of RhD-positive blood to females of childbearing potential will lead to haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn, with one predicted case every 0.5–20 years depending on region and model.

Study type
Modelling
Design
Forecasting model (regression on US transfusion trends, 500 instantiations per model)
Participants
779 females of childbearing potential projected to receive prehospital transfusion in the US in 2029
Main finding
One HDFN case forecast every 0.5–20 years depending on region and model (base case: 0.5 years in the South, 20.0 in the Northeast).
Caveat
A forecasting exercise built on US trends and assumptions — no observed HDFN cases.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is limited to a few Australian services, so this informs the debate more than current practice.

Learning goal

Weigh the risk of haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn when giving RhD-positive blood to females of childbearing potential.

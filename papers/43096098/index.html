PTSD symptoms climb in the first year on road

Post-traumatic stress, distress, coping and sleep quality in early-career paramedics
Occupational Medicine · 2026-11-26 · Added 27 November 2026

An Australian longitudinal study followed 21 intern paramedics across five timepoints in their first year and found PTSD symptoms rose significantly, with poorer average sleep quality tracking both PTSD and psychological distress.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Longitudinal cohort, five timepoints over 12 months
Participants
21 intern paramedics
Main finding
PTSD symptoms increased over the first year (B 0.9, 95% CI 0.2–1.6); between-person sleep quality was associated with both PTSD symptoms and distress (p < 0.001), while coping style and within-person sleep quality were not.
Caveat
Only 21 interns with no control group, and 12-month associations can't establish what drives what.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). An Australian intern cohort — first-year crews and sleep are the same issue in every service — though 21 participants is a very small sample.

Learning goal

Understand how sleep quality relates to PTSD symptoms and distress during my early years of practice.

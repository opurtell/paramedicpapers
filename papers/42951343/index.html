Teen drinking falls, alcohol ambulance call-outs don't

Do trends in alcohol harm mirror consumption? An analysis of adolescent ambulance attendances
Journal of Public Health · 2026-10-28 · Added 30 October 2026

An Australian study of the National Ambulance Surveillance System tracked 25,928 adolescent alcohol-intoxication ambulance attendances in Victoria from 2012 to 2023 and found rates stayed largely flat despite falling teen drinking, with a small decline only among males in major cities.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective registry analysis with multivariable regression, stratified by gender and remoteness
Participants
25,928 ambulance attendances for adolescent alcohol intoxication (ages 10–19), Victoria 2012–2023
Main finding
A small but significant monthly decline occurred only among males in major cities (B = −0.01, P < .001); no significant trends for males in regional areas or for females in metropolitan or regional areas
Caveat
Attendance data capture acute harm presentations rather than drinking behaviour, and the data are Victorian only
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Australian ambulance data on a Friday-night workload that lands on crews everywhere — the gap between survey-reported drinking and actual ambulance harm matters for prevention planning in any state.

Learning goal

Recognise how adolescent alcohol harm persists across gender and remoteness despite falling teen drinking.

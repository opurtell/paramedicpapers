SSEP amplitudes predict good arrest recovery

Combined short- and mid-latency SSEP amplitudes for early prediction of favorable neurological recovery after cardiac arrest
Resuscitation · Added 7 June 2026

They tested whether combined somatosensory evoked potential amplitudes could predict favorable neurological recovery after cardiac arrest and found the combined measure outperformed traditional single-latency assessments.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort
Participants
73 comatose adult cardiac arrest patients post-ROSC with SSEP within 24 h
Main finding
29 (40%) had favourable 6-month outcome; P25-N30 amplitude AUC 0.943 (95% CI 0.862-0.984), 100% sensitivity and 84.1% specificity
Caveat
Very small single-centre retrospective sample
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Learning goal

Weigh the role of somatosensory evoked potentials in early prognostication of comatose post-cardiac arrest patients.

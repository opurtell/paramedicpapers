Mobile app lifts simulated trauma performance

A Mobile Application Improves Paramedic Performance in Simulated Trauma Care: A Crossover Randomized Controlled Trial
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-17 · Added 19 November 2026

A US crossover RCT found paramedic pairs using a mobile app with protocols, decision-support tools and procedure videos completed all critical actions in simulated trauma far more often than with standard practice (35.4% vs 6.3%).

Study type
RCT
Design
Crossover randomised controlled trial, high-fidelity simulation
Participants
48 paramedic pairs analysed (112 recruited), adult and paediatric trauma scenarios
Main finding
35.4% of app-supported scenarios met all critical actions vs 6.3% without (adjusted OR 8.28, 95% CI 2.21–31.09); paediatric completion 30.4% vs 0%; users rated the app practical and valuable.
Caveat
Simulated scenarios rather than patient outcomes, at a single US agency.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Decision-support apps sit on top of clinical practice every Australian crew already carries; the transferable question is integration with existing app-based guidelines, not the technology itself.

Learning goal

  • rSIG cutoff flags trauma patients needing intervention

    BMJ Open · 17 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese single-centre retrospective cohort tested prehospital rSIG (reverse shock index multiplied by GCS) in 747 trauma patients, finding rSIG below 24.8 was associated with resuscitative interventions (adjusted OR 3.17) and 24-hour transfusion (aOR 4.20) in the validation cohort, but not with surgery, interventional radiology or in-hospital mortality.

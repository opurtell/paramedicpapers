Smoking flags wider heart risk in EMS staff

More Than a Tobacco Problem: Cigarette Smoking as a Marker of Poorer Cardiovascular Health Among Emergency Medical Services Clinicians
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-20 · Added 21 November 2026

A US cross-sectional survey analysis of nationally certified EMS clinicians found current smokers had poorer cardiovascular health beyond the tobacco exposure itself — lower adjusted Life's Essential 7 scores (β −6.02) and lower odds of ideal cardiovascular health (aOR 0.37) than never smokers.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional secondary analysis of a voluntary recertification survey
Participants
7,703 EMTs, advanced EMTs and paramedics with complete data (of 36,436 respondents)
Main finding
Current smokers (6.0%) had adjusted mLE7 score −6.02 (95% CI −7.63 to −4.41) and aOR 0.37 for ideal cardiovascular health versus never smokers; former smokers intermediate (aOR 0.73)
Caveat
Voluntary self-reported survey data with selection and recall bias
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A US certified-clinician survey, but workforce cardiovascular health is the same occupational question here, and smoking as a marker of broader risk clusters is relevant to any service's wellbeing programme.

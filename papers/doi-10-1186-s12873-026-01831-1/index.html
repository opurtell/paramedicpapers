DNAR directives missing when crews arrive

Challenges in managing do-not-attempt-resuscitation cases in prehospital settings: a qualitative study of emergency medical technicians in Japan
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-19 · Added 22 November 2026

Japanese survey of 302 EMS personnel found over half had encountered cardiac arrest with a DNAR directive yet 80% still performed basic life support and 91% transported, with thematic analysis pointing to gaps in advance care planning and system rules as the upstream drivers of on-scene conflict.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Mixed-methods survey with reflexive thematic analysis
Participants
302 EMS personnel responses (111 free-text)
Main finding
54.0% had encountered CPA with DNAR; among them 80% performed basic life support and 91% transported; four themes centred on inadequate DNAR decision-making processes, absent accommodating systems, scope-of-practice conflicts with patient wishes, and emotional burden
Caveat
Single Japanese medical-control region, and EMT scope of practice differs from Australian paramedicine
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Japanese crews work under tighter legal constraints than Australian paramedics, but the themes — absent or unclear directives forcing treatment on scene — are recognisable in Australian practice.

Learning goal

Understand how advance care directives shape my resuscitation decisions and communication when patients' documented wishes are unclear.

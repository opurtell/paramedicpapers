One in five severe trauma cases may need prehospital blood

Prehospital transfusion requirements in severe trauma: A multicenter, retrospective estimate of requirements
Injury · Added 13 September 2026

Multicentre French retrospective study applying the ABC score across four Paris-region prehospital services to estimate demand for prehospital blood — 27 of 139 severe trauma patients (19.7%) met ABC ≥2 with prehospital intervention time >20 min and median total prehospital time of 79 min, only 67% of those eligible received TXA, and 5 arrested prehospital, leading the authors to argue prehospital transfusion should not remain a rarity.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Multicentre retrospective observational cohort
Participants
139 severe trauma patients from 5,921 missions across four Paris-region prehospital services
Main finding
27 (19.7%, 95% CI 13.7-26.8%) met ABC ≥2 with prehospital intervention time >20 min; median total intervention time 79 min; 67% of eligible received TXA; five had prehospital cardiac arrest
Caveat
Retrospective with assumed eligibility criteria; French physician-staffed system limits generalisability
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Blood products and TXA are not in the extracted ACTAS formulary — adoption would need a guideline change — though QAS, AV, SJWA and NSWA already carry blood products.

Learning goal

Recognise which severe trauma patients in my practice are likely to need early prehospital blood transfusion.

    Retrospective cohort of 128 frostbite admissions in eastern Turkey — injury depth drove amputation risk (81.5% of third-degree injuries required amputation vs 1.6% of first/second-degree), and combining degree with %TBSA predicted amputation at admission (AUC 0.948), while inflammatory markers added nothing after adjustment.

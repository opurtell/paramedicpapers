Junctional tourniquet harm after pelvic GSW

Between Hemorrhage and Harm: Complications of the Abdominal Aortic and Junctional Tourniquet-Stabilized
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 1 September 2026

Case report of a patient with AAJT-S placed prehospital for pelvic gunshot wounds who suffered cardiac arrest upon device removal and developed metabolic acidosis, compartment syndrome, rhabdomyolysis, renal and liver infarcts, pulmonary emboli, and stroke

Study type
Case series
Design
Case report with literature review
Participants
1 young adult male with pelvic and lower limb gunshot wounds
Main finding
Removal of a prehospital AAJT-S was followed by cardiac arrest and complications including metabolic acidosis, compartment syndrome, rhabdomyolysis, renal and liver infarcts, pulmonary emboli and stroke
Caveat
Single case; confounding by injury severity and shock cannot be excluded
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — Cautionary finding for junctional tourniquet use — AAJT-S isn't specified as ACTAS equipment, but principles apply to non-compressible torso haemorrhage management

Learning goal

Weigh the risks and benefits of junctional tourniquet use in pelvic haemorrhage, including complications at removal.

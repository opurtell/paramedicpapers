AR-guided FAST training sped up students

FAST Performance Changes After a Training Sequence Including Augmented Reality Guidance Among Paramedic Students
Simulation in Healthcare · 2026-11-09 · Added 11 November 2026

A Japanese single-arm training study gave 18 undergraduate paramedic students a two-hour FAST course including augmented-reality-guided scans, and total examination time fell by a median 263.5 seconds with quality scores improving significantly.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Single-arm interventional training study, pre/post design
Participants
18 undergraduate paramedic students
Main finding
Median total FAST time fell 263.5 s (95% CI 177–352.5, P<0.001); Task-Specific Checklist and Global Rating Scale scores both improved (P<0.001); no time gain in the Douglas pouch view
Caveat
No parallel control group and a fixed scan sequence — the authors call the findings cautious
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Point-of-care ultrasound is carried by several Australian services but not most, and this was a small student cohort with no control group

Learning goal

Strengthen my FAST scanning technique, including the Douglas pouch view, when using point-of-care ultrasound in the field.

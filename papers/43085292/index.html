Warming prehospital whole blood: no coagulopathy difference

Prehospital whole blood warming and trauma-induced coagulopathy in combat casualties: A retrospective cohort study
The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-20 · Added 27 November 2026

An Israeli military registry cohort of 209 combat casualties given prehospital low-titre group O whole blood tested whether active blood warming reduced coagulopathy on ED arrival — it made no difference (aOR 1.07) and didn't shift massive transfusion or survival.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective military registry cohort (linked prehospital trauma and national trauma registries)
Participants
209 combat casualties (61 warmed, 148 cold whole blood)
Main finding
Trauma-induced coagulopathy on ED arrival in 21.3% (warmed) vs 27.7% (cold); adjusted OR 1.07 (95% CI 0.45–2.49), with no association with massive transfusion or 24-hour or 30-day survival.
Caveat
Retrospective with non-randomised warming allocation, and combat casualties are young with largely penetrating injury — hard to generalise to civilian road trauma.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is limited to a handful of Australian services, most don't carry it — and this is a young military combat population, so it informs the civilian debate more than current practice.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and limitations of warming prehospital whole blood when managing traumatic haemorrhage in civilian practice.

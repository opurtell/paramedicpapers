Clearer AED voice prompts fix lay-user errors

Competence in the use of a semi-automated external defibrillator among undergraduate dental students: evaluation and proposal for improvement
BMC Medical Education · 2026-07-27 · Added 25 October 2026

A simulated cardiac arrest assessment of 81 final-year Spanish dental students found fewer than 10% correctly performed 8 of 10 AED steps with standard voice prompts (median time to shock 143 s), and a modified set of instructions significantly improved key actions.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Simulation-based before/after assessment with a manikin
Participants
81 final-year dental students
Main finding
With standard AED instructions, fewer than 10% of students correctly completed 8 of 10 assessed items (median time to shock 143 s); modified instructions significantly improved device activation and later steps (P<0.001)
Caveat
Single university, simulated scenario, students are not representative of trained responders
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The respondents are dental students, not paramedics, but lay-responder AED performance is the same weak link in Australian public-access defibrillation — the finding that clearer voice prompts fix real errors is a device-design lesson for the AEDs we depend on before we arrive.

Learning goal

Improve my ability to guide bystanders through AED use during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

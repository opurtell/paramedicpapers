Tabletop MCI exercises: how they're run and measured

Design, Facilitation, and Evaluation of Tabletop Exercises for Prehospital Mass Casualty Preparedness: Scoping Review
Interactive Journal of Medical Research · 2026-10-05 · Added 6 October 2026

Scoping review of 13 studies from 9 countries on tabletop exercises for prehospital mass casualty preparedness — it built a preliminary three-tier 'TTX Design Spectrum' typology and classified outcomes with the Kirkpatrick model (plus an applied-learning level), finding evaluation focused on participant learning with less assessment of applied performance or system outcomes.

Study type
Scoping review
Design
Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR) of prehospital MCI tabletop exercises 2015–2026, outcomes classified by Kirkpatrick model
Participants
13 studies from 9 countries
Main finding
A preliminary three-tier 'TTX Design Spectrum' typology; most evaluation sat at Kirkpatrick levels 1–2 (participant learning), with an added level 2+ (applied learning) category — little assessment of applied performance or system outcomes
Caveat
Scoping review of a small, heterogeneous literature; no outcome-effectiveness conclusions possible
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). tabletop exercises are standard MCI preparedness across Australian services, so the design-and-evaluation framing is useful for anyone running them — but it won't change road practice

Learning goal

Understand how exercise design and evaluation depth shape prehospital mass casualty preparedness in Australian services.

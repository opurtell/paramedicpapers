Most physician-unit dispatches are overtriage

Overtriage of a physician-staffed prehospital emergency resource in Austria : An exploratory retrospective analysis
Wiener Klinische Wochenschrift · 2026-10-15 · Added 17 October 2026

A retrospective Austrian analysis of 3,454 dispatches of a physician-staffed response vehicle found 76% met retrospective overtriage criteria, and dispatch priority didn't predict appropriateness at all.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective single-centre analysis
Participants
3,454 primary dispatches over one year
Main finding
76.1% of dispatches were retrospectively classified as overtriage (38% with no patient contact); priority level showed no association with appropriateness.
Caveat
Retrospective design with appropriateness criteria defined after the fact, at a single service.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). Physician-staffed response vehicles aren't part of the Australian ambulance model — this informs dispatch-accuracy debates generally, not current practice.

Learning goal

Understand how dispatch triage criteria affect appropriate use of advanced prehospital resources in Australian settings.

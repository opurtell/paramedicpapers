Three post-ROSC lab phenotypes predict outcome

Early Post-ROSC Laboratory Phenotypes and Clinical Outcomes after Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A 10-Year National Cohort Study
Resuscitation · 2026-10 · Added 11 October 2026

Latent class analysis of 14 laboratory values drawn within six hours of ROSC in 1,514 resuscitated OHCA patients identified three phenotypes, with the multi-organ dysfunction group at roughly double the odds of poor neurological recovery compared with the others.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective national registry cohort with latent class analysis
Participants
1,514 resuscitated adults with presumed cardiac OHCA
Main finding
Multi-organ dysfunction phenotype had higher odds of poor neurological recovery versus homeostatic (aOR 0.50, 95% CI 0.30–0.78) and immune-dysregulated (aOR 0.46, 95% CI 0.26–0.78) phenotypes, with mortality differences persisting to 3 years
Caveat
Exploratory derivation study from a single country's database, awaiting external validation before guiding therapy
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Phenotyping depends on in-hospital bloods taken within six hours of ROSC, so this shapes post-arrest care pathways and handover conversations more than anything done roadside.

Learning goal

Recognise how early post-ROSC laboratory patterns may identify cardiac arrest patients at risk of poor neurological recovery.

    Resuscitation · 11 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 10,786 EMS-treated OHCAs in a Canadian registry found opioid-associated arrests had similar outcomes to undifferentiated arrests (OR 1.02), but Utstein variables like initial rhythm and witnessed status predicted outcome differently in the opioid group.

  • ITDs in OHCA: early wins, late losses

    Resuscitation · 10 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A 97,439-patient US national EMS sample found impedance threshold device use was associated with better prehospital ROSC (aOR 1.09) but lower survival to discharge (23.9% vs 32.0%, aOR 0.68), with even more extreme trends when combined with active compression-decompression.

  • Unwitnessed arrests: mostly women, mostly at home

    Heart & Lung · 10 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    A systematic review of 22 studies (210,028 people) found female sex and arrest at home were the most consistent determinants of unwitnessed OHCA, with weaker, mostly unadjusted evidence for night or sleep arrests, deprivation and psychiatric illness.

  • CPR injuries common after long arrests

    Resuscitation Plus · 9 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre retrospective cohort of 136 OHCA patients found CPR-related injuries in 43% (16.9% clinically significant), with CPR duration over 20 minutes — not ECPR or mechanical CPR — independently associated with injury (OR 2.45).

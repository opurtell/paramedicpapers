Indirect ICU admissions from Australian ED

Indirect ICU Admissions From an Australian Emergency Department: Risk Factors and Outcomes
Emergency Medicine Australasia · Added 29 August 2026

Australian observational study found indirect ICU admissions (ED admission to non-critical care ward requiring ICU transfer within 24 hours) accounted for 11.3% of ED-to-ICU admissions, with circulatory insufficiency as the most common reason and hospital mortality of 12.8%.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Observational cohort study
Participants
148 indirect ICU admissions at one Australian hospital
Main finding
11.3% of ED-to-ICU admissions were indirect; circulatory insufficiency most common (43%); 53% had normal observations; hospital mortality 12.8%
Caveat
Single-centre, in-hospital ED cohort with small sample
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Australian study directly relevant to local practice—indirect ICU admissions reflect missed deterioration recognition after handover, which has implications for paramedic documentation and communication.

Learning goal

Recognise early signs of circulatory insufficiency in patients whose observations appear normal before ICU deterioration.

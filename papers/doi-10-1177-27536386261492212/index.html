NOF fracture care runs on tacit knowledge

‘These people don’t float into hospital’: Australian paramedic perspectives on pre-hospital neck of femur fracture care
Paramedicine · 2026-09-29 · Added 6 October 2026

Australian exploratory qualitative study running eight online focus groups with 21 registered paramedics on suspected neck of femur fracture care — participants described managing these patients through diagnostic uncertainty, tacit knowledge, peer learning and contextual judgement in the absence of dedicated prehospital guidance, and called for clearer evidence-informed direction covering movement, dignity, communication and analgesia.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Exploratory qualitative study — eight online focus groups with 21 registered paramedics, framework-method analysis
Participants
21 registered paramedics across 8 focus groups
Main finding
Four themes: balancing rapid assessment with diagnostic uncertainty; managing immediate care amid competing priorities; the strengths and limits of the existing knowledge base; and a call for clearer, evidence-informed guidance — participants relied on tacit knowledge, peer learning and accumulated experience
Caveat
Qualitative perspectives from a self-selected survey sample; no patient outcomes measured
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Australian paramedics, Australian context, and suspected NOF fractures are a weekly presentation — the identified uncertainty and the call for dedicated guidance are directly actionable for any Australian service writing clinical practice guidelines

Learning goal

Understand how tacit knowledge and evidence gaps shape my assessment and care decisions for suspected neck of femur fractures.

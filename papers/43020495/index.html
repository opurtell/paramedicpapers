rSIG cutoff flags trauma patients needing intervention

Prehospital Reverse Shock Index multiplied by the Glasgow Coma Scale score (rSIG) as a predictor of urgent interventions in trauma patients: a retrospective cohort study
BMJ Open · 2026-11 · Added 17 November 2026

A Japanese single-centre retrospective cohort tested prehospital rSIG (reverse shock index multiplied by GCS) in 747 trauma patients, finding rSIG below 24.8 was associated with resuscitative interventions (adjusted OR 3.17) and 24-hour transfusion (aOR 4.20) in the validation cohort, but not with surgery, interventional radiology or in-hospital mortality.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Single-centre retrospective observational cohort with derivation and validation arms
Participants
747 trauma patients (505 derivation, 242 validation)
Main finding
Optimal prehospital rSIG cutoff 24.8; lower rSIG associated with resuscitative interventions (aOR 3.17, 95% CI 1.34-7.50) and blood transfusion within 24 hours (aOR 4.20, 95% CI 1.36-12.95); no significant association with surgery or interventional radiology within 24 hours or with in-hospital mortality
Caveat
Single-centre retrospective design with a derived cutoff needing external validation before any clinical use
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). rSIG comes from vital signs and GCS every crew records, so the tool itself travels - but this is one Japanese centre with a derived cutoff and mixed results, not an adopted triage rule.

Learning goal

Weigh the value of prehospital rSIG in recognising trauma patients likely to need urgent resuscitative intervention.

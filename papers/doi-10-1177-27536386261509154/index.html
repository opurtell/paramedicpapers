Paramedic students learn safety from unsafe events

Learning from patient-safety-related events in paramedic education: A qualitative study using the SLERT
Paramedicine · 2026-11-18 · Added 19 November 2026

A Czech qualitative study of 58 undergraduate paramedic students using structured SLERT narratives found patient-safety learning in placements was shaped by role modelling, feedback, emotional processing and reflective meaning-making.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative descriptive study (conventional content analysis)
Participants
58 undergraduate paramedic students
Main finding
Four themes emerged: professional role modelling and feedback, unsafe situations as learning triggers, emotional impact and student vulnerability, and developing patient-safety awareness for future practice.
Caveat
Single-country self-reported student narratives, with no outcome follow-up.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Paramedic students on placements face the same safety pressures here; the findings speak to how Australian educators structure debriefing and safety reflection rather than to clinical practice.

Learning goal

Strengthen my reflective practice so unsafe events and emotional responses on placement deepen my patient-safety awareness.

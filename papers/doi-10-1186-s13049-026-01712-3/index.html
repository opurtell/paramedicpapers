What helps kids in pain in ambulances

What works well for children and young people suffering acute pain attended by ambulance? A realist evaluation
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-11-03 · Added 4 November 2026

A UK realist evaluation across three English ambulance services and two children's EDs — 44 interview transcripts plus 34,068 clinical records — refined the programme theory for paediatric pain care, with clinician preparedness and compassionate person-centred care emerging as the key mechanisms.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Realist evaluation (qualitative interviews, diaries, artwork + routine clinical records)
Participants
44 participants (21 ambulance clinicians, 14 parents/carers, 9 children/young people); 34,068 clinical records
Main finding
Around 60% of children and young people needing an ambulance for a painful condition experience poor pain management; preparedness (education, training, exposure, experience) and compassionate person-centred care were the refined programme theory's central mechanisms
Caveat
Realist evaluation generating theory, not effectiveness evidence, and participants were self-selected
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Paediatric pain is core ambulance work across Australia and the mechanisms described — preparedness, privacy, reassurance, allaying negative assumptions — are actionable in any service, whatever the drug chart looks like.

Learning goal

Improve my paediatric pain assessment and management by strengthening clinician preparedness and delivering compassionate, person-centred care.

  • Two paramedic attitudes to paediatric pain

    Turkish Journal of Emergency Medicine · 13 Oct 2026 · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    Cross-sectional survey of 129 paramedics in a standardised Turkish EMS system used latent class analysis to identify two paediatric analgesia attitudinal profiles — proactive/system-oriented (57.4%) and conservative/low-engagement (42.6%) — unrelated to age, sex or experience.

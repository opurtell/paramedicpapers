Missed prehospital transfusions in paediatric trauma

Missed Opportunities for Prehospital Transfusion in Pediatric Trauma
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-10-19 · Added 7 October 2026

Analysis of 1.66 million US paediatric trauma EMS activations found only 0.1% of children with a positive shock index — those plausibly benefiting from blood — received a prehospital transfusion, suggesting major underuse.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective national EMS registry cohort
Participants
1,663,601 paediatric trauma EMS activations; 363,844 with positive shock index
Main finding
Only 287 of 363,844 shock-index-positive children (0.1%) received prehospital transfusion; 10 of 468 (2%) children with EMS-attended arrest got a hospital transfusion
Caveat
Positive shock index is a crude surrogate for haemorrhagic shock, and NEMSIS coding may undercount transfusions
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is limited to a few Australian services, so this informs the debate more than current practice — and the paediatric evidence base is thinner still, given most prehospital blood programmes stock adult units.

Learning goal

Recognise early signs of haemorrhagic shock in paediatric trauma where prehospital blood transfusion may be warranted.

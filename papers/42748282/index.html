Medetomidine withdrawal transfers on dexmedetomidine

Management of Patients with Medetomidine Withdrawal During Interfacility Transfer
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 22 September 2026

Cohort of 100 interfacility transfers on dexmedetomidine infusions for medetomidine withdrawal — transports were largely uncomplicated but profound hypertension was common (39%), reflecting the growing US opioid-supply adulterant problem

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective observational cohort of interfacility critical care transfers
Participants
100 non-intubated adults receiving dexmedetomidine infusion for suspected medetomidine withdrawal
Main finding
78% hypertensive (SBP >140) and 39% profoundly hypertensive during transfer; 10% needed dexmedetomidine titration, 12% antiemetics, 9% sedatives; no alternate sedative infusions required
Caveat
Retrospective single-agency US cohort; medetomidine adulteration not established in Australia
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). transferability — dexmedetomidine isn't carried by ACTAS and medetomidine adulteration is a US drug-supply phenomenon — relevant only if such an infused patient ever moves through ACT interfacility transfer

Learning goal

Recognise the clinical features and transfer risks of novel opioid-supply adulterant withdrawal syndromes.

Neonatal calls rare on South African ambulances

Infrequent exposure to prehospital neonatal interventions and resource utilisation by a public EMS in the Western Cape, South Africa
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-07-27 · Added 25 October 2026

A two-year review of 9,825 neonatal activations in the Western Cape public EMS found neonates were just 0.7% of workload with rare high-risk interventions (prehospital intubation 0.4%), and non-specialist crews attended two-thirds of high-acuity cases despite limited neonatal scope of practice.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective review of electronic patient care records, 2022–2023
Participants
9,825 neonatal activations
Main finding
Neonatal activations were 0.7% of EMS workload; prehospital intubation 0.4%; recorded mechanical ventilation 4.6% versus sedation/analgesia 1.1%; specialist retrieval teams were strongly associated with advanced interventions
Caveat
Descriptive record review in a low-resource public system — staffing, scope of practice and case mix differ from Australian services
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A low-resource South African public EMS with different scopes of practice, but the core problem is identical in Australia — most paramedics almost never touch a neonate, so skills decay is a shared training challenge.

Learning goal

Strengthen my preparedness for rare high-acuity neonatal cases given limited routine exposure in Australian prehospital practice.

Airway strategy and survival in Polish HEMS

Advanced airway management practice patterns and short-term survival in Poland’s national physician-staffed helicopter emergency medical service, 2020–2024: a retrospective observational study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-10-16 · Added 18 October 2026

A retrospective analysis of Poland's national physician-staffed HEMS registry tested airway strategies in 8,980 intubated adults against survival to hospital handover — HEMS-performed intubation was most common (49.8%), and in non-traumatic cardiac arrest a sequential supraglottic-airway-then-intubation strategy showed no significant difference from HEMS-performed intubation, while some other strategies carried higher unadjusted odds of not being alive at handover.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective national HEMS registry cohort
Participants
8,980 adults requiring advanced airway management
Main finding
HEMS-performed intubation was the most common strategy (49.8%); sequential supraglottic-to-intubation did not differ significantly from HEMS-performed intubation in non-traumatic cardiac arrest; 32.9% were not alive at hospital handover
Caveat
Univariate analyses only, so strategy effects are confounded by patient severity, and the physician-staffed setting limits direct comparison with paramedic-only systems
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Delivered by a physician-staffed HEMS crew, a model Australian services don't run — but the questions it speaks to (intubation vs supraglottic first, and who performs the intubation) sit squarely in the intensive care paramedic skill set across Australia.

Learning goal

Weigh a sequential supraglottic airway approach against primary intubation when managing advanced airways in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

Physician burnout dimension linked to self-rated health

Self-Rated Health and Burnout Dimensions Among Prehospital Emergency Medical Services Physicians: A Cross-Sectional Study
Behavioral Sciences · 2026-09-24 · Added 28 October 2026

A Serbian cross-sectional study of 151 prehospital emergency physicians using the Maslach Burnout Inventory found higher emotional exhaustion was associated with less favourable self-rated health (OR per 10-point increase 1.31, 95% CI 1.04-1.64), with depersonalisation and reduced personal accomplishment showing no clear adjusted associations.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional study, ordinal regression models, convenience sample
Participants
151 prehospital emergency physicians, one Belgrade EMS
Main finding
Higher emotional exhaustion was associated with less favourable self-rated health (OR per 10-point increase 1.31, 95% CI 1.04-1.64); the association weakened after mutual adjustment (OR 1.30, 95% CI 0.96-1.75); depersonalisation and reduced personal accomplishment showed no clear associations
Caveat
Cross-sectional convenience sample from a single physician-staffed service — no conclusions about direction or causality
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Serbia crews its ambulances with physicians, but the emotional exhaustion and perceived health link is workforce-wellbeing territory that translates to paramedic crews, whose burnout research shows the same pattern.

Learning goal

Understand how emotional exhaustion relates to how paramedics perceive their own health and wellbeing.

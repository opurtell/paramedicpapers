One in five transports: brief stay, no intervention

Short hospital contacts without documented in-hospital intervention following emergency medical services transport in the Central Denmark Region: a population-based cohort study
BMJ Open · 2026-10-13 · Added 16 October 2026

Danish population-based cohort of 89,560 emergency calls found 19.1% of transported patients had a hospital stay of six hours or less with no documented in-hospital intervention, with short-term mortality of just 0.4% in that group.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Population-based cohort study
Participants
89,560 EMS-attended emergency calls, Central Denmark Region 2022–2023
Main finding
15.1% of patients were non-conveyed; among the 84.9% transported, 19.1% had a ≤6-hour stay with no documented procedure, medication or imaging, and short-term mortality was 0.4% versus 1.3% for stays over six hours
Caveat
Short-term (3-day) mortality only in a single Danish region, and a short stay without intervention doesn't necessarily mean the transport was unnecessary
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The low-acuity transport question is live in every Australian service, though this comes from a single Danish tax-funded system with linked national hospital data.

Learning goal

Understand how low-acuity transports without documented intervention shape my conveyance decisions and alternative care pathways.

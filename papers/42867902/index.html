Dispatcher CPR and survival in shockable OHCA

From call to home: The role of dispatcher-assisted CPR in patient survival
Physiology International · 2026-10-13 · Added 15 October 2026

Nationwide Hungarian cohort of 3,674 shockable OHCAs found dispatcher-assisted CPR ongoing at ambulance arrival was associated with far better outcomes than crew-initiated CPR — 30-day survival 54.6% vs 7.1% and one-year survival 45.8% vs 6.2%.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective nationwide observational cohort, Utstein-style reporting
Participants
3,674 adults with VF/VT OHCA
Main finding
DA-CPR ongoing at arrival vs crew-initiated: ROSC 92.0% vs 12.4%, hospital admission with sustained circulation 90.8% vs 11.7%, 30-day survival 54.6% vs 7.1%, one-year survival 45.8% vs 6.2% (all P < 0.001)
Caveat
Massive confounding by indication — DA-CPR still running at arrival marks callers who started bystander CPR early, not a randomised comparison
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Dispatcher-assisted CPR is standard practice across Australian ambulance services, so this is about doing it well rather than adopting anything new.

Learning goal

Strengthen my telephone coaching of bystander CPR so chest compressions begin earlier in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

Prehospital antibiotics earlier in sepsis

Association Between Early Prehospital Administration Antibiotic and 28-Day Mortality in Sepsis
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 22 September 2026

Thai observational cohort of 364 adults with suspected sepsis testing prehospital antibiotics — prehospital administration was associated with lower adjusted 28-day mortality (ARR 8.9%, 95% CI 0.8–17.1; aOR 0.51) and antibiotics ~125 minutes sooner, but scene times ran 8.5 minutes longer.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Single-centre observational cohort
Participants
364 adults with suspected sepsis
Main finding
Prehospital antibiotics (154/364) associated with lower 28-day mortality (25.9% vs 28.1%; adjusted ARR 8.9%, 95% CI 0.8–17.1; aOR 0.51) and antibiotics given ~125 minutes sooner, at the cost of ~8.5 minutes longer scene time
Caveat
Single-centre observational design; residual confounding likely despite adjustment
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Single-centre Thai observational design with residual confounding likely; ACTAS CMG 38 (Sepsis) has no prehospital antibiotic step — ceftriaxone is scoped to suspected meningococcal disease (CMG 38A) only, so adoption would need a guideline change.

Learning goal

Weigh the survival benefit of prehospital antibiotics in sepsis against the added scene time before hospital arrival.

  • Prehospital qSOFA adds a little prognostic signal

    Journal of Clinical Medicine · 17 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Turkish retrospective cohort tested whether the prehospital qSOFA already recorded on the chart adds prognostic information for ambulance-transported suspected infection — it predicted 30-day mortality within every ED triage stratum but added almost nothing to a full arrival model (ΔAUC 0.007).

  • EMS sepsis suspicion speeds up ED antibiotics

    Wiener Klinische Wochenschrift · 15 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Single-centre Austrian cohort of 134 ED sepsis admissions found EMS documentation of sepsis suspicion was linked to antibiotics within 1 hour in 63.3% vs 19.2% of patients (aOR 5.77) and a median time-to-antibiotics of 32 vs 124.5 minutes.

  • Sepsis intubations: haemodynamic trouble common

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 9 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    A retrospective analysis of the ANZEDAR registry (79 hospitals, 2010–26) covering 280 sepsis intubations found haemodynamic complications in 27.9% and peri-intubation cardiac arrest in 4.6%, with less waveform capnography use than non-sepsis intubations.

  • Eight sepsis subgroups found, SENECA not replicated

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 5 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective consensus clustering of 953 ED sepsis admissions at Oslo University Hospital could not replicate the four SENECA phenotypes, instead finding eight stable subgroups with more granular characteristics — and only 49% of patients meeting SENECA inclusion criteria fulfilled Sepsis-3, underlining sepsis heterogeneity and cohort-definition problems.

Prehospital blood linked to arrival hypocalcaemia in trauma

Prehospital factors associated with calcium derangements upon arrival to the trauma center from scene - A subanalysis of a multicenter prospective study
The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · 2026-09-23 · Added 29 October 2026

A US prospective multicentre subanalysis of 1,047 trauma patients brought by EMS found calcium derangements on arrival associated with lower survival, with 56.6% of the 136 patients given prehospital blood hypocalcaemic on arrival and 5 hypercalcaemic patients having received prehospital calcium.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Preplanned subanalysis of a prospective multicentre observational study
Participants
1,047 EMS-transported trauma patients at 3 US trauma centres
Main finding
235 patients hypocalcaemic and 39 hypercalcaemic on arrival, both associated with lower survival; 77 of 136 (56.6%) who received prehospital blood were hypocalcaemic; 5 hypercalcaemic patients had received prehospital calcium, suggesting possible iatrogenic hypercalcaemia.
Caveat
Subgroup analysis confounded by indication — the sickest patients were the ones given blood, and calcium was not measured before arrival.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is limited to a few Australian services, so this informs the debate more than current practice; prehospital calcium itself is not something Australian ambulances carry.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on when prehospital blood products may drive calcium derangements in major trauma.

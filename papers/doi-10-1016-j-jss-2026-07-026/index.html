Prehospital whole blood improves shock physiology

Prehospital Whole Blood Administration in Adults With Hemorrhagic Shock and Its Association With Shock Physiology and Clinical Outcomes: A Systematic Review
Journal of Surgical Research · Added 16 August 2026

Systematic review of 13 studies found prehospital whole blood consistently improved shock physiology (greater SI reduction, higher shock reversal rates) and showed potential survival benefit in high-risk and penetrating trauma patients, though overall mortality benefit was not uniform.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review
Participants
13 studies of adults with haemorrhagic shock
Main finding
Mortality differences were not significant (24-h: 15.8% vs 11.2%; 28-day: 25.0% vs 26.1%, P=0.85), but whole blood was consistently associated with greater shock index reduction (0.43 vs 0.10, P<0.05) and potential survival benefit in select high-risk populations
Caveat
Variable mortality findings across heterogeneous studies; no uniform survival benefit demonstrated
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Directly relevant to prehospital blood product protocols, growing evidence base with clear physiologic advantages over component therapy.

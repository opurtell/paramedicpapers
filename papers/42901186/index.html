Dispatcher training + volunteer app lifts bystander CPR

A multifaceted intervention to increase bystander CPR rates after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in France: a stepped-wedge, cluster-randomised, controlled trial
The Lancet. Public Health · 2026-11 · Added 22 October 2026

A stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial across 14 French EMS agencies found dispatcher training in arrest recognition, telephone CPR and a geolocated volunteer responder app increased bystander CPR from 61.4% to 70.9% (adjusted OR 1.54), though survival did not improve.

Study type
RCT
Design
Stepped-wedge cluster-randomised controlled trial
Participants
1,684 adults with non-traumatic OHCA across 14 French EMS agencies
Main finding
Bystander CPR before EMS arrival rose from 61.4% to 70.9% (adjusted OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.09–2.19), with no improvement in survival to discharge or neurological outcome
Caveat
The effect was no longer significant after adjustment for cluster-specific secular trends or baseline characteristics, and it did not translate into better clinical outcomes
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Dispatch-assisted CPR and community responder schemes are core business for every Australian ambulance service, and this is a large prospectively registered randomised trial.

Learning goal

Understand how dispatcher training and volunteer responder systems influence bystander CPR and survival outcomes in cardiac arrest care.

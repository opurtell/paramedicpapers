Mild hypercapnia during ECPR in pigs

Effects of systemic mild hypercapnia on brain function in a refractory cardiac arrest model treated with extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation
Resuscitation Plus · Added 16 September 2026

Porcine refractory-VF arrest model (n=12) testing mild hypercapnia (PaCO2 50-55 mmHg) during ECPR — the hypercapnia group showed higher cerebral glucose (p=0.01) and lower caspase-8 expression, but no significant difference in brain tissue oxygen tension (49.9 vs 36.4 mmHg, p=0.13), intracranial pressure, EEG activity or serum brain-injury biomarkers.

Study type
Other
Design
Randomised animal experimental study (porcine refractory VF arrest with ECPR)
Participants
12 pigs (6 normocapnia, 6 hypercapnia)
Main finding
Hypercapnia group had higher cerebral glucose (p=0.01) and lower caspase-8 expression, but no significant difference in brain tissue oxygen tension (49.9 vs 36.4 mmHg, p=0.13), intracranial pressure, EEG activity or serum brain-injury biomarkers.
Caveat
Small animal model with only 6 animals per group; limited generalisability to human OHCA.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). Animal lab model with molecular surrogate endpoints; ECPR is an ECMO-centre therapy well outside ACTAS prehospital scope.

Learning goal

Weigh the evidence for targeting mild hypercapnia during resuscitation to protect the brain after cardiac arrest.

Children's participation in ambulance care depends on clinicians

Child representatives’ conceptions of children's participation in prehospital emergency care: A phenomenographic study
Paramedicine · 2026-11-12 · Added 13 November 2026

A Swedish qualitative interview study of ambulance child representatives found children's participation in prehospital emergency care conceived as shaped chiefly by clinicians' attitudes and experience, then by the child's age and situational urgency, and constrained by the adult-oriented ambulance environment.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative phenomenographic interview study
Participants
Child representatives in the Swedish ambulance service (number not reported)
Main finding
Three hierarchical conceptions: participation shaped by ambulance clinicians' attitudes and experience, conditioned by age, developmental level and urgency, and influenced by the adult-oriented ambulance environment; participation was more constrained in very young children and time-critical situations.
Caveat
Interviews with child representatives only — not children, parents or frontline ambulance clinicians.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Interviews from a Swedish ambulance service, but communicating with children on scene is the same challenge for crews anywhere.

Learning goal

Recognise how my communication style and the ambulance environment affect children's participation in their own emergency care.

FDSV rarely detected in virtual ED

Family, Domestic and Sexual Violence Presentations to a Virtual Emergency Department: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Emergency Medicine Australasia · Added 4 August 2026

Retrospective cohort of Victoria's Virtual ED — only 21 FDSV presentations identified among 173,936 visits (0.012%), suggesting significant under-detection

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective cohort study of Victorian Virtual Emergency Department presentations
Participants
173,936 VVED presentations, 21 FDSV cases identified
Main finding
FDSV presentations were 0.012% of all visits; all female, median age 37 years; intimate partner violence most common (81%); head/face/neck injuries most frequent (53%); 76% referred by healthcare professionals
Caveat
Cases identified retrospectively via documentation, so figures likely reflect under-detection rather than true prevalence
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). transferability — Direct Australian context, virtual EDs are expanding, and paramedics need awareness that FDSV presentations may be missed in telehealth settings

Learning goal

Recognise how family, domestic and sexual violence may present in virtual care settings where disclosure is often missed.

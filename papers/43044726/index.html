MIRA-29: early prehospital prognosis after arrest

Early Prehospital Prediction of Neurological Outcome After Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest Derivation and External Validation of the MIRA-29 Score
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 19 November 2026

A French derivation-and-validation study built the MIRA-29 score from five prehospital variables and externally validated it on 82,696 registry patients, identifying poor neurological outcome with 0.99 specificity.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Prospective derivation cohort with external registry validation
Participants
1,099 derivation patients plus 82,696 external validation patients
Main finding
Low-flow duration, shock count, adrenaline dose and arrest site predicted neurological outcome; precision 0.86 and recall 0.88 in external validation, specificity for poor outcome 0.99; age was not retained in the final model.
Caveat
Derived and validated in a physician-staffed French system, and highly accurate poor-outcome flags raise futility-decision questions that discrimination statistics alone don't answer.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The variables (low-flow time, shock count, adrenaline dose, arrest site) are collected by paramedics everywhere, but the score grew out of a physician-staffed French system and would need local validation before touching termination-of-resuscitation decisions.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement on using early prehospital prognostic factors when considering futility after cardiac arrest.

Blood stored on ambulances stays within limits to day 42

Prolonged Onboard Storage of Red Blood Cells in Ground Ambulances for Prehospital Transfusion: A Real-World Study
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-19 · Added 21 November 2026

A French randomised experimental study stored 12 red cell units continuously in operational ground ambulances for 35 days and found all units stayed within the European 0.8% haemolysis limit at day 42, though ambulance-stored units showed more haemolysis and storage-induced microerythrocytes than blood-bank controls.

Study type
RCT
Design
Randomised controlled experimental study of blood units (physician-staffed French EMS conditions)
Participants
26 leukoreduced RBC units (12 onboard ambulance, 14 blood bank)
Main finding
All units below the 0.8% haemolysis threshold at day 42, but ambulance storage gave higher haemolysis at day 28 (+0.043 pp) and day 42 (+0.096 pp) and extra microerythrocyte accumulation, mostly between days 28 and 42
Caveat
Sample too small to exclude moderate or larger differences, and no patient outcomes were measured
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital blood is carried by a handful of Australian services and most don't, so this speaks to the practicality debate rather than everyday practice; a French physician-staffed EMS, but the storage question is the same anywhere blood rides a truck.

Learning goal

Weigh the risks and benefits of storing red blood cells onboard ambulances for prehospital transfusion readiness.

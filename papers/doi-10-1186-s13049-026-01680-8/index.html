Why ambulance workplace violence occurs

Workplace violence from the ambulance personnel's perspective, a qualitative study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 26 August 2026

Qualitative study with Swedish ambulance personnel identified three themes driving workplace violence: personnel lack of competence and willingness to adapt, inability to cope with emotional responses to provocation, and limited control in unpredictable situations involving intoxicated or unpredictable patients.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative focus group study with reflexive thematic analysis
Participants
10 Swedish ambulance personnel in 3 focus groups
Main finding
Three themes: personnel lack of competence/willingness to adapt, inability to cope with emotional responses to provocation, and limited control in unpredictable situations (e.g. intoxicated patients)
Caveat
Small Swedish sample of personnel who personally experienced violence; findings may not transfer to Australian systems
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Swedish ambulance service context differs from Australian models in staffing, scope of practice, and patient demographics, though the themes of situational control and emotional coping translate broadly to prehospital work.

Learning goal

Understand how emotional responses and limited situational control contribute to workplace violence risk in paramedic practice.

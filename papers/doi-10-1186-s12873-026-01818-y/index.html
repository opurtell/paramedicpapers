Mass casualty care: slower but no deadlier

Prehospital management and hospital outcomes of severely injured patients during routine civilian mass casualty incidents: a 1:4 matched cohort analysis of the TraumaRegister DGU®
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-10-20 · Added 22 October 2026

A matched German trauma-registry cohort (423 severely injured MCI patients vs 1,692 routine controls) found mass-casualty patients waited longer on scene (85 vs 67 minutes) and received more intubation and tranexamic acid, yet in-hospital mortality was similar (13.5% vs 11.7%).

Study type
Cohort
Design
1:4 matched registry cohort
Participants
423 severely injured MCI patients matched to 1,692 controls
Main finding
Median prehospital time was 85 vs 67 minutes; prehospital intubation (33.6% vs 20.9%) and TXA (32.1% vs 15.0%) were more frequent, but in-hospital mortality did not differ significantly
Caveat
Observational matching could not fully balance injury severity, which was higher in the MCI group
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Mass-casualty triage, on-scene time and transport decisions are core ambulance planning everywhere, though the German physician-staffed and helicopter-heavy response model differs from Australian MCI plans.

Learning goal

Weigh the impact of prolonged on-scene times on outcomes for severely injured patients in mass casualty incidents.

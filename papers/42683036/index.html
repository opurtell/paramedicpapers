What makes paramedic teleconsultation work

Closing the gap: staff perspectives on the effectiveness of teleconsultation in pre-hospital emergency care ‐ a qualitative study
British Paramedic Journal · 2026-09-01 · Added 29 August 2026

Qualitative study of 12 UK critical care and clinical advisory paramedics on teleconsultation — its effectiveness hinges on a 'teleconsultation competence gap' in training, organisational culture and whether governance feels punitive rather than supportive.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative — critical realist thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews
Participants
12 paramedics in critical care and clinical advisory roles
Main finding
Three mechanisms shaped effectiveness: a teleconsultation competence gap in education, environmental determinism (culture, leadership, workload), and an autonomy-governance balance.
Caveat
Small purposive sample from a single large NHS trust of experienced critical care paramedics — perspectives of frontline paramedics may differ.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian services run clinician consultation and electronic support desks with the same governance and training tensions, though the interviewed roles were UK critical-care specific.

Learning goal

Understand how competence, culture and governance shape effective teleconsultation in my prehospital practice.

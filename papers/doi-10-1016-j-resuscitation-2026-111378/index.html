Wearables detecting cardiac arrest: systematic review

Wearable-based automated cardiac arrest detection and alerting: A systematic review
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 3 November 2026

This systematic review of wearable-based automated cardiac arrest detection found 13 studies (predominantly wristbands using photoplethysmography) with sensitivities of 43.8–100%, ten reporting ≥90%, but false alert rates ranging from multiple per day to one per 21 user-years; the multinational evidence base remains largely unvalidated in real life.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review (PRISMA) of diagnostic accuracy studies of wearable cardiac arrest detection systems
Participants
13 studies, with 18 to 1,423 participants each
Main finding
Sensitivity ranged from 43.8% to 100%, with ten studies reporting ≥90%; false-positive alert rates ranged from multiple per day to one per 21 user-years; wristbands (n=9) and photoplethysmography (n=9) were the most common device and sensor types.
Caveat
External validation and real-life performance data are scarce, and study populations were small and heterogeneous, so reported sensitivity and false-alarm rates may not reflect actual EMS-linked use.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Wearables that alert EMS to unwitnessed cardiac arrest could shorten time to bystander CPR and dispatch across Australia, but until devices are externally validated and false-alarm burdens are resolved, Australian paramedics should treat this as an emerging technology rather than current practice.

Learning goal

Understand how wearable cardiac arrest detection technology may shape early warning and community response pathways.

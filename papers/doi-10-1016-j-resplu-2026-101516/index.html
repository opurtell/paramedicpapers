Family CPR helps — but strangers' CPR helps more

Association Between Family Member Bystander CPR and Survival for Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest
Resuscitation Plus · 2026-10 · Added 8 October 2026

US CARES registry cohort of 824,960 OHCAs comparing family-member vs non-family bystander CPR — both beat no bystander CPR, but bystanders outside the family achieved notably higher survival.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective registry cohort (multivariable mixed-effects logistic regression)
Participants
824,960 non-traumatic OHCAs, 2013-2024
Main finding
Survival to discharge was 9.6% with family-member bystander CPR (aOR 1.31 vs none) and 17.3% with non-family bystander CPR (aOR 1.58 vs none); non-family CPR beat family CPR directly (aOR 1.21), with the same pattern for favourable neurological survival
Caveat
Observational registry — who witnesses the arrest and who steps in is entangled with arrest circumstances, so the family/non-family gap may be partly confounded
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Most arrests happen at home in front of family, so the gap between who is present and who does effective CPR is a community training problem every Australian service shares.

Learning goal

Understand why arrests witnessed by family receive bystander CPR less effectively and how community systems can close that gap.

  • Only a third of arrests get bystander CPR

    Heart, Lung & Circulation · 8 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    Systematic review and meta-analysis of 229 studies quantifying global bystander CPR rates in OHCA — just under a third of patients get bystander CPR, with arrests at home 28% less likely to receive it than public-location arrests.

  • Amiodarone after non-shockable-to-shockable conversion

    Resuscitation · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    German Resuscitation Registry propensity-matched cohort testing whether amiodarone helps OHCA patients whose initial non-shockable rhythm converts to shockable — it was associated with better 24-hour and survival-to-discharge, though not clearly better neurological outcome.

  • Long resuscitations don't rule out good recovery

    Resuscitation · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Danish national registry study of bystander-witnessed OHCA survivors asking whether longer time-to-ROSC means worse long-term outcomes — anoxic brain damage rose with time, but long-term survival, return to work and nursing-home admission did not, showing prolonged resuscitation doesn't preclude good recovery.

  • Bystander AEDs work — when someone actually grabs one

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Korean multicentre OHCA registry analysis estimating bystander AED effectiveness against a no-defibrillation reference — AED application was associated with outcomes comparable to or better than EMS shocks, but was applied in only a fraction of eligible cases, especially at home.

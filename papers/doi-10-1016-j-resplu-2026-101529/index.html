Bedside observations beat NEWS for spotting deterioration

Changes in vital signs of hospitalized patients may not identify the need to escalate care as well as other bedside observations
Resuscitation Plus · 2026-10 · Added 12 October 2026

Retrospective analysis of 4,581 rapid response team callouts testing whether eight equipment-free bedside observations identify dying patients better than NEWS — a model built from them outperformed NEWS for predicting death within 24 hours (AUC 0.790 vs 0.707).

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data on rapid response team arrivals
Participants
4,581 adults in general medical, surgical or intermediate care units at a 450-bed US regional centre
Main finding
Eight equipment-free observations predicted death within 24 hours — jaundice, supplemental oxygen need, capillary refill time, cold skin, distressed breathing, altered mentation, dry skin and cyanosis — and a model of all eight beat NEWS (AUC 0.790 vs 0.707, p < 0.0001)
Caveat
Single US centre, retrospective design, and 24-hour death is a narrow surrogate for 'needs escalation'
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). In-hospital rapid response data, but the observation set — capillary refill, skin temperature, cyanosis, mentation, work of breathing — is the prehospital sick-patient assessment paramedics already do without a monitor.

Learning goal

Strengthen my recognition of equipment-free bedside signs that signal clinical deterioration beyond standard vital sign scores.

Paramedic training problems vary by placement setting

Improving emergency medical services in the context of paramedic training: a phenomenological study
BMC Medical Education · 2026-10-26 · Added 28 October 2026

A Turkish phenomenological study of fifteen paramedic participants found problems in paramedic training and service delivery varied by setting — station issues were linked to the system itself while emergency department problems were tied to patients and staff attitudes — with large student numbers negatively affecting both education and service delivery.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative phenomenological design, semi-structured interviews, thematic analysis
Participants
15 paramedic and student participants, Turkey
Main finding
10 sub-themes across 36 codes: station problems were linked to the system itself, emergency department problems to patients and staff attitudes, and high student numbers were seen as harming both education and service delivery
Caveat
Single-country qualitative study of 15 participants in a different training structure; no outcome measures
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Turkey's paramedic model and student volumes differ from Australian pathways, but the placement-setting split — system problems at stations versus interpersonal problems in EDs — is a familiar tension in Australian clinical placement discussions.

Learning goal

Understand how placement setting and student supervision capacity shape the quality of paramedic clinical education.

Prehospital NEWS beats shock index in trauma

Prehospital national early warning score outperforms shock index in predicting mortality and resource utilisation in adult trauma: A systematic review
Injury · Added 16 August 2026

Systematic review of 22 studies (184 to 1.4M patients) found prehospital NEWS showed excellent discrimination for short-term mortality (AUROC 0.87-0.96) and reliably predicted transfusion needs, outperforming Shock Index.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review of prognostic accuracy studies
Participants
22 studies, 184 to 1,439,221 adult trauma patients
Main finding
Prehospital NEWS showed excellent discrimination for short-term mortality (AUROC 0.87-0.96) and predicted transfusion and resource use; shock index performed worse (AUROC 0.48-0.79), with NEWS outperforming SI across all mortality endpoints
Caveat
Substantial heterogeneity in study designs, populations and outcome measures meant only narrative synthesis was possible
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Directly relevant to prehospital trauma triage, large evidence base across diverse EMS systems with clear clinical application.

Learning goal

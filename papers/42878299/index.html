Guideline updates changed prehospital birth care

Progression of emergency obstetric care for unplanned out-of-hospital births: contributions from a 13-year prospective multicenter study in France
Emergencias · 2026-10 · Added 22 September 2026

A 13-year French registry of 3,473 unplanned out-of-hospital births attended by 79 EMS centres found guideline dissemination drove practice change — more prophylactic oxytocin, less routine suctioning — but response times lengthened and neonatal thermal protection worsened.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective analysis of a prospective national registry
Participants
3,473 unplanned out-of-hospital births across 79 EMS centres (2011–2024)
Main finding
Prophylactic oxytocin use rose while nasopharyngeal suctioning and in-situ temperature measurement fell; more newborns received no thermal protection measures (P = .045) and call-to-team arrival times increased
Caveat
Registry data without clinical outcome linkage, and the physician-led mobile ICU model differs from Australian crewing
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A physician-staffed system, but the clinical questions — oxytocin after birth, neonatal warming — are ones paramedics here deliver, so the practice-change lessons transfer even if the staffing model does not.

Learning goal

Strengthen my management of neonatal thermoregulation at unplanned out-of-hospital births.

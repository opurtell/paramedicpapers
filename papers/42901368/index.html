Pad application is the AED time bottleneck

Identifying the Operational Bottleneck to Shock Delivery After Automated External Defibrillator Activation
Resuscitation · 2026-10 · Added 22 October 2026

Timestamps from 8,115 remotely monitored Japanese AEDs showed pad application consumed a median 81% of the power-on-to-first-analysis interval, with completion inside about 50 seconds marking shocks delivered within 60 seconds of power-on.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective analysis of remotely monitored AED timestamps
Participants
8,115 AED activation events (900 initially shock-advised with recorded shock)
Main finding
Median power-on-to-pad-application was 42 seconds, accounting for 81% of the power-on-to-first-analysis interval; a threshold of ≥51 seconds identified shock delivery beyond 60 seconds
Caveat
Device log data only, with no linkage to patient outcomes or rescuer type
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Public access defibrillation performance is the same problem everywhere — the device workflow, not the health system, is the bottleneck.

Learning goal

Strengthen my ability to minimise delays between AED power-on and pad application during cardiac arrest resuscitation.

  • Keep pulse checks under 5 seconds

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A 252-patient multicentre study in Türkiye found pulse checks completed within 5 seconds were associated with roughly double the ROSC rate of checks lasting 6–10 seconds (36.3% vs 19.5%, OR 2.39), alongside better capnographic dynamics.

  • Fatigue common a year after cardiac arrest

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    A Victorian population-based cohort of 728 out-of-hospital cardiac arrest survivors found fatigue in 35.8%, anxiety in 22.6% and depression in 18.0% at 12 months, with heavier symptom burden linked to worse quality of life, function and return to work.

  • Physician-directed ALS: no survival gain

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    A 129,207-case Korean difference-in-differences study found regions adopting video physician-directed prehospital ALS for cardiac arrest had modestly higher prehospital ROSC (+1.35 percentage points) but no gain in survival or neurological outcome, with prehospital times stretching about 4 minutes.

  • Dispatcher training + volunteer app lifts bystander CPR

    The Lancet. Public Health · 22 Oct 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial across 14 French EMS agencies found dispatcher training in arrest recognition, telephone CPR and a geolocated volunteer responder app increased bystander CPR from 61.4% to 70.9% (adjusted OR 1.54), though survival did not improve.

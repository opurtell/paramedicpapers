Instrumented manikin neck for immobilisation training

Six-degree-of-freedom manikin neck for immobilisation training, incorporating linear potentiometers for real-time motion feedback
Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers. Part H, Journal of Engineering in Medicine · 2026-10-25 · Added 26 October 2026

Australian engineers built and tested a proof-of-concept instrumented manikin neck with six-degree-of-freedom motion and real-time head-position feedback for immobilisation training, achieving angle measurement error under 0.89° versus motion capture.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Device design, manufacture and bench validation
Participants
One manikin neck prototype (bench-tested against motion capture)
Main finding
The prototype delivered six-degree-of-freedom neck motion with realistic low stiffness and head-angle accuracy within 0.89° RMSE versus optoelectronic motion capture
Caveat
Proof-of-concept only: range of motion and stiffness were limited by the build, and it hasn't been evaluated in actual paramedic training
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). A device prototype, not evidence about patient care — interesting for training programs, but nothing here changes what crews do yet, and the authors note it needs work before paramedic training settings.

Learning goal

Improve my spinal immobilisation technique by understanding how real-time feedback on neck movement reveals excess motion during handling.

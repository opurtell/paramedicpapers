Delays in NSW paediatric trauma retrieval

Getting it Right in Pediatric Trauma Inter-Hospital Retrieval: Experience of a Pediatric Specialist Team in New South Wales, Australia
Air Medical Journal · 2026-10 · Added 16 October 2026

Ten-year review of paediatric trauma retrievals by NSW's specialist paediatric transport service found helicopter launch times worsened after 2017 operational changes and teams took over 10 minutes to depart the referring hospital in 80% of missions.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective observational study
Participants
Children aged 0–15 retrieved by NETS NSW to paediatric trauma centres, 2010–2019 (number not reported)
Main finding
Median team departure from base was 10 minutes by day and 17 at night; time from team departure to helicopter launch worsened from a median of 17 minutes (epoch 1) to 30 (epoch 2, p = .03), and 80% of missions took >10 minutes to leave after stabilisation
Caveat
Single-service retrospective operational data ending in 2019, with no patient outcomes reported
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Australian paediatric retrieval data with operational lessons — launch delays and slow departures from referring hospitals — that apply to any coordinated retrieval service.

Learning goal

Understand how system and coordination factors contribute to delays in paediatric trauma inter-hospital retrieval.

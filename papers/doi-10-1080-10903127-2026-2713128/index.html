High adverse event rate after prehospital RSI

The Prevalence of Adverse Events After Prehospital Neuromuscular Blockade-Assisted Airway Management
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 4 August 2026

Investigates the prevalence of adverse events following prehospital airway management using neuromuscular blockade

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional analysis of 2023 NEMSIS national dataset
Participants
11,525 adult prehospital neuromuscular blockade-assisted airway cases
Main finding
27.5% had at least one adverse event within 10 minutes: hypoxemia 25.0%, hypotension 15.0%, bradycardia 2.7%, cardiac arrest 1.9%; first pass success 80.6%; abnormal pre-blockade vitals markedly increased odds (e.g. hypotension OR 16.8, 95% CI 13.1-21.8)
Caveat
Administrative dataset with no chart-level verification; excludes cardiac arrest cases; US data only
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). No abstract available — cannot assess safety outcomes or transferability without full text

Learning goal

Recognise how abnormal pre-intubation vitals predict adverse events after prehospital rapid sequence intubation.

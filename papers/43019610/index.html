Paramedics miss many non-convulsive seizures

Prehospital recognition and differentiation of seizure presentations by paramedics and ambulance nurses: A scoping review
Seizure · 2026-10-30 · Added 14 November 2026

An Australian scoping review of 18 studies found paramedic recognition of seizure presentations remains suboptimal, with sensitivity of 54.0–58.5% and non-recognition of non-convulsive seizures reaching 53.0–81.4%.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Scoping review (PRISMA-ScR) of 18 studies across five databases
Participants
18 studies (16 paramedic, 2 ambulance nurse) from 1,264 citations
Main finding
Sensitivity 54.0–58.5% and specificity 95.9–96.6% against variable reference standards; recognition rates 56.8–89.7%; non-convulsive seizures missed in 53.0–81.4% of cases
Caveat
Included studies used variable reference standards and EMS workforce configurations, limiting pooled interpretation
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Diagnostic accuracy of seizure recognition is a crew-level problem everywhere, and the review covers paramedic practice directly comparable to Australian scope.

Learning goal

Recognise non-convulsive seizure presentations in the field to reduce missed diagnoses.

Hot days, more injury calls to ambulance services

Apparent temperature, heat waves, and unintentional injury-related emergency medical service calls: A case-crossover study
Ecotoxicology and Environmental Safety · 2026-11-13 · Added 15 November 2026

A Shanghai case-crossover study of 170,538 unintentional injury-related EMS calls found extreme apparent temperature (42.5 °C vs −2.9 °C) was associated with 21% higher odds of injury calls, peaking at 80% higher for traffic injuries.

Study type
Case-control
Design
Time-stratified case-crossover with distributed lag models
Participants
170,538 unintentional injury-related EMS calls, July 2021 – June 2025
Main finding
Cumulative OR 1.21 (95% CI 1.12–1.31) comparing the 99th percentile apparent temperature (42.5 °C) with the exposure minimum; traffic injuries OR 1.80 (95% CI 1.56–2.07); adults 18–64 OR 1.38; heat waves OR 1.07
Caveat
Single Shanghai district, and comparisons across heat-wave definitions and exposure windows were exploratory
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Shanghai demand data, but heat-driven injury call surges map straight onto Australian summers and the dispatch resourcing that comes with them.

Learning goal

Understand how extreme heat drives demand surges and injury risk in Australian ambulance operations.

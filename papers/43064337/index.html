Gastric distension after OHCA similar across airways

Total gastric and intragastric air volumes in immediate post-resuscitation whole-body computed tomography after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: Association with airway management, aspiration, diaphragmatic position and initial blood gas analysis
Resuscitation · 2026-11 · Added 22 November 2026

German single-centre study quantifying gastric volumes on post-resuscitation CT in 120 OHCA patients found no significant difference between tracheal and supraglottic airway groups and no association between gastric air volume and CT-defined aspiration.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective single-centre CT volumetry cohort
Participants
120 OHCA patients with CT within 180 minutes (of 255 screened)
Main finding
Median total gastric volume 594 mL; no significant gastric-volume difference between tracheal (n=87) and supraglottic (n=28) groups; CT-defined aspiration numerically higher with supraglottic airways (78.6% vs 57.5%, OR 2.7, p=0.071)
Caveat
Retrospective, single-centre, and limited to patients well enough to reach early CT; mechanisms and clinical consequences remain untested
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Both tracheal tubes and supraglottic airways are standard in Australian cardiac arrest care, but the retrospective selected cohort keeps this hypothesis-generating rather than practice-guiding.

Learning goal

Weigh the evidence on supraglottic versus tracheal airway choices during cardiac arrest and their association with aspiration risk.

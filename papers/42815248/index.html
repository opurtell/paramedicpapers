Prehospital whole blood no mortality benefit

Prehospital whole blood transfusion in traumatic hemorrhagic shock: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Injury · Added 1 October 2026

A multinational systematic review and meta-analysis of prehospital whole blood in traumatic haemorrhagic shock found no significant reduction in 24-hour (RR 1.00) or 28/30-day mortality (RR 1.09) versus standard care across six studies with 4040 patients.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs and comparative cohorts (random-effects models)
Participants
6 studies (3 RCTs, 3 cohorts), 4040 adult trauma patients (1558 whole blood; 2482 standard care)
Main finding
No significant difference in 24-hour mortality (RR 1.00; P=0.98), 28/30-day mortality (RR 1.09; P=0.30), 6-hour or in-hospital mortality, or safety outcomes (ARDS, stroke, thrombotic events). Whole blood was associated with lower 24-hour red blood cell requirements (MD -0.97; P=0.03), but this lost significance on sensitivity analysis and was driven by cohorts at serious risk of bias.
Caveat
Certainty of evidence was very low to moderate, with the only positive finding driven by observational cohorts at serious risk of bias and highly sensitive to individual study weight.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). Prehospital whole blood programs are an emerging interest for Australian ambulance services and trauma networks; this meta-analysis, including recent RCTs, suggests current evidence does not demonstrate a survival benefit, which should temper expectations and inform any national decisions about implementing or expanding such programs.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and limitations of prehospital whole blood transfusion in traumatic haemorrhagic shock.

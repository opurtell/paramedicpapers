Field amputation: broad capability, little standardisation

Field Amputation by helicopter emergency service personnel: A cross-sectional survey of clinical practice and experience within air ambulance services in England
Injury · 2026-11 · Added 17 November 2026

A survey of all English air ambulance services found every one could perform prehospital field amputation and four had completed six cases in five years, with a Gigli saw the standard bone instrument and little national standardisation.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional survey of air ambulance services
Participants
14 UK helicopter emergency medical services (all England)
Main finding
All 14 services could provide field amputation; 12 (86%) had a specific clinical practice guideline; 4 services performed 6 cases in five years; a Gigli saw was the most common disarticulation instrument (93%); none used hospital response teams
Caveat
Self-reported survey with only six cases nationally in five years, so the clinical practice data are thin
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Field amputation sits with physician-staffed helicopter services rather than routine road crews in Australia, but the questions it raises — governance, training, equipment, case registries — are the same ones Australian HEMS teams face.

Learning goal

Understand how rare prehospital amputation capability is planned and governed in a low-frequency, high-risk setting.

  • rSIG cutoff flags trauma patients needing intervention

    BMJ Open · 17 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Japanese single-centre retrospective cohort tested prehospital rSIG (reverse shock index multiplied by GCS) in 747 trauma patients, finding rSIG below 24.8 was associated with resuscitative interventions (adjusted OR 3.17) and 24-hour transfusion (aOR 4.20) in the validation cohort, but not with surgery, interventional radiology or in-hospital mortality.

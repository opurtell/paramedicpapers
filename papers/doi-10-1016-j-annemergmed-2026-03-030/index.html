Paediatric IV ketamine sedation very safe

Systematic Review of Pediatric Ketamine in Emergency Department Procedural Sedation: Frequency and Predictors of Adverse Events
Annals of Emergency Medicine · Added 19 September 2026

Systematic review of 20 studies covering 67,871 children having IV ketamine procedural sedation in EDs — no deaths or permanent adverse outcomes, sentinel events pooled at about 1 in 11,558 sedations, serious adverse events 0.34%, with age ≥10, upper respiratory infection and opioid co-administration modest predictors while ASA status and benzodiazepine co-administration were not.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review of studies with ≥250 children
Participants
58,752 children receiving IV ketamine in EDs across 20 studies
Main finding
No deaths or permanent adverse outcomes; pooled sentinel events 0.0041% (~1 in 24,390 sedations); serious adverse events 0.33%; predictors of serious events were age ≥10 years, upper respiratory infection and co-administered opioids.
Caveat
Heterogeneity across studies; ED (not prehospital) setting; rare-event estimates imprecise.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). ED-based procedural sedation rather than prehospital practice, but reassuring safety data for a heavily used AP formulary drug

Learning goal

Strengthen my risk assessment for paediatric ketamine sedation by recognising which children face higher adverse event risk.

NRB + nasal cannula inferior to flush-rate preoxygenation

Combined NRB and Nasal Cannula Oxygen Delivery Is Inferior to Flush Rate NRB for Emergency Preoxygenation
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 29 September 2026

Healthy-volunteer crossover study — NRB plus nasal cannula at 15–25 L/min each produced expired oxygen of 68–75% versus 85% for flush-rate NRB, so the portable-cylinder workaround is inferior when supply allows flush rate.

Study type
Other
Design
Experimental crossover study in healthy volunteers
Participants
30 healthy volunteers
Main finding
Mean FeO2: flush-rate NRB 85% vs 68% (NRB + nasal cannula at 15 L/min each) and 75% (at 25 L/min each); differences of 16% (95% CI 13–20%) and 10% (95% CI 6–14%), both failing non-inferiority.
Caveat
Healthy volunteers in a controlled setting, not patients requiring emergency airway management.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). transferability — uses only kit in ACTAS scope (NRB, nasal cannula, oxygen) and applies from AP airway care through ICP pre-RSI

Learning goal

Refine my preoxygenation technique for emergency airway management by weighing flush-rate oxygen against combined delivery methods.

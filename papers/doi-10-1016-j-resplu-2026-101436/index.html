Medical students can teach infant BLS

Can physician-supervised medical students expand access to infant basic life support training? A prospective quasi-experimental study on long-term knowledge retention among caregivers
Resuscitation Plus · Added 20 August 2026

Quasi-experimental study testing whether physician-supervised medical students can deliver infant BLS training to caregivers — knowledge scores improved significantly after training and were sustained at 12 months, with no difference between physician and student-led groups.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Prospective quasi-experimental simulation-based training study
Participants
59 caregivers of infants (complete-case n=51)
Main finding
Knowledge scores increased significantly after training (adjusted mean difference 8.0 points, 95% CI 7.1-8.9), sustained at 12 months with no difference between physician- and student-led groups (p=0.093); private-sector caregivers scored higher (1.3 points, 95% CI 0.5-2.1).
Caveat
Quasi-experimental, small sample, exploratory — supports feasibility under supervision, not equivalence
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Model for expanding community BLS training capacity; relevant to paramedic outreach programs though not clinical practice.

Learning goal

Understand how supervised peer-led education models can expand community infant basic life support training.

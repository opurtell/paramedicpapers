FAST feasible in a moving ambulance (manikin)

Emergency Life-Saving Technician-Performed Focused Assessment with Sonography for Trauma in Moving Ambulances: A Pilot Innovation Report
Prehospital and Disaster Medicine · 2026 · Added 6 November 2026

Twenty Japanese Emergency Life-Saving Technicians performed FAST on a manikin in a moving ambulance with near-identical checklist scores stationary, at 15 km/h and 30 km/h, with transmitted image quality adequate for remote physician review.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Prospective observational simulation pilot with non-inferiority analysis
Participants
20 Emergency Life-Saving Technicians, three speed conditions
Main finding
Mean Task-Specific Checklist scores 23.4 stationary, 23.4 at 15 km/h and 23.7 at 30 km/h (of 24); non-inferiority met at both speeds, and specialist physicians could review transmitted images.
Caveat
Manikin pilot in a controlled setting — no patients, and Japanese ELSTs can't yet perform ultrasound clinically.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prehospital ultrasound is carried by a handful of Australian services and most don't, and Japan's physician-supervised technician model adds a layer we don't have.

Learning goal

Understand how prehospital ultrasound skill requirements and image quality vary when scanning during transport.

  • Lab work targets haemolysis in stored whole blood

    Resuscitation Plus · 1 Nov 2026 · Other · Context only

    A US-led laboratory study in hamsters found that haemolysis in stored whole blood correlated with worse microvascular flow and more organ injury after shock resuscitation, and pre-treating the blood with an experimental scavenger protein cocktail binding haemoglobin, heme and iron mitigated capillary collapse and tissue injury in moderate- and high-haemolysis blood.

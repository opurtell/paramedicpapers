Airway assistant training halved sim intubation time

Effect of airway assistant training on intubation time during simulated prehospital airway management: A pilot study
Prehospital Emergency Care · 2026-11-13 · Added 15 November 2026

An Australian pilot randomised simulation of 12 paramedic–ICP pairs found a one-hour airway assistant training course cut median intubation time in a continuously regurgitating airway from 102.5 to 53 seconds.

Study type
Simulation
Design
Pilot randomised simulation study (SALAD manikin)
Participants
12 qualified paramedics in two groups of 6, paired with intensive care paramedics
Main finding
Median intubation time 53 s (IQR 19) trained vs 102.5 s (IQR 36) untrained, p = 0.009, very large effect size (Cohen's d = 1.86); no oesophageal intubations in either group
Caveat
Pilot simulation with 12 participants — no patient outcomes, and the authors call for larger studies
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Airway management at intubation is core business for crews across Australian services and the assistant role exists on every two-paramedic response — the open question is whether a 12-person manikin result holds on patients.

Learning goal

Strengthen my ability to assist effectively during prehospital intubation in soiled or regurgitating airways.

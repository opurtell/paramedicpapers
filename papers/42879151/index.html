POC GFAP for brain haemorrhage in coma

Prehospital Point-of-Care GFAP for Detection of Intracranial Hemorrhage in Acute Nontraumatic Coma
JAMA Network Open · 2026-10-01 · Added 17 October 2026

A UK physician-paramedic HEMS team tested whether prehospital point-of-care GFAP and UCH-L1 blood tests could detect intracranial haemorrhage in comatose patients — GFAP performed strongly (97% sensitivity, AUC 0.94), UCH-L1 less so.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Diagnostic accuracy study (prehospital point-of-care biomarkers vs CT)
Participants
65 adults with acute nontraumatic coma (GCS ≤12)
Main finding
POC GFAP detected ICH with 97.0% sensitivity (95% CI 84.2–99.9%) and AUC 0.94; ICH was present in 51% of patients and 30-day survival was 46%.
Caveat
Small, highly selected cohort — physician-staffed HEMS patients with suspected ICH already undergoing prehospital RSI.
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Point-of-care brain biomarker testing isn't something Australian ambulances carry, and the cohort came from a physician-staffed helicopter service already inducing patients — adoption here would need a guideline change and a very different crew model.

Learning goal

Weigh the value of point-of-care biomarkers like GFAP when assessing comatose patients with suspected intracranial haemorrhage.

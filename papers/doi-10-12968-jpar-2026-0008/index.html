Paramedics underconfident with alcohol-related call-outs

Paramedics' perceptions of patients who use alcohol
Journal of Paramedic Practice · 2026-10-02 · Added 30 September 2026

A narrative review of paramedics' perceptions of patients who use alcohol found five themes — workload, impact, training, confidence and attitudes — with confidence dropping beyond basic conversations and attitudes often negative due to limited training and resources.

Study type
Other
Design
Narrative literature review
Participants
Not reported (review of published literature)
Main finding
Five themes emerged: workload, significance, training, confidence/adequacy and attitudes, with mixed views on role legitimacy and often negative attitudes shaped by limited training
Caveat
Narrative review without systematic search or quality appraisal of included studies
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). UK-based literature, but alcohol-related call-outs and referral pathway gaps are the same pressure points in Australian services, making the training and pathway findings broadly applicable.

Learning goal

Understand how limited training and role legitimacy shape my attitudes towards patients presenting with alcohol-related issues.

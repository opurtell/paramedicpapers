Blunt vs penetrating trauma physiology and outcomes

Blunt versus penetrating trauma: differences in early physiology, coagulopathy, and outcomes in a Scandinavian trauma centre
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 30 August 2026

This Swedish registry of 2,193 trauma patients (353 penetrating) found penetrating trauma patients were younger (median 34 vs 49 years), more often male (78% vs 66%), more frequently in shock on arrival (5.9% vs 2.2%), and more likely to require emergency surgery (53% vs 30%) and early blood transfusion (20% vs 4.5%).

Study type
Registry
Design
Registry-based observational cohort study
Participants
2,193 adult trauma patients (353 penetrating)
Main finding
Penetrating trauma patients more often in shock (5.9% vs 2.2%), more emergency surgery (53% vs 30%) and early transfusion (20% vs 4.5%); blunt trauma had higher ISS (median 10 vs 4) and worse functional outcomes; 30-day mortality not significantly different after adjustment
Caveat
Single Scandinavian trauma centre; not generalisable to Australian prehospital systems
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). CMG 14 Shock and CMG 39 Major Trauma are relevant AP scope, but the paper focuses heavily on blood products (not carried by ACTAS) and hospital-based outcomes — early shock recognition is useful, transfusion findings don't translate

Learning goal

Recognise how early shock and transfusion needs differ between blunt and penetrating trauma in my assessment.

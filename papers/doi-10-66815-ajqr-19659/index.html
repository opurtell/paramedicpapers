EMS alcohol education: limited and patient-focused

Enhancing Educational Programs and Communication Strategies to Address Alcohol Use Among EMS Providers
American Journal of Qualitative Research · Added 27 October 2026

A US qualitative study of 31 EMS clinicians found alcohol-related education for staff is limited and patient-focused rather than wellbeing-focused, with participants wanting EMS-specific content, personal narratives and stronger peer and family support.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Qualitative semi-structured interviews
Participants
31 EMS clinicians across multiple US certification levels and regions
Main finding
Existing alcohol-related education described as limited, passive and patient-care focused; participants wanted EMS-specific data, physiological content, personal narratives and interactive delivery plus peer and family support
Caveat
Self-selected qualitative sample of non-fire-based US providers
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Occupational drinking as a coping mechanism and how education addresses it translate across ambulance services, though the US certification mix and sample differ from Australian crews.

Learning goal

Understand how alcohol-related education and peer support shape clinician wellbeing within the Australian paramedic workforce.

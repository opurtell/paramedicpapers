US border counties: more trauma, more delays

Differences in EMS Calls, Delays, and Payer Types in U.S. Southern Border Counties and Non-Border Counties
Prehospital Emergency Care · Added 22 September 2026

Comparison of 421,877 EMS encounters in US border versus matched non-border counties — border areas carried more severe trauma and environmental exposures (25.5% vs 8.2%) alongside larger response and scene delays

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective analysis of national EMS registry data with matched county comparison
Participants
421,877 EMS encounters across eight rural US counties (242,029 border; 179,848 comparison)
Main finding
Border counties had more severe trauma or environmental exposure cases (25.5% vs 8.2%), more falls from ≥20 feet, and moderate-to-large differences in payer type, response and scene delays
Caveat
US-specific border setting; secondary data analysis with unadjusted comparisons
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Low relevance). transferability — US border-specific system pressures with no Australian analogue — descriptive interest only

Learning goal

Understand how geographic and socioeconomic context shapes case mix and system delays in ambulance response.

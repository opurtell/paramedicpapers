Shift-worker nutrition evidence: thin for responders

A Systematic Review of Nutrition Interventions for Shift Workers, With Subgroup Analysis of First Responder Populations
Journal of Human Nutrition and Dietetics · 2026-10 · Added 4 October 2026

Systematic review of nutrition interventions for shift workers, with a pre-specified first-responder subgroup, found 43 eligible studies but most involved nurses and healthcare workers, with only a small minority in first-responder populations.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Systematic review (PROSPERO-registered) with narrative synthesis and first-responder subgroup analysis
Participants
43 studies from 6,909 screened articles
Main finding
Most included studies were crossover RCTs (49%) or observational (33%); 40% involved nursing and healthcare professionals and only 9% first responders such as firefighters and police
Caveat
Few first-responder studies and much of the evidence is observational, so conclusions for ambulance crews are indirect
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Shift work and its links to chronic disease are universal in Australian ambulance work, and dietary interventions don't depend on how a health system is organised.

Learning goal

Understand how shift-work nutrition strategies apply to paramedic fatigue and long-term health.

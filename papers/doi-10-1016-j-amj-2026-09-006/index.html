HEMS burnout high even without PTSD

Psychological Well-Being of Helicopter Emergency Medical Service Personnel: A Nationwide Cross-Sectional Survey
Air Medical Journal · 2026-10 · Added 25 October 2026

A nationwide survey of Danish HEMS personnel (73 of 82 responding) found no participant met criteria for PTSD, anxiety, depression or secondary traumatic stress despite high trauma exposure — but 48.6% exceeded the personal burnout cutoff and 23.2% the work-related burnout cutoff.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Nationwide cross-sectional survey, Danish HEMS (April–May 2024)
Participants
73 physicians, HEMS crew paramedics and pilots (89% response rate)
Main finding
48.6% exceeded the personal burnout cutoff, 23.2% work-related and 17.4% patient-related; no one met PTSD criteria; trauma exposure correlated with PTSD symptoms (r=0.30) and work-related burnout (r=0.33)
Caveat
Cross-sectional self-report in a single country's physician-staffed HEMS, with wide confidence intervals on the between-group exposure comparisons
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Physician-staffed HEMS is not our crewing model, but the pattern it shows — trauma scores low, burnout high — is one Australian critical-care and road crews will recognise from their own wellbeing surveys.

Learning goal

Recognise early signs of personal burnout in my critical care practice before they affect patient safety.

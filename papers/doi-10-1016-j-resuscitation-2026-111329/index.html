GPS beats app taps for measuring responder arrival

How to Measure First Responders’ Arrival Time in a Smartphone Alerting System? An observational study
Resuscitation · 2026-09 · Added 26 September 2026

An observational study of 1,498 smartphone first-responder alerts in Germany found GPS geofence entry (50 m) gave more reliable and earlier arrival times (median 4 min 40 s) than manual app confirmation (median 5 min 50 s, often delayed or missing).

Study type
Cohort
Design
Observational study of alert system data
Participants
1,498 alerts with 2,480 first-responder acceptances over 12 months
Main finding
Median arrival times were 4:21 (100 m geofence), 4:40 (50 m geofence) and 5:50 (manual confirmation); manual confirmation was frequently delayed or missing
Caveat
Single German region, GPS accuracy varies with urban density and phone settings, no patient outcomes
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Community first-responder smartphone systems are run by some Australian services while others don't have them, but the measurement lesson applies to anyone evaluating a responder network or GoodSAM-style app.

Learning goal

Understand how measurement methods shape recorded first-responder arrival times in community alerting systems.

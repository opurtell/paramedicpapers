Ultrasound tube confirmation: thin evidence on the road

Point-of-care ultrasound for confirming endotracheal tube placement in prehospital and critical care transport: a scoping review
Internal and Emergency Medicine · 2026-10-26 · Added 30 October 2026

A scoping review of 51 studies on point-of-care ultrasound for confirming endotracheal tube placement in prehospital and critical care transport found only one primary study of diagnostic accuracy in actual out-of-hospital patients and none measuring patient-oriented outcomes.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Scoping review (Joanna Briggs Institute framework, PRISMA-ScR)
Participants
51 studies from 2,447 records
Main finding
Only one of 51 included studies evaluated diagnostic performance in adult patients in an out-of-hospital setting; no included study measured patient-oriented outcomes
Caveat
Most included work is cadaver, simulation, training and feasibility studies rather than diagnostic accuracy in transported patients
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Ultrasound isn't carried on every Australian ambulance and intubation is an advanced-practice skill in most services — and the review shows the out-of-hospital evidence base behind in-hospital accuracy figures simply doesn't exist yet.

Learning goal

Weigh the reliability of ultrasound for confirming endotracheal tube placement when verifying intubation in the prehospital setting.

  • Transport ventilation during CPR feasible in pilot

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 28 Oct 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    A Thai exploratory pilot compared transport mechanical ventilation (HAMILTON-T1, volume-controlled) with bag-valve ventilation during CPR in adult OHCA and found no significant differences in PCO2, pH, pO2, sustained ROSC or 28-day survival, with mechanical ventilation operationally feasible.

  • Real-time feedback tidies BVM ventilation in arrest

    Resuscitation · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Paris before-and-after study of 163 arrests found real-time feedback during bag-valve-mask ventilation improved insufflated-volume adherence (68% vs 42% of breaths in the 400–600 mL range) and cut leak volume by 12%, although expired tidal volumes were unchanged.

  • Airway strategy and survival in Polish HEMS

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 18 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A retrospective analysis of Poland's national physician-staffed HEMS registry tested airway strategies in 8,980 intubated adults against survival to hospital handover — HEMS-performed intubation was most common (49.8%), and in non-traumatic cardiac arrest a sequential supraglottic-airway-then-intubation strategy showed no significant difference from HEMS-performed intubation, while some other strategies carried higher unadjusted odds of not being alive at handover.

Dispatch stress, depression and suicidal ideation linked

Associations of Secondary Traumatic Stress and Emotional Labor with Suicidal Ideation Among Emergency Dispatch Personnel in South Korea: A Cross-Sectional Moderated Mediation Analysis
Healthcare · 2026-10-26 · Added 15 November 2026

A South Korean survey of 211 emergency dispatch personnel found secondary traumatic stress and depression were associated with suicidal ideation, with the indirect pathway through depression weakening as social support rose.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional moderated mediation survey
Participants
211 fire-service emergency dispatch personnel in Busan and Gyeongnam
Main finding
Conditional indirect association of secondary traumatic stress with suicidal ideation through depression fell from 0.191 at low social support to 0.013 at high; emotional labour pathways held only at low and mean support
Caveat
Cross-sectional, and key suicidal-ideation interaction terms were not significant in a Tobit sensitivity analysis
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Dispatchers sit upstream of every ambulance response, and the secondary-trauma and social-support findings speak to Australian ambulance communications centres too.

Learning goal

Understand how social support buffers secondary traumatic stress and depression in my emergency communication workforce.

Trauma analgesia rarely given prehospital

Evaluation of Paramedic Pre-hospital's Analgesic Management of Trauma Patients
Nigerian Journal of Clinical Practice · 2026-09-30 · Added 3 October 2026

A Turkish review of 506 trauma patients found prehospital analgesia was given to only 1%, with 77.5% of surveyed paramedics saying they withheld it for fear of interfering with the hospital doctor's examination.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective record review plus paramedic practice survey
Participants
506 adult trauma patients
Main finding
Prehospital analgesia in 1% of patients; 77.5% of paramedics cited concern about interfering with the physician's hospital examination as the reason for not giving it
Caveat
Single-centre retrospective design in a very different EMS culture; no pain-score data reported
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Analgesia is something paramedics here deliver and are encouraged to give early, so the finding is directly actionable — but this is a small single-centre system with a physician-dependence culture that doesn't match Australian practice.

Learning goal

Recognise my own barriers to giving early trauma analgesia, including concern about masking findings on hospital assessment.

  • Low analgesia rates in sickle cell EMS pain

    The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · 18 Sep 2026 · Cross-sectional · Context only

    US nationwide cross-section of 10,023 9-1-1 ALS encounters for sickle cell disease complications in 2023 — pain scores documented in 73% but only 13.5% received any analgesia (11.7% opioids), and among patients scoring ≥7/10, contact times ≥20 min were associated with roughly five-fold more IV access and ten-fold more analgesia/opioid administration.

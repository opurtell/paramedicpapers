Commotio cordis outcomes hinge on fast AED

Sports-Related Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest due to External Causes, Including Commotio Cordis, at Schools in Japan
Acute Medicine & Surgery · 2026-10-14 · Added 17 October 2026

A nationwide Japanese school-sports database reconstructed 30 sports-related OHCAs from external causes and found commotio cordis cases did dramatically better when AEDs were applied fast — 80% favourable neurological outcome — while other external causes fared poorly.

Study type
Registry
Design
Nationwide registry case series (SPIRITS database, 2008–2022)
Participants
30 cases of sports-related OHCA from external causes
Main finding
Among 15 commotio cordis cases, AED pads were applied in 93%, shocks in 87%, and 80% achieved favourable neurological outcome; among the other 15 external-cause cases, shocks were rare (7%) and just 7% had favourable outcomes.
Caveat
Only 30 cases across 15 years — descriptive data that can't establish causality for the AED programmes.
Bottom line
Supports current practice

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). School and sporting-ground AED response is the same public-access defibrillation landscape Australian crews work within, so the mechanism-to-response lessons apply directly.

Learning goal

Recognise commotio cordis after chest impact in young athletes and act quickly to apply defibrillation.

Fentanyl in prehospital RSI for TBI linked to lower mortality

Association between fentanyl use and mortality during prehospital emergency anaesthesia in traumatic brain injury – a retrospective cohort study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 8 September 2026

In 423 TBI patients intubated by a Finnish HEMS unit, fentanyl as an anaesthetic adjuvant was associated with lower mortality (OR 0.38), though selection bias can't be excluded.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective single-HEMS cohort with logistic regression
Participants
423 intubated TBI patients (353 fentanyl, 70 no fentanyl), single Finnish HEMS unit, 2014–2022
Main finding
Fentanyl use was associated with lower mortality before discharge (OR 0.376, 95% CI 0.172–0.823, p=0.014); more post-intubation blood pressure decreases (48% vs 30% at 10% threshold) but fewer increases (18% vs 41%) in the fentanyl group.
Caveat
Substantial baseline differences suggest treatment selection bias may partly explain the finding
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — fentanyl is on the ACTAS formulary but prehospital anaesthesia is ICP-only, and the data is confounded

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and risks of fentanyl as an anaesthetic adjuvant when facilitating intubation in traumatic brain injury.

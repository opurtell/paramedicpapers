Only 60% of paramedic-suspected STEMI confirmed

Final Diagnoses and Outcomes After Paramedic-Suspected ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction
Annals of Emergency Medicine · 2026-10 · Added 9 October 2026

A retrospective Victorian statewide ambulance registry cohort (2020–25) followed up paramedic-suspected STEMI presentations and found STEMI confirmed in only 60% of those with a recorded discharge diagnosis, with 30-day mortality of 6.5%.

Study type
Registry
Design
Retrospective statewide registry cohort
Participants
7,205 suspected STEMI visits with a recorded discharge diagnosis (of 8,077)
Main finding
STEMI confirmed in 4,330/7,205 (60.1%); non-ST-elevation ACS or other coronary syndromes 11.4%; other cardiac 10.5%; noncardiac 8.5%; coronary angiography 67.1%, PCI 44.4%; 30-day mortality 6.5% and 17.5% over median 2.8 years follow-up
Caveat
Retrospective registry design relying on coded discharge diagnoses, and 10.8% of visits had no recorded diagnosis at all
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). Prehospital STEMI pathways are standard practice nationally, and this is local data — it speaks directly to how well those pathways sort true STEMI from mimics.

Learning goal

Strengthen my judgement in recognising STEMI mimics when prehospital ECG findings do not confirm the working diagnosis.

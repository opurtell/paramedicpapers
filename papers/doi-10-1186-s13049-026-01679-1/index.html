ED intubation success high with videolaryngoscopy

Endotracheal intubation practices, success rates and adverse events in four Dutch emergency departments: a multicentre retrospective study
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · Added 25 August 2026

Retrospective study of 1,250 ED intubations showed 93.8% first-pass success with videolaryngoscopy as primary technique, with hypotension and desaturation as most common adverse events.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Multicentre retrospective cohort at four Dutch teaching hospital EDs
Participants
1,250 intubations in four EDs, 2019–2023
Main finding
First-pass success 93.8%; videolaryngoscopy primary in 89.5%; adverse events in 14.5% (hypotension 6.5%, desaturation 4.7%); increasing age (OR 1.07/yr) and cardiac indication (OR 3.50) associated with hypotension; higher BMI (OR 1.08/point), younger age and multiple attempts (OR 2.9) with desaturation
Caveat
Retrospective design using registrations and codes; ED-based, not prehospital
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). transferability — ED setting differs from prehospital, but videolaryngoscopy success rates and complication patterns are informative for airway protocols

Learning goal

Refine my first-pass intubation technique and recognise which patients are at risk of desaturation or hypotension.

Drone triage feasible but unproven in real MCIs

Remote triage with unmanned aerial vehicles in mass-casualty and prehospital emergency care: a scoping review
Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 2026-10-30 · Added 1 November 2026

A scoping review of 20 studies on UAV-assisted remote triage in mass-casualty and prehospital care found technical feasibility of remote vital-sign acquisition is demonstrated, but only one randomised comparison reached effectiveness — UAV-assisted triage took about 3.5 minutes longer with identical accuracy — and no study validated remote triage in real-world responses.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Scoping review (JBI methodology, PRISMA-ScR), 20 original studies
Participants
20 studies — 8 acquisition, 11 decision-support, 1 effectiveness
Main finding
Remote capture of heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature, SpO2, consciousness and location is feasible; the single randomised comparison showed UAV-assisted triage took ~3.5 min longer with identical accuracy; no validated remote triage standard exists
Caveat
Evidence is concentrated in disaster/mass-casualty settings under controlled conditions, with no real-world validation or patient-centred outcomes
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). UAV triage is not part of Australian MCI practice today, but drones are actively trialled here, and this review maps what would need to be proven before it changes triage on the road.

Learning goal

Weigh the benefits and delays of unmanned aerial triage against conventional methods in mass-casualty responses.

Machine-learning OHCA survival model from 370 patients

Research on a prediction model for outcomes of cardiopulmonary resuscitation in community out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients based on the Utstein model
BMC Emergency Medicine · 2026-08-24 · Added 21 November 2026

A Chinese single-centre retrospective study built an AutoML model on 370 OHCA patients that predicted ROSC and 30-day survival with a test-set ROC-AUC of 0.98, with on-scene defibrillation, cumulative adrenaline dose and time to CPR the leading features.

Single-centre retrospective prediction-model study (Hangzhou)
Participants
370 adults with non-traumatic OHCA (114 achieved ROSC)
Main finding
Test-set ROC-AUC 0.9815 and PR-AUC 0.9515, Brier score 0.127; key features were on-site defibrillation, cumulative adrenaline dose, time to CPR, time to intubation, PCO2 and time to IV access
Caveat
370 patients at one centre — the reported discrimination is almost certainly overfitted and unvalidated externally
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Prediction models built on one small centre rarely keep near-perfect discrimination elsewhere, and two of its top features — intubation timing and cumulative adrenaline — are decisions made at an intensive care paramedic tier in most services.

Learning goal

Weigh how early defibrillation, adrenaline dosing and treatment timing shape my prognostic judgement in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

    A US retrospective analysis of 4,423 OHCA encounters in Minnesota and Wisconsin communities found OHCA incidence rose across all seasons during the pandemic (largest spike fall 2020), termination-of-resuscitation rates increased from 56.6% to 61.2%, and scene times lengthened modestly.

  • Choking arrests: ROSC common, survival still poor

    Resuscitation · 20 Nov 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    A Canadian CanROC registry cohort of 105,178 EMS-treated out-of-hospital cardiac arrests (2005–2023) found 1,106 (1.1%) were obviously foreign-body-airway-obstruction-related: prehospital ROSC was far more frequent than in presumed cardiac arrests (62.7% vs 37.8%) but survival to discharge was similar (10.6% vs 9.0%).

  • First responder alerts linked to more defibrillation

    Resuscitation · 19 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Danish registry study of 5,753 OHCAs found HeartRunner community-first-responder activation was associated with more bystander defibrillation at every AED distance (TMLE +6.1 pp) and a modest 1.8-point gain in 30-day survival.

  • Five years of automated head-up CPR outcomes

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 19 Nov 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A five-year US registry of 3,709 OHCAs treated with automated head-up CPR (head/thorax positioning plus active compression–decompression and an impedance threshold device) reported 10.5% survival to discharge with favourable neurology, 91% of survivors at CPC 1.

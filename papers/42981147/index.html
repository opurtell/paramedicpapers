First consensus on TBI in first responders

Consensus Statement on Traumatic Brain Injury in First Responders: The 1st International Conference on TBI in First Responders
The Journal of Head Trauma Rehabilitation · 2026-10-16 · Added 6 November 2026

A 52-member international group used a modified Delphi process to produce the first consensus recommendations on traumatic brain injury in first responders — 28 statements covering injury reporting, assessment, return to duty, mental health and workplace policy, all reaching consensus.

Study type
Guideline / statement
Design
Consensus statement (modified Delphi, two voting rounds)
Participants
52 panel members; 28 final recommendations
Main finding
27 of 29 preliminary statements reached consensus in round 1 and all 28 final recommendations reached the 80% threshold in round 2, across six domains from injury risk and reporting to workplace policy.
Caveat
Consensus opinion rather than evidence of effectiveness, and focused on TBI suffered on the job rather than patient care.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A US-led consensus, but the occupational questions — nonpunitive reporting, return to duty, mental health — apply to Australian first-responder agencies equally.

Learning goal

Understand how workplace TBI risks, reporting culture and return-to-duty practices affect my own wellbeing and professional practice.

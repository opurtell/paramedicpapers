How ambulance models shape end-of-life care

Investigating the impact of ambulance service models on paramedic delivery of end-of-life care in England: an exploratory study using case study design
BMC Palliative Care · 2026-08-12 · Added 25 October 2026

Case studies of three English ambulance services found paramedics deliver end-of-life care with learning on the job and patchy access to patient records, anticipatory medicines and palliative advice, with more innovative models (advanced-paramedic anticipatory medicines, an in-service palliative team and palliative care ambulances) improving resources.

Study type
Qualitative
Design
Embedded qualitative case study of three English ambulance service models, framework analysis
Participants
101 participants (75 healthcare professionals, 26 patients and carers) across 3 services
Main finding
Learning on the job and limited access to records, anticipatory medicines and palliative advice dominated practice across all three models; paramedic workload was driven partly by primary and community service shortages
Caveat
Exploratory, mainly qualitative case studies; three English services selected for their end-of-life service models, so findings may not transfer to other services
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (High relevance). End-of-life calls are a growing share of ambulance work everywhere, and the gaps it names — no records access, no anticipatory medicines, no one to ring for advice — are the same ones Australian crews describe; the innovative service models are ones our services could copy.

Learning goal

Understand how system design shapes paramedic decision-making in end-of-life care in the Australian context.

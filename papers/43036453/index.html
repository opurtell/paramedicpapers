What competence actually means for paramedics

Competences of Paramedics in Prehospital Emergency Care: An Umbrella Review
Emergency Medicine International · 2026-11-15 · Added 17 November 2026

A Finnish-led umbrella review of 20 studies defined the competences paramedics need in out-of-hospital emergency care, organised into five domains: interpersonal and teamwork, cognitive and decision-making, professionalism and ethical, clinical, and situation-specific or high-stress competence.

Study type
Systematic review
Design
Umbrella review (Joanna Briggs Institute methodology, GRADE-CERQual)
Participants
8 reviews synthesised from 2,224 screened records
Main finding
Five competence domains identified: interpersonal and teamwork; cognitive and decision-making; professionalism and ethical; clinical; and competence across certification levels and in special or high-stress situations
Caveat
The review stops at describing domains — which educational approaches best build and maintain competence remains unknown
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Finnish-led, but the five competence domains map closely onto Australian paramedic registration standards and the expanding scope of practice debate.

Learning goal

Understand how the five competence domains apply to my scope of practice across routine and high-stress situations.

  • A framework for caring at the scene: STOP

    Scandinavian Journal of Caring Sciences · 12 Nov 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    A Swedish-Finnish concept synthesis developed the STOP framework — acute Suffering, act of Togetherness, On-scene space — positioning holistic, patient-centred caring, not just biomedical intervention, as the core of prehospital emergency care.

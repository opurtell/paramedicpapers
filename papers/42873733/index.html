Mobile stroke units: value depends on setup

Mobile Stroke Units in Acute Ischemic Stroke: Thrombectomy Efficiency, Workflow Models, and Implementation Considerations: A Review
Cerebrovascular Diseases · 2026-10-14 · Added 16 October 2026

Narrative review concludes mobile stroke units deliver thrombectomy-relevant time savings only when onboard CTA links to direct routing and receiving-centre activation — non-contrast-CT units mainly speed prenotification.

Study type
Other
Design
Narrative review
Participants
Not reported
Main finding
CTA-enabled mobile stroke units can confirm large-vessel occlusion in the field and support direct-to-angiography pathways, while non-contrast-CT units accelerate prenotification; economic value is highly sensitive to volume, staffing and catchment
Caveat
Narrative (not systematic) review whose own message is that benefits are configuration- and system-dependent
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). No Australian service runs a mobile stroke unit routinely, so this informs the future-case debate rather than current practice; stroke destination decisions themselves are core business.

Learning goal

Understand how mobile stroke unit configuration shapes direct-to-angiography pathways and prehospital stroke system value in Australia.

  • Scene-to-door time and stroke thrombectomy

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 25 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A single-centre Japanese cohort of 121 stroke thrombectomy patients transported directly from scene found scene-to-door time was only associated with outcome in patients with pre-stroke disability, where longer intervals were linked to lower odds of a favourable 90-day outcome (aOR 0.91; ROC cut-off 14 minutes in that subgroup).

  • Decision support eases stroke triage load

    The Western Journal of Emergency Medicine · 22 Oct 2026 · Simulation · Context only

    In a randomised crossover simulation trial, 35 Taiwanese emergency medical technicians using a stroke triage decision-support interface showed reduced mental workload (RMSSD up 4.3 ms, heart rate down, NASA-TLX lower) versus dispatch orders alone.

  • POC GFAP for brain haemorrhage in coma

    JAMA Network Open · 17 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A UK physician-paramedic HEMS team tested whether prehospital point-of-care GFAP and UCH-L1 blood tests could detect intracranial haemorrhage in comatose patients — GFAP performed strongly (97% sensitivity, AUC 0.94), UCH-L1 less so.

  • Stroke app speeds prehospital-to-ED handover

    JMIR Medical Informatics · 10 Oct 2026 · Other · Context only

    A South Korean pilot of a mobile app that streams stroke data from ambulances to EDs found shorter onset-to-door times in 34 app-supported transports versus 129 matched standard transports.

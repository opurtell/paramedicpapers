GP clinics under-equipped for cardiac arrest

Preparedness of primary health care practices for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a cross-sectional study of training, equipment, and cardiac arrest experience in a large Polish metropolitan area
BMC Health Services Research · 2026-09-01 · Added 27 November 2026

A Polish cross-sectional survey of 177 primary care practices in Poznań found OHCA preparedness was poor — recent BLS training in as few as 5% of practices, AEDs in 32–44%, and supraglottic airways in 2–13%.

Study type
Cross-sectional
Design
Cross-sectional survey with structured interviews and partial visual equipment checks
Participants
177 primary care practices (81.6% response) in a Polish metropolitan area
Main finding
40% of county and 28.9% of urban practices had an OHCA in the last 10 years; BLS training within two years in 10.0% and 5.2%; practices reporting BLS training had more AEDs and supraglottic airway devices.
Caveat
Cross-sectional, so whether training drives equipment or the reverse can't be established.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Polish GP clinics rather than ambulance crews, but a practice near a collapse is part of the chain of survival anywhere — the equipment gaps would likely look similar here.

Learning goal

Recognise the gaps in community-based primary care readiness that shape early cardiac arrest response before paramedic arrival.

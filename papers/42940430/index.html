Tele-paramedics matched physicians in MCI drill

Telemedicine in Disaster Medicine-Use of Paramedics as Tele-Consultants to Guide Personnel with Limited Medical Training at the Disaster Site
Healthcare · 2026-10-11 · Added 28 October 2026

In a German mass-casualty simulation, tele-consult support delivered by experienced paramedics performed similarly to tele-physician support and on-site physician care on indicated treatments addressed (51% vs 56-57%) and analgesia provision (~60%), both far ahead of EMT-only care (8% and 7%).

Study type
Simulation
Design
Prospective simulation-based controlled quasi-experimental study within a 7.5-hour MCI exercise
Participants
151 simulated patients across 7 treatment tents
Main finding
Indicated treatments addressed: 51% tele-paramedic vs 57% tele-physician vs 56% on-site physician vs 8% EMT-only (p < 0.001); analgesia addressed in 60%, 59%, 68% and 7% respectively; time to first treatment order did not differ
Caveat
Single simulation exercise with simulated patients — real-disaster performance untested, and patient contact definitions differed between telemedicine and non-telemedicine groups
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian services don't run tele-consultant models for mass-casualty scenes, but the finding that experienced paramedics can fill the remote expert role speaks directly to any service designing MCI telehealth support.

Learning goal

Build my understanding of how paramedic tele-consultation can support less-trained clinicians at mass-casualty incidents.

First responders notifying schools after child trauma

Implementing Handle With Care: A Cross-Sector Model Linking Schools and First Responders to Support Children Exposed to Potentially Traumatic Events
Journal of Child & Adolescent Trauma · 2026-09-18 · Added 7 November 2026

A US evaluation of the Handle With Care programme linking first responders with schools after potentially traumatic child events found it feasible in an under-resourced Mississippi district, with significant gains in staff trauma-informed attitudes but low notification volume in year one.

Study type
Other
Design
Mixed-methods first-year implementation evaluation (training, notification process, surveys, qualitative interviews)
Participants
75 school staff and 64 first responders, one Mississippi school district
Main finding
Significant gains in school staff trauma-informed attitudes; greater first-responder training exposure linked with lower burnout; disciplinary referrals decreased overall while in-school suspensions increased; attendance stable.
Caveat
Single under-resourced district, first year of implementation, student outcomes exploratory.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). A US police–fire–ambulance-to-school notification model that doesn't map directly onto Australian practice, though the trauma-informed training findings travel.

Learning goal

Understand how early notification between emergency services and schools can support children exposed to traumatic events.

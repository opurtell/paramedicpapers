CPR injuries common after long arrests

Incidence of Traumatic Injuries Sustained Following Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation: A Single-Centre Retrospective Cohort Study
Resuscitation Plus · 2026-10 · Added 9 October 2026

A single-centre retrospective cohort of 136 OHCA patients found CPR-related injuries in 43% (16.9% clinically significant), with CPR duration over 20 minutes — not ECPR or mechanical CPR — independently associated with injury (OR 2.45).

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective single-centre cohort
Participants
136 OHCA patients (of 313 screened)
Main finding
58/136 (43%) sustained injuries, 23 (16.9%) clinically significant; CPR >20 minutes associated with injury (OR 2.45, 95% CI 1.15–5.23); mortality higher in injured patients (70% vs 43%)
Caveat
Small single-centre retrospective sample with confounding by arrest duration and selection toward ECPR-eligible patients
Bottom line
Hypothesis-generating

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). ECPR is an in-hospital therapy, but the core finding — injuries come with the minutes of compressions, not the devices — applies to any prolonged arrest worked anywhere.

Learning goal

Recognise how prolonged resuscitation duration influences the likelihood of clinically significant CPR-related injuries in my practice.

  • Bystander CPR up but outcomes flat in the very old

    Resuscitation Plus · 9 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Analysis of 322,943 bystander-witnessed OHCA patients aged ≥65 in the All-Japan Utstein Registry (2005–22) found bystander CPR rose over time but stalled near 50%, and 1-month favourable neurological outcome stayed around 1% in those aged ≥90 despite CPR rates near 80%.

  • Amiodarone after non-shockable-to-shockable conversion

    Resuscitation · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    German Resuscitation Registry propensity-matched cohort testing whether amiodarone helps OHCA patients whose initial non-shockable rhythm converts to shockable — it was associated with better 24-hour and survival-to-discharge, though not clearly better neurological outcome.

  • Long resuscitations don't rule out good recovery

    Resuscitation · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Danish national registry study of bystander-witnessed OHCA survivors asking whether longer time-to-ROSC means worse long-term outcomes — anoxic brain damage rose with time, but long-term survival, return to work and nursing-home admission did not, showing prolonged resuscitation doesn't preclude good recovery.

  • Bystander AEDs work — when someone actually grabs one

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 8 Oct 2026 · Registry · Supports current practice

    Korean multicentre OHCA registry analysis estimating bystander AED effectiveness against a no-defibrillation reference — AED application was associated with outcomes comparable to or better than EMS shocks, but was applied in only a fraction of eligible cases, especially at home.

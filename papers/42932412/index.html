STEMI deaths now mostly prehospital

Optimizing US Prehospital Care of ST-Segment–Elevation Myocardial Infarction Because Time Is Life, Not Just Muscle
Circulation · 2026-10-27 · Added 4 November 2026

A US Circulation viewpoint argues that because most STEMI deaths now occur out of hospital, the biggest gains lie in shortening symptom-onset-to-reperfusion, and sets out five recommendations spanning patient education, prehospital pharmaceuticals and reimbursement reform.

Main finding
Five recommendations to shorten symptom onset to reperfusion: patient education, prehospital glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors or thrombolytics, rapid PCI access, and changes to prehospital care reimbursement
Caveat
Opinion piece with no new data, and the prehospital-drug recommendations assume a system that doesn't exist in every Australian service
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). The funding machinery is American, but the central argument — most STEMI deaths are prehospital — applies here, and thrombolytics are carried by a handful of Australian services while most don't.

Learning goal

Recognise the patient and system factors that delay reperfusion in Australian STEMI care.

NSW data linkage tracks sudden cardiac arrest

SUDDEN CARDIAC ARREST IN NEW SOUTH WALES (NSW), AUSTRALIA: A DATA LINKAGE STUDY
Heart Rhythm · 2026-11-11 · Added 13 November 2026

An Australian data-linkage study identified 13,345 presumed sudden cardiac arrests aged 1–50 in NSW from 1.7 million linked records — 54% cardiac in aetiology and 83% fatal — and extrapolated roughly 370 cardiac SCA deaths a year among Australians aged 1–35.

Study type
Cohort
Design
Retrospective multi-source administrative data linkage
Participants
13,345 presumed SCA cases aged 1–50 in NSW
Main finding
54% of cases had cardiac aetiology and 83% were fatal; rhythm-related and structural diagnoses dominated ages 1–11 while ischaemic disease dominated ages 35–50; older cardiac cases were more likely to die prehospital (aOR 3.56 for 35–50 vs 1–11 years); extrapolated ~370 cardiac SCA deaths per year in Australians aged 1–35.
Caveat
Retrospective coded administrative data with cardiac aetiology assigned from hospital and death records, and national figures extrapolated from one state.
Bottom line
Context only

Why it matters in Australia (Medium relevance). Australian data built on the NSW Ambulance OHCA register, but it maps the size and shape of the problem rather than changing what crews do.

Learning goal

Understand the national burden and age-related causes of sudden cardiac arrest in young Australians.

  • Rhythm changes during CPR track neurological outcomes

    Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026 · Registry · Hypothesis-generating

    A South Korean nationwide registry study of adults with initial nonshockable OHCA found rhythm transitions during CPR graded neurological outcome: asystole converting to PEA was linked to better outcomes than persistent asystole (OR 2.28), and persistent PEA carried the best odds (OR 8.70 versus persistent asystole).

  • Higher admission PaCO2 linked to worse post-arrest survival

    Journal of Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Chinese single-centre retrospective cohort of 617 adults resuscitated from OHCA found admission PaCO2 above 50 mmHg associated with higher one-month mortality in a stepwise gradient (each 10-mmHg increase aOR 1.45, 95% CI 1.24–1.70).

  • Night-time OHCA outcomes linked to bystander availability

    Journal of Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026 · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    A Czech-led scoping review of 18 studies found OHCA and STEMI incidence peaks in the morning with a secondary afternoon/evening peak, and night-time events were linked to lower ROSC and worse one-month and one-year survival, explained by limited bystander availability, less bystander CPR and defibrillation and longer times to hospital intervention rather than time of day itself.

  • Longer collapse-to-contact times linked with ROSC

    Frontiers in Medicine · 12 Nov 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Chinese single-centre retrospective cohort of 152 OHCA cases found longer collapse-to-first-medical-contact intervals were independently associated with prehospital ROSC — a paradox the authors attribute to case-mix differences rather than a real benefit of slower response.

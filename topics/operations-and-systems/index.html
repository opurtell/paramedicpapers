Operations & systems research

60 recent papers on operations & systems in paramedic and prehospital care, newest first. Each has a plain-English summary, the study design, the main finding and caveat, and what it means for Australian practice.

  • On-site CT slashes event transports

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 7 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective before–after study at the Munich Oktoberfest found an on-site mobile CT led to 429 of 498 imaged patients (86%) being treated and discharged on-site, avoiding an estimated 22,737 minutes of EMS deployment time.

  • EMS dashboards: thin crisis evidence

    Archives of Academic Emergency Medicine · 7 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    Scoping review of 26 studies found EMS performance dashboards are used almost exclusively for routine operations — only one study addressed crisis conditions and it used no dashboard.

  • Bigger crews work faster, don't save more

    Journal of Trauma and Injury · 3 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Korean nationwide cohort of 78,203 severe trauma patients found crews of three or more achieved better prehospital IV access (aOR 1.47) and scene times ≤10 minutes (aOR 1.09) than two-person crews, with no survival difference after adjustment.

  • Risk-score-guided conveyance safe in chest pain

    Heart · 3 Oct 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    A Dutch pragmatic RCT testing whether a prehospital risk score could guide ambulance conveyance in 5170 adults with undifferentiated chest pain — it was non-inferior to usual care for 3-day serious adverse events while increasing non-conveyance, cutting 30-day ED presentations and lowering costs.

  • Co-design of urine culture stewardship in ED

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 29 Sep 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Qualitative co-design study at Townsville ED (7 workshops, 35 emergency physicians) building a theory-informed intervention against low-value urine cultures — culture, complexity and efficiency drove over-ordering, with education, nudges, clinical champions and audit/feedback chosen as the fix.

  • Governance gaps cripple Ethiopian prehospital care

    Surgery · 24 Sep 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Qualitative needs assessment using the WHO prehospital assessment tool across 15 interviews with EMS and health bureau leaders in Hawassa, Ethiopia identified six recurring barriers — infrastructure, medicines/equipment, communication, community perceptions, workforce and, cutting across all of them, unclear governance and accountability — leaving services functioning as interfacility transport rather than true prehospital care.

  • US border counties: more trauma, more delays

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 22 Sep 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Comparison of 421,877 EMS encounters in US border versus matched non-border counties — border areas carried more severe trauma and environmental exposures (25.5% vs 8.2%) alongside larger response and scene delays

  • IMPACC paramedic model cuts ED transport

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 22 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Evaluation of 7,382 encounters under Toronto's IMPACC model, which lets specially trained paramedics redirect lower-acuity 911 callers — activated encounters had transport rates of 36% vs 82% for others (still 43% vs 79% after matching) but added roughly 24 minutes on scene, with 4% re-contact at 72 hours

  • Hot debrief tool in metropolitan EDs

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 17 Sep 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    Multi-site Australian quality improvement study of a structured hot debrief tool after critical events (46 debriefs across two metropolitan EDs) found 98% of attendees rated it psychologically safe and 59 of 60 found it useful, with themes surfacing system issues like medication dosing in rare events and staff safety during behavioural emergencies.

  • AI voice assistants in EMS: survey

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 16 Sep 2026 · Cross-sectional · Context only

    Multinational survey of 401 EMS clinicians in Germany, Norway and Switzerland exploring how AI-based voice assistance systems could be designed for prehospital care — respondents were technically proficient and already using digital tools mainly for documentation, knowledge access, hospital pre-notification and occupancy checks, with the study mapping requirements, expectations and implementation obstacles rather than testing clinical outcomes.

  • Embedded low-acuity pathway improves rural ED performance

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 15 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Quasi-experimental study of an embedded ultra-low acuity pathway at Dubbo Base Hospital (n=4,798) — pathway availability halved did-not-wait rates (14.2%→6.9%), lifted seen-on-time and treatment performance, and cut mean ED length of stay by 51.7 minutes.

  • 2026 review of prehospital guideline quality

    The 2026 update of the systematic review appraising evidence-based guidelines for prehospital care — abstract not yet available (ahead-of-print), but this recurring review is the reference map of what guideline-level evidence exists in prehospital practice.

  • ED volume not linked to mortality

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 8 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Cohort of 724,256 ED presentations across Dutch and Danish hospitals — annual ED volume had no association with in-hospital mortality, while mortality was lower on busier days, driven by excess deaths on quiet ones.

  • Ramping driven by hospital 'back door'

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 8 Sep 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    A mixed-methods Queensland study of six years of ambulance and hospital records found access block is largely a hospital discharge ('back door') problem, with recommendations spanning discharge metrics, primary care support and prehospital flow.

  • AI predicts ED admitting team from triage notes

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 8 Sep 2026 · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

  • ED ultrasound archiving works in Australia

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 3 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Four-year implementation study of a middleware ED ultrasound archiving and QA workflow at Nepean Hospital — 7,281 studies archived across 15 modalities with zero data loss or failures, automated trainee logbooks saving each trainee ~2 h/week, and clinician time (not software) the dominant recurring cost.

  • Japanese mobile EMS app rollout data

  • Public accepts drone-delivered AEDs

  • Indirect ICU admissions from Australian ED

  • Defining diagnostic excellence in the ED

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 21 Aug 2026 · Guideline / statement · Context only

    Consensus study using a modified RAND/UCLA panel method to define diagnostic excellence and missed diagnostic opportunity in emergency departments — diagnostic excellence is accurate, timely, evidence-based, and patient-centered communication of a diagnosis based on available information, while a missed diagnostic opportunity is a measurable divergence from optimal practice that may lead to inaccurate/delayed explanations or communication failures.

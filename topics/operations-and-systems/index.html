Operations & systems research

94 recent papers on operations & systems in paramedic and prehospital care, newest first. Each has a plain-English summary, the study design, the main finding and caveat, and what it means for Australian practice.

  • What drives lay community first responders in Denmark

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 28 Oct 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    An interpretive phenomenological study of ten Danish lay community first responders — part of a 60,000-volunteer national system, about 70% without healthcare backgrounds — identified four themes: a sense of duty, acting under uncertainty as a civilian-turned-responder, navigating relational roles with crews, and carrying personal identity into crisis.

  • Tele-paramedics matched physicians in MCI drill

    Healthcare · 28 Oct 2026 · Simulation · Context only

    In a German mass-casualty simulation, tele-consult support delivered by experienced paramedics performed similarly to tele-physician support and on-site physician care on indicated treatments addressed (51% vs 56-57%) and analgesia provision (~60%), both far ahead of EMT-only care (8% and 7%).

  • Longer on-scene intervals linked to 24-hour mortality

    Archives of Academic Emergency Medicine · 28 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    A Thai retrospective cohort of Code Red ALS activations found on-scene intervals of 21-25 minutes were associated with higher odds of 24-hour mortality compared with 5 minutes or less (adjusted OR 5.61), and intervals of 20 minutes or longer came with 321.7 fewer expected survival minutes.

  • What patients think of alternative response units

    Paramedicine · 27 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    A scoping review of patient, family and carer experiences of alternative ambulance response units found that people value receiving competent care at home, avoiding hospital transport, and referral pathways with continuity of care beyond the initial episode.

  • How ambulance models shape end-of-life care

    BMC Palliative Care · 25 Oct 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Case studies of three English ambulance services found paramedics deliver end-of-life care with learning on the job and patchy access to patient records, anticipatory medicines and palliative advice, with more innovative models (advanced-paramedic anticipatory medicines, an in-service palliative team and palliative care ambulances) improving resources.

  • Paramedics on digital decision support

    BMC Emergency Medicine · 24 Oct 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Qualitative interview study of 12 Norwegian paramedics on digital health tools for prehospital non-emergency decision-making — four themes emerged around call assessment and resource allocation, shared situational awareness, interprofessional communication, and the challenges of patchy implementation.

  • Hot days drive dizziness call-outs

    Environmental Health and Preventive Medicine · 24 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Population-based study of 270,120 emergency transports in Kochi, Japan linking weather to dizziness-related ambulance call-outs — hotter days and short-term temperature spikes raised the odds of high-volume dizziness days, peaking in summer.

  • OHCA registry clock times run short

    Resuscitation Plus · 22 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Linking 56,969 Swedish OHCA registry records to dispatch-centre data showed unit response times matched almost exactly, but registry-recorded total response times ran a median 81 seconds shorter than the dispatch clock.

  • What makes a HEMS nurse: Spanish views

    A qualitative Spanish study of 17 HEMS professionals found flight nursing was seen as a highly specialised profile requiring critical-care experience, advanced technical skills, aviation safety training and structured in-person education — with no formalised national training pathway.

  • Drone AI for mass-casualty triage

    A research team built and pilot-tested a drone-integrated AI system that measures heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and temperature from drone-captured video and thermal imaging, assigns triage categories, and streams casualty data to dispatch — feasible under controlled indoor conditions.

  • Australian alcohol harms keep rising

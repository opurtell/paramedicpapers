Mental health (patients) research

6 recent papers on mental health (patients) in paramedic and prehospital care, newest first. Each has a plain-English summary, the study design, the main finding and caveat, and what it means for Australian practice.

  • Mapping medical clearance of psychiatric ED patients

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 20 Aug 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    Scoping review mapping medical screening practices for adult psychiatric patients presenting to ED — identified 8 themes (laboratory testing, history taking, vital signs, physical examination, diagnostic imaging, screening tools, ECG, system-level factors), with society recommendations discouraging routine lab testing.

  • FDSV rarely detected in virtual ED

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 4 Aug 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective cohort of Victoria's Virtual ED — only 21 FDSV presentations identified among 173,936 visits (0.012%), suggesting significant under-detection

  • Perspectives on paediatric ED mental health care

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 27 Jul 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Care recipients and care providers identified challenges in service coordination, clinician readiness and the ED environment.

  • Research agenda for paediatric mental health emergencies

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 27 Jul 2026 · Guideline / statement · Context only

    This consensus-based research agenda, which integrates diverse perspectives, informs the need for research to improve identification and management of children at risk of harming themselves or others in the out-of-hospital and emergency department settings.

  • Teen and caregiver views on lethal means counselling

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 27 Jul 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Lethal means safety counseling is an essential, high-yield component of suicide prevention.

  • Ketamine vs propofol for aeromedical psychiatric sedation

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 27 Jul 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    In this RCT of 170 patients, ketamine was associated with fewer adverse events compared to propofol when used for sedation during aeromedical retrieval of patients experiencing acute psychiatric agitation.

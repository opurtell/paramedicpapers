Sepsis & infection research

6 recent papers on sepsis & infection in paramedic and prehospital care, newest first. Each has a plain-English summary, the study design, the main finding and caveat, and what it means for Australian practice.

  • Prehospital antibiotics earlier in sepsis

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 22 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Thai observational cohort of 364 adults with suspected sepsis testing prehospital antibiotics — prehospital administration was associated with lower adjusted 28-day mortality (ARR 8.9%, 95% CI 0.8–17.1; aOR 0.51) and antibiotics ~125 minutes sooner, but scene times ran 8.5 minutes longer.

  • Eight sepsis subgroups found, SENECA not replicated

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 5 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective consensus clustering of 953 ED sepsis admissions at Oslo University Hospital could not replicate the four SENECA phenotypes, instead finding eight stable subgroups with more granular characteristics — and only 49% of patients meeting SENECA inclusion criteria fulfilled Sepsis-3, underlining sepsis heterogeneity and cohort-definition problems.

  • Vaccine-preventable diseases re-emerging in EDs

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 4 Aug 2026 · Guideline / statement · Context only

    Editorial on the re-emergence of vaccine-preventable diseases post-COVID — declining vaccination rates are causing outbreaks of diphtheria, measles, and other VPDs in Australasia

  • EDs: opportunistic immunisation, not vaccine clinics

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 4 Aug 2026 · Guideline / statement · Context only

    Editorial arguing EDs should provide opportunistic, proportionate immunisation for vulnerable patients rather than functioning as routine vaccine clinics

  • Diphtheria re-emergence and ED impact

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 27 Jul 2026 · Other · Context only

    ABSTRACT Diphtheria has re‐emerged in Australia in 2026, prompting significant public health concern and placing additional demands on healthcare services.

  • Ambulances and clinicians contaminated with pathogens

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 1 Jun 2026 · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    This study found that ambulance surfaces and clinicians remain contaminated with clinically important pathogens even after routine cleaning, suggesting current disinfection protocols may be inadequate to prevent healthcare-associated infections during patient transport.

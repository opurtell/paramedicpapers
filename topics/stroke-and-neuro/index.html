Stroke & neuro research

11 recent papers on stroke & neuro in paramedic and prehospital care, newest first. Each has a plain-English summary, the study design, the main finding and caveat, and what it means for Australian practice.

  • Stroke triage tools: cost-effective, hard to sustain

    Journal of Evaluation in Clinical Practice · 6 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    Systematic review of prehospital stroke triage tools (mobile stroke units, telemedicine, biomarkers, AI) found most reports of cost-effectiveness positive, but sustained implementation is limited by complexity, workflow fit and costs.

  • Online stroke education boosts EMS-seeking

    Journal of Education and Health Promotion · 5 Oct 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    RCT in 106 high-risk residents in Huanggang, China testing a 6-week online stroke education program against usual health education — it significantly improved stroke knowledge, warning-sign awareness and EMS-seeking intent.

  • Two-thirds of stroke patients delay seeking care

    Cerebrovascular Diseases · 3 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Context only

    Meta-analysis of 27 studies and 266,336 ischaemic stroke patients across 14 countries found 67.7% experienced prehospital delay before seeking care, with stroke knowledge and symptom recognition the key modifiable drivers.

  • Stroke care gaps for First Nations patients

    The Medical Journal of Australia · 3 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Victorian linked-data study of 32,163 EMS-attended stroke/TIA patients found First Nations patients had more than double the age-standardised incidence of EMS attendance, were less likely to receive a paramedic stroke assessment (58.2% vs 67.3%) and less often taken to thrombectomy-capable hospitals, though adjusted mortality was similar.

  • AI triage of ED CT brain scans

    Emergency Medicine Australasia · 29 Sep 2026 · Cross-sectional · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective validation of an AI model classifying 3,424 ED CT brain scans as urgent or not — 98.3% negative predictive value with no false negative needing urgent intervention at the index visit, putting ~64% of scans in the possibly-expedite bucket pending a prospective trial.

  • IM midazolam non-inferior for prehospital seizures

    Seizure: European Journal of Epilepsy · 27 Aug 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    Randomised double-blind non-inferiority trial testing whether IM midazolam is non-inferior to IV clonazepam for prehospital seizures — it was, with 75% treatment success for IM midazolam versus 62.5% for IV clonazepam (risk difference +12.5 percentage points; 95% CI -10.0 to +33.4).

  • Prehospital magnesium before thrombolysis: no overall benefit

    Journal of Stroke and Cerebrovascular Diseases · 31 Jul 2026 · RCT · Hypothesis-generating

    Secondary analysis testing whether prehospital magnesium sulfate followed by thrombolysis improves 90-day outcomes — feasible and safe but no significant overall clinical benefit, though severe stroke patients (NIHSS >15) showed better outcomes in hypothesis-generating subgroup analysis.

  • Over-rapid BP drop worse after ICH

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 27 Jul 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective cohort examining whether rapid systolic BP reduction ≤150 mm Hg after spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage improved outcomes — early target achievement and overshooting <120 mm Hg were associated with worse functional outcome.

  • HEMS dispatch timing matters for LVO stroke thrombectomy

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 27 Jul 2026 · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    The efficacy of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) for large vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke is highly time-dependent.

  • Helicopter + ground transport doubles thrombectomy access

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 27 Jul 2026 · Modelling · Hypothesis-generating

    A hybrid transportation strategy improved access to MT in a single center study.

  • Overshooting BP targets worsens ICH outcomes

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 1 May 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective cohort testing whether rapid systolic BP reduction ≤150 mm Hg after spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage improved outcomes — early target achievement and overshooting <120 mm Hg were associated with worse functional outcome.

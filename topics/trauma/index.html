Trauma research

57 recent papers on trauma in paramedic and prehospital care, newest first. Each has a plain-English summary, the study design, the main finding and caveat, and what it means for Australian practice.

  • NOF fracture care runs on tacit knowledge

    Paramedicine · 6 Oct 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Australian exploratory qualitative study running eight online focus groups with 21 registered paramedics on suspected neck of femur fracture care — participants described managing these patients through diagnostic uncertainty, tacit knowledge, peer learning and contextual judgement in the absence of dedicated prehospital guidance, and called for clearer evidence-informed direction covering movement, dignity, communication and analgesia.

  • US racial gaps in trauma interventions

    JAMA Surgery · 3 Oct 2026 · Registry · Context only

    US trauma–EMS linked cohort of 18,280 injured adults found Black patients received fewer prehospital airway interventions and intubations than White patients, and patients from disadvantaged neighbourhoods received fewer prehospital interventions overall.

  • Prehospital vasopressors in trauma unproven

    European Journal of Emergency Medicine · 3 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    Systematic review of five retrospective studies (31,849 trauma patients) found prehospital vasopressor infusions before haemorrhage control showed inconsistent mortality associations — one military study found markedly lower adjusted survival and another linked vasopressors to more massive transfusion and coagulopathy — on very-low-certainty evidence.

  • Language barriers change trauma assessment

    Journal of Surgical Research · 2 Oct 2026 · Cross-sectional · Context only

    US retrospective analysis of 1,220,982 EMS-attended blunt trauma activations examining whether patient-provider language barriers change prehospital care — the 2% with documented language barriers were more often treated and transported (80.3% vs 75.1%) yet less likely to have pain scores documented (60.6% vs 63.6%), with slightly more GCS 3–8 presentations, showing assessment and documentation patterns shift when clinician and patient can't share a language.

  • Prehospital whole blood no mortality benefit

    Injury · 1 Oct 2026 · Systematic review · Supports current practice

    Systematic review and meta-analysis of prehospital whole blood transfusion in traumatic haemorrhagic shock; abstract not yet available (ahead-of-print), so no findings can be reported yet.

  • Prehospital minithoracostomy vs chest drain

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 29 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective physician-led HEMS cohort (94 trauma patients) comparing prehospital minithoracostomy with chest drainage — minithoracostomy carried no meaningful scene-time penalty and fewer recorded complications, but non-random allocation and few events make it hypothesis-generating only.

  • Calcium with prehospital blood products for trauma

    The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · 25 Sep 2026 · Systematic review · Hypothesis-generating

    Meta-analysis of calcium supplementation during prehospital blood-product trauma resuscitation. Abstract not yet available (ahead-of-print).

  • Causal model for prehospital blood trials

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 22 Sep 2026 · Modelling · Context only

    Methodology paper building an expert-refined causal model for prehospital blood transfusion trials in traumatic haemorrhagic shock — maps confounders (system times, training, units available) and mediators so future studies can actually isolate a treatment effect against crystalloid

  • Whole blood during prolonged entrapment rescue

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 22 Sep 2026 · Case series · Context only

    Case report of an entrapped trauma patient after a motor vehicle collision receiving advanced airway management, finger thoracostomies and multiple units of low-titre O-positive whole blood during a prolonged multi-agency extrication — the blood came via the regional prehospital program supporting a physician response team

  • Traumatic arrest in older adults

    Resuscitation Plus · 22 Sep 2026 · Registry · Context only

    French national registry study of 5,595 traumatic cardiac arrests comparing older (≥65) with younger adults — older patients achieved ROSC more often (22.5% vs 18.6%) but 30-day survival was lower (0.5% vs 1.2%), leading the authors to argue age alone shouldn't preclude resuscitation though a concurrent medical cause should be sought

  • Fluids in traumatic cardiac arrest: rat model

    Resuscitation Plus · 17 Sep 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    Rat model of traumatic cardiac arrest (30% haemorrhage plus asphyxia) comparing four fluids after ROSC found 96-hour survival of 90% with whole blood, 80% with HES, 60% with lactated Ringer's and just 30% with normal saline — crystalloids prolonged survival without improving haemodynamics.

  • Frostbite depth and extent predict amputation

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 15 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Context only

    Retrospective cohort of 128 frostbite admissions in eastern Turkey — injury depth drove amputation risk (81.5% of third-degree injuries required amputation vs 1.6% of first/second-degree), and combining degree with %TBSA predicted amputation at admission (AUC 0.948), while inflammatory markers added nothing after adjustment.

  • Gelatin and HES impair clot formation ex vivo

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 15 Sep 2026 · Simulation · Hypothesis-generating

    Ex vivo human whole-blood model testing graded dilution with gelatin 4%, HES 130/0.42 and a balanced crystalloid — both colloids produced a clearly hypocoagulable ROTEM profile (prolonged clotting and clot formation times, reduced alpha-angle and maximum clot firmness, worst with HES) while the crystalloid alone stayed haemostatically neutral, and combining crystalloid with colloid dilution worsened the coagulopathy further.

  • One in five severe trauma cases may need prehospital blood

    Injury · 13 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Multicentre French retrospective study applying the ABC score across four Paris-region prehospital services to estimate demand for prehospital blood — 27 of 139 severe trauma patients (19.7%) met ABC ≥2 with prehospital intervention time >20 min and median total prehospital time of 79 min, only 67% of those eligible received TXA, and 5 arrested prehospital, leading the authors to argue prehospital transfusion should not remain a rarity.

  • LVOT VTI unreliable for mild hypovolaemia

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 8 Sep 2026 · Other · Hypothesis-generating

    Prospective study measuring LVOT VTI in 63 volunteers before and after donating about 6–7 mL/kg of blood — VTI fell 11.5% on average but with marked inter-individual variability, so it can't reliably flag mild hypovolaemia on its own.

  • Admission hypocalcaemia predicts trauma mortality

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 8 Sep 2026 · Registry · Context only

    Multicentre trauma-registry cohort of 977 severely injured patients — admission hypocalcaemia was independently associated with hospital mortality and trauma-induced coagulopathy, while apparently elevated ionised calcium mostly reflected profound acidosis.

  • Fentanyl in prehospital RSI for TBI linked to lower mortality

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 8 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    In 423 TBI patients intubated by a Finnish HEMS unit, fentanyl as an anaesthetic adjuvant was associated with lower mortality (OR 0.38), though selection bias can't be excluded.

  • Predicting who needs prehospital blood

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 8 Sep 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Retrospective cohort of 25,473 EMS-transported trauma patients in Los Angeles County — of those meeting prehospital blood transfusion criteria, 52% received blood within four hours, with SBP <90 mmHg and HR ≥110 bpm the best predictors of emergent transfusion.

  • How GPs decide to respond to trauma alarms

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 5 Sep 2026 · Qualitative · Context only

    Qualitative interview study of 20 Norwegian primary care doctors on how they decide whether to call out to acute trauma alarms from limited EMCC voice messages — despite the sparse information it remained a decisive factor, with experienced doctors using recognition-primed cues and weighing severity against travel distance and the risk of concurrent incidents.

  • Prehospital blood for non-traumatic haemorrhage

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 1 Sep 2026 · Case series · Hypothesis-generating

  • Blunt vs penetrating trauma physiology and outcomes

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 30 Aug 2026 · Registry · Context only

    This Swedish registry of 2,193 trauma patients (353 penetrating) found penetrating trauma patients were younger (median 34 vs 49 years), more often male (78% vs 66%), more frequently in shock on arrival (5.9% vs 2.2%), and more likely to require emergency surgery (53% vs 30%) and early blood transfusion (20% vs 4.5%).

  • Predicting prehospital blood need after MVC trauma

  • Trauma undertriage still high in Tromsø

    Retrospective registry study assessing trauma undertriage using ISS ≥16 and NISS ≥16 as reference — found 15% undertriage (above the recommended <5%), with 63% of undertriaged patients meeting national criteria but not receiving trauma team activation, mostly low-energy falls.

  • Trauma quality improvement opportunities by cohort

  • Prehospital whole blood improves shock physiology

    Systematic review of 13 studies found prehospital whole blood consistently improved shock physiology (greater SI reduction, higher shock reversal rates) and showed potential survival benefit in high-risk and penetrating trauma patients, though overall mortality benefit was not uniform.

  • Prehospital NEWS beats shock index in trauma

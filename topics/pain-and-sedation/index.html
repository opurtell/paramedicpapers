Pain & sedation research

9 recent papers on pain & sedation in paramedic and prehospital care, newest first. Each has a plain-English summary, the study design, the main finding and caveat, and what it means for Australian practice.

  • Methoxyflurane feasibility confirmed in Canada

    Pain Management · 3 Oct 2026 · Cohort · Supports current practice

    Canadian feasibility study of patient-controlled inhaled methoxyflurane in 97 adult trauma patients found 95% protocol compliance, median pain falling from 8/10 to 5/10, mostly mild side effects in 23%, and no serious drug-related adverse events.

  • Low analgesia rates in sickle cell EMS pain

    The American Journal of Emergency Medicine · 18 Sep 2026 · Cross-sectional · Context only

    US nationwide cross-section of 10,023 9-1-1 ALS encounters for sickle cell disease complications in 2023 — pain scores documented in 73% but only 13.5% received any analgesia (11.7% opioids), and among patients scoring ≥7/10, contact times ≥20 min were associated with roughly five-fold more IV access and ten-fold more analgesia/opioid administration.

  • IV magnesium adds little for ED headache

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 25 Aug 2026 · RCT · Context only

    RCT of 1,028 ED patients with acute nontraumatic headache found IV magnesium sulfate plus paracetamol achieved more frequent treatment success (78.9% vs 65.1%) but pain score differences were below clinical importance thresholds, with less rescue analgesia needed but more mild side effects.

  • Intranasal equals subcutaneous ketamine for pain

    Annals of Emergency Medicine · 20 Aug 2026 · RCT · Supports current practice

    RCT testing whether intranasal ketamine is non-inferior to subcutaneous for acute traumatic pain — both routes provided equivalent analgesia with minimal clinically important differences, though minor adverse events were more common with subcutaneous administration.

  • Droperidol for nausea: low adverse event rate

    Prehospital Emergency Care · 28 May 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    This study evaluated adverse events associated with prehospital droperidol use for nausea or vomiting — the incidence of clinically significant adverse events was low, suggesting droperidol may be safe for prehospital use.

  • No benefit from prophylaxis for opioid nausea

    Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine · 7 May 2026 · Cohort · Hypothesis-generating

    Prospective study of 205 HEMS patients comparing ondansetron prophylaxis, aromatherapy, and no prophylaxis for opioid-induced nausea — neither intervention showed benefit compared to no prophylaxis in unselected patients.
